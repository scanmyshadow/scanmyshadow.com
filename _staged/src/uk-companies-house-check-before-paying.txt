=== TLDR
Companies House's free **Find and update company information** service shows a company's **status, directors, registered address and filing history**, and lets you search **dissolved companies** too. Before paying a builder, letting agent, online shop or investment firm, check the company **exists, is active, isn't brand new, files on time**, and that the names and address match what you were told. Since **18 November 2025**, directors have to **verify their identity** with Companies House, which makes fake directors harder. But a registered company isn't automatically an honest one.

=== BODY
## Why bother checking

Companies House's own blog puts it plainly: "It's quite easy for someone to say 'I'm a company director'" ([Companies House blog](https://companieshouse.blog.gov.uk/2019/01/29/checking-the-public-company-record/)). Checking takes five minutes. Chasing a deposit from a company that never existed takes considerably longer.

## What the free search shows

The service lives at [find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/). According to [ACCA's summary of the service](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/in-practice-archive_2023/June/Companies-House-Find-update-service.html), it offers:

- **advanced company searches**;
- **dissolved company searches** (records of companies dissolved since January 2010, kept for 20 years);
- **company snapshot reports**;
- an alphabetical name search.

From the Companies House blog, each record shows **directors' names and service addresses**, the **filing history** (accounts, confirmation statements, charges) and other business activity.

## The five-minute check

1. **Search the exact name** and the **company number** if it's on the invoice or website. A company number that doesn't match the name is a bad sign.
2. **Status**: is it "Active"? Walk away from "Dissolved," "Liquidation" or "Proposal to strike off."
3. **Age**: incorporated last month? Be careful with big payments up front.
4. **People**: do the directors match the person you're dealing with?
5. **Filing history**: are accounts and confirmation statements up to date? A string of overdue filings is a red flag.
6. **Address**: does the registered office match the address on the website or invoice?
7. **Dissolved search**: if the name isn't on the live register, search dissolved companies. A builder trading under a company that closed two years ago is telling you something.

## Red flags at a glance

| What you see | What it can mean |
|---|---|
| No match for the name or number | The company may not exist, or you have the wrong details |
| Dissolved or proposal to strike off | The company is closed or being closed |
| Incorporated in the last few weeks | No track record yet; keep deposits small |
| Overdue accounts or confirmation statements | Poorly run, or abandoned |
| Directors don't match who you're dealing with | Possible impersonation or a "clone" of a real firm |
| Registered office is a different address from the invoice | Ask why before paying |

## What changed: director ID verification

Under the new company law reforms, **identity verification** became mandatory from **18 November 2025**, according to [Howard Kennedy's summary](https://insights.howardkennedy.com/post/102kz33/companies-house-announces-id-verification-rollout-from-18-november-2025):

- **new directors** must verify their identity to set up a company or be appointed;
- **existing directors** must confirm verification in their company's **next confirmation statement**;
- people can verify through **GOV.UK One Login** (free) or through an **Authorised Corporate Service Provider**.

For you as a customer, this means a director's name on the register is more likely to belong to a real, verified person than it used to be. It doesn't vouch for their workmanship, their honesty or whether the person emailing you is actually them.

## What a clean record doesn't prove

A registration isn't a seal of approval. Scammers register companies too, and some "clone" real firms by copying their name and number. So also:

- check investment firms on the [[uk-fca-firm-checker-clone-firms-fca-impersonation|FCA Firm Checker]];
- read reviews beyond the first page of results;
- for property, verify ownership with HM Land Registry ([[uk-spareroom-flatshare-rental-scams|as SpareRoom advises]]);
- call the company on a number you found yourself, not the one in the email or invoice;
- never pay a business invoice into a personal bank account.

Doorstep builders deserve extra care. A roofing gang that targeted older homeowners was jailed for 63 years in total ([[uk-roofing-gang-jailed-63-years-1m-fraud-older-homeowners|the case]]).

## If you're a director: your own privacy

The register shows a lot about directors, and people-finder sites copy it. The government's [Companies House changes page](https://changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk/protecting-your-information/) lists what you can now hide:

- **since 27 January 2025**: your **home address**, where it was used as a registered office, in historical documents;
- **since 21 July 2025**: **residential addresses** elsewhere on the register in most cases, **day of birth** on documents filed before 10 October 2015, **signatures** and **business occupation**.

Since 10 October 2015, only the **month and year** of birth have been shown publicly. If your details have spread beyond the register, see our [[uk-192-com-remove-your-details|192.com removal guide]].

=== FAQ
Q: Is a Companies House search free?
A: Yes. The Find and update company information service on GOV.UK is free to search.
Q: Does being on Companies House mean a company is legitimate?
A: No. It shows the company is registered, not that it's honest. Use it alongside the FCA register, reviews and your own checks.
Q: What are the red flags on Companies House?
A: No match for the name or number, dissolved or strike-off status, very recent incorporation, overdue filings, and directors who don't match the person you're dealing with.
Q: Can I check a company that has closed?
A: Yes. The service includes a dissolved company search covering companies dissolved since January 2010.
Q: Do company directors have to verify their identity now?
A: Yes. Identity verification became mandatory from 18 November 2025 for new directors, and existing directors confirm it in their next confirmation statement.
Q: Can company directors hide their home address?
A: Directors can use a service address, and since July 2025 they can apply to suppress residential addresses elsewhere on the register in most cases.
