=== TLDR
Origin Energy disclosed a breach on **28 July 2026** affecting about **900,000 current and former customers**, roughly one in five. Exposed: **names, dates of birth, phone numbers, addresses, account information and partial card or bank account numbers**. The attacker claims 2 million; Origin hasn't confirmed that. No credit monitoring has been announced. Expect very convincing "Origin" bills, refunds and "account problem" calls, and treat them all with suspicion.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [Daily Security Review](https://dailysecurityreview.com/cyber-security/origin-energy-breach-exposes-data-on-900000-australian-customers/), Origin began investigating in early July 2026 after receiving a threat report it first didn't think was credible. New evidence on **22 July** escalated things, and Origin disclosed on **28 July**.

- **Affected:** about 900,000 of roughly 4.8 million customers
- **Attacker's claim:** 2 million customers, not confirmed by Origin
- **How:** not disclosed; CEO Frank Calabrese cited an ongoing investigation by authorities

Reuters' list of major Australian breaches includes Origin as the latest big name before [the OpenAI agent incident](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/24/factbox-openai-data-breach-latest-in-long-list-of-hacks-in-australia).

## What was exposed

| Data | Exposed |
|---|---|
| Names | Yes |
| Dates of birth | Yes |
| Phone numbers and addresses | Yes |
| Account information | Yes |
| Card or bank account numbers | **Partial** |

"Partial" is better than full. But a birthday, address and a few digits of your bank account are exactly what a scammer needs to sound like your bank on the phone.

## The real risk: targeted scams

Origin itself warned that even if the original attacker never publishes the data, **other criminals could get hold of it** for scams and phishing. Banks noticed too: NAB put the Origin incident on its [scam alerts page](https://www.nab.com.au/about-us/security/latest-fraud-scam-alerts), warning of targeted phishing.

Expect:

- fake Origin bills or "overdue" notices with a payment link;
- "refund" or "compensation for the breach" emails;
- calls from "your bank's fraud team" quoting your partial account number.

## What to do

1. **Be sceptical of any contact claiming to be Origin**, another utility or your bank. Call back on a number you look up yourself.
2. **Pay bills only through the Origin app or a site you type in.**
3. **Watch your bank statements** for unfamiliar direct debits.
4. **Consider a credit ban** if you're worried about your birthday and address being misused ([[australia-credit-ban-how-to-request|how]]).
5. **Turn on multi-factor authentication** on email and banking.
6. **Contact IDCARE** on 1800 595 160 for free support if you think your identity is being misused.
