=== TLDR
A text or letter says thousands in “forgotten property” has been matched to your name, and you can claim it today for a small fee, often around $29.95 or $49.95. Real unclaimed property searches and claims through your state are free. Search yourself at unclaimed.org (which links to official state pages) or MissingMoney.com, and never pay an upfront fee to a stranger who found “your money.”

=== BODY
## Real money, fake messengers

Unclaimed property is real: old paychecks, deposits, refunds and insurance payouts that states hold until the owner claims them. Scammers love it because it sounds believable and almost everyone has a little out there.

## How the text works

[MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/?p=409031) broke down a September 2026 wave:

1. A precise amount, such as **$8,742**, “matched to your name.”
2. A generic agency name that sounds official.
3. A deadline: the claim period ends today, or has been extended “just for you.”
4. A link that asks for identity details, including your Social Security number.
5. A small release fee, such as **$29.95** or **$49.95**.
6. Then more fees: “state certification,” tax withholding, insurance, shipping, a bond.

The small fee isn't the real prize. Your SSN and card details are.

## The letter version

Fake “attorneys” and “finders” send letters too. The BBB described one where a fake Canadian attorney offered to recover about $10.6 million in unclaimed life insurance, with a relative's name close enough to be convincing ([Boston 25 News](https://www.boston25news.com/news/trending-now/scam-alert-fake-unclaimed-property-letter-may-look-real-but-don-t-fall-for-it-bbb-warns/990129453/)). The BBB's rule: legitimate finders don't charge upfront fees. They take a cut only after you're paid, and you don't need them anyway.

## Search for free, safely

- **[unclaimed.org](https://unclaimed.org/)** from the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators links to each state's official search.
- **MissingMoney.com** searches participating states at once.
- Your **state treasurer's or comptroller's website**, typed in yourself.

Searching and claiming through your state is free. If you find money, the claim process is on the state site, not in a text.

## If you paid or shared your SSN

Call your card issuer to dispute the charge, then freeze your credit ([[how-to-freeze-your-credit-us|how]]) and consider an [[irs-identity-protection-pin-how-to-get|IRS IP PIN]]. Report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) and forward the text to 7726.

## Why you were picked

Every text goes to thousands of numbers; the “match” is fiction. Lists come from breaches and brokers. Check yours with a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]].

=== FAQ
Q: Do I have to pay to claim unclaimed property?
A: No. Searching and claiming through your state is free.
Q: Is a text saying unclaimed money is matched to my name real?
A: Treat it as a scam. Search yourself through unclaimed.org or your state's official site.
Q: What fees do these scams ask for?
A: Often a small "release fee" like $29.95 or $49.95, followed by more fees and requests for your SSN.
Q: Where can I safely search for unclaimed money?
A: unclaimed.org (official state links), MissingMoney.com, or your state treasurer's website.
