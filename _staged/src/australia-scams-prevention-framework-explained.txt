=== TLDR
Federal Parliament passed the **Scams Prevention Framework (SPF)** on **13 February 2025**. It puts legal duties on businesses expected to be designated first: **banks, telcos and digital platforms** (social media, paid search ads, messaging). They must **prevent, detect, report, disrupt and respond** to scams. Penalties go up to about **$52.7 million**. But unlike the UK, there's **no mandatory reimbursement**; instead you get internal complaints, a single external scheme (likely **AFCA**) and the right to sue.

=== BODY
## The basics

From [Bird & Bird's explainer](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/explainer-australias-new-scam-prevention-framework):

- **Passed:** 13 February 2025, and has Royal Assent.
- **Lead regulator:** the **ACCC**.
- **Sector codes:** **ASIC** enforces the banking code; **ACMA** the telco code.
- **First sectors expected:** telecommunications, banking, and digital platforms (social media, paid search advertising, direct messaging). Designation still needs confirming.

## The six principles

| Principle | What businesses must do |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | Written anti-scam policies, reviewed and certified yearly |
| **Prevent** | Reasonable steps to stop scams |
| **Detect** | Investigate scam intelligence; identify affected customers |
| **Report** | Share actionable scam intelligence with the ACCC |
| **Disrupt** | Act to stop scams and losses (with legal protection for good-faith action) |
| **Respond** | Easy scam reporting for customers, plus internal dispute resolution |

## Penalties

- **Tier 1** (prevent, detect, disrupt, respond): up to the greater of about **$52.7 million**, three times the benefit, or **30% of adjusted turnover**.
- **Tier 2** (codes, governance, reporting): up to about **$10.5 million**, three times the benefit, or 10% of turnover.

## What it means if you're scammed

Here's the honest part. **There's no automatic refund.** The UK made banks reimburse most push-payment scam victims; Australia chose a different path:

1. Complain to the business through its **internal dispute resolution**.
2. Escalate to a **single external dispute resolution scheme**, expected to be **AFCA**.
3. You (or a regulator on your behalf) can **sue** a business that broke its SPF obligations to recover losses.

So the framework gives you leverage: if your bank ignored obvious red flags, or a platform kept running a reported scam ad, that's now a legal failing, not just bad manners.

## What's still to come

The government still has to confirm designated sectors, consult on sector codes, and make the SPF Rules. Start dates for specific obligations depend on that.

=== FAQ
Q: When was the Scams Prevention Framework passed?
A: Federal Parliament passed it on 13 February 2025.
Q: Does the Scams Prevention Framework make banks refund scam victims?
A: No. Australia did not adopt mandatory reimbursement. Victims can use internal complaints, external dispute resolution and court action.
Q: Which businesses does the SPF cover?
A: Banks, telcos and digital platforms are expected to be designated first.
Q: What are the penalties under the SPF?
A: Up to about $52.7 million, three times the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover for the most serious breaches.
