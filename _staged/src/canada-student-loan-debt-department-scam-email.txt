=== TLDR
In **March 2025**, Newfoundland and Labrador warned that emails asking people to contact a **"Student Loan Debt Department"** are scams. **No such department exists in any Canadian jurisdiction.** Delete them without replying. If you want to check your real loan, log in to your official student aid account or call your provincial student aid office using a number you look up yourself.

=== BODY
## The email

The [Government of Newfoundland and Labrador's advisory](https://www.gov.nl.ca/releases/2025/education-en/0307n03/) on March 7, 2025 described emails directing recipients to a **"Student Loan Debt Department."** The province was clear: that department doesn't exist anywhere in Canada. The province had issued a [similar warning in 2023](https://www.gov.nl.ca/releases/2023/education-en/0714n04/), so this isn't a one-off.

Inventing a government department is bold. It's also handy for spotting fakes: if you can't find the department on any official government site, it's not real.

## What they want

These emails typically aim to:

- scare you about **debt, collections or penalties**, or tempt you with **forgiveness**;
- get you to **call or reply**;
- collect your **SIN, banking info, card numbers or passwords**;
- take a **fee** for "processing" relief you could get for free, or that doesn't exist.

## The official advice

- **Don't open** questionable links.
- **Keep personal details**, including card numbers, to yourself.
- **Don't share passwords.**
- **Ignore urgent demands** from senders you don't know.
- **Report** suspected scam emails right away.

NL students can verify with Student Financial Services at **709-729-5849** or **1-888-657-0800**.

## How to check your real student loan

- Log in to your official federal or provincial student aid account by typing the address yourself.
- Call your provincial student aid office using a number from its official website.

## If you replied or paid

1. Call your bank if you shared card or account details.
2. If you shared your SIN, see [[canada-sin-stolen-what-to-do|SIN stolen: what to do]].
3. Report to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/) and local police.

Students are prime targets because their emails leak through school systems, as the [[was-i-affected-by-the-powerschool-data-breach|PowerSchool breach]] showed. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows which breaches your address is in.

Q: Is there a Student Loan Debt Department in Canada?
A: No. Newfoundland and Labrador says no such department exists in any Canadian jurisdiction.
Q: What should I do with a student loan debt department email?
A: Delete it without replying and don't click links.
Q: How do I check my real Canadian student loan?
A: Log in to your official student aid account or call your provincial student aid office using an official number.

A: Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre and your provincial student aid office.
