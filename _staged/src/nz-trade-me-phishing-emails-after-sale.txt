=== TLDR
You sell something on **Trade Me**. The "buyer" wins, then you get an email posing as **Trade Me, NZ Post or a bank** asking you to **"finalise the sale"** by confirming details through a **link**. The fake site wants your **card details, banking login or Trade Me password**. Trade Me says it **never** asks for your Trade Me login, credit card details or banking password. Turn on **MFA** and allow bids from **authenticated members only**.

=== BODY
## How it works

From [Trade Me's help centre](https://help.trademe.co.nz/hc/en-us/articles/12434190471439-Phishing-emails-after-a-sale):

1. A scammer **wins your listing**.
2. You receive an email posing as **Trade Me, NZ Post or a bank**.
3. It asks you to **"finalise the sale"** by confirming your details.
4. A **button or link** leads to a fake site asking for **card details, banking logins or your Trade Me login**.

It's the PayID trick from across the Tasman with a Kiwi accent ([[australia-payid-scam-marketplace-sellers|Australia's version]]).

## Warning signs

- The sender isn't Trade Me (NZ Post or a bank "about your sale")
- The address doesn't end in a **Trade Me domain**
- **Urgency or threats**: "your sale will fall through"
- Stiff, formal or **poorly written** language
- **Low-quality or misplaced logos**

## What Trade Me actually does

- It **never** asks for your **Trade Me login**, **credit card details** or **banking password**.
- Its support team **may** contact you to confirm bank account details or identity, but **never** asks for passwords.
- After a sale, Trade Me **emails you the buyer's details** so you can send or arrange pickup. That's it.

## Protect your listings

1. Turn on **multi-factor authentication** on your Trade Me account.
2. Tick **"Allow bids from authenticated members only."**
3. Don't click links in sale-related emails; log in through the app.
4. Ask a friend for a second opinion when something feels off.

## The courier-link version

Police warned in December 2024 about scammers posing as **buyers** on **Trade Me and Facebook Marketplace**, [RNZ reported](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/536138/police-warn-of-scammers-posing-as-buyers-targeting-online-sales-platforms). The pattern:

- The buyer is **in a hurry** and offers to **arrange the courier**.
- They send a **fake courier link**.
- The link asks for your personal and **banking details** and a **four- or five-digit code**.
- That's enough to take money from your account.

Detective Inspector Stuart Mills said the scam is likely run by **international organised crime**, and targets people under **financial pressure**. His rule: "You should never give those authorisations or confirmation details to a third person at any time." Go to your bank's site yourself, never through a link a buyer sends.

## If you clicked

- Change your **Trade Me** and **email** passwords.
- If you entered card or bank details, **call your bank** now.
- Report scam listings through **Community Watch**, and report the scam to **Netsafe**.

Note: the [[nz-bank-scam-reimbursement-rules-explained|bank reimbursement rules]] don't cover marketplace purchase scams, which makes prevention even more important. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] shows if your email is in breach data.

=== FAQ
Q: Is it safe to let a Trade Me buyer arrange the courier?
A: Be careful. Police warned of fake buyers who send a courier link that asks for bank details and a code. Never enter banking details or codes through a buyer's link.
Q: Does Trade Me email you to finalise a sale?
A: Trade Me emails you the buyer's details after a sale. It never asks for your Trade Me login, card details or banking password.
Q: How do I report a Trade Me scam?
A: Report suspicious listings through Community Watch and scams to Netsafe.
Q: How can sellers avoid Trade Me scams?
A: Turn on MFA, allow bids only from authenticated members, and don't click links in sale emails.
