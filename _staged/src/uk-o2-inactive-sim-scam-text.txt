=== TLDR
O2 warned on June 30, 2026 about texts claiming your SIM will become inactive unless you sign updated terms, linking to a fake O2 site that steals your My O2 login. Separately, Which? flagged calls from spoofed UK numbers offering O2 bill discounts, then asking for a one-time passcode. O2 doesn't text asking you to sign new terms or lose your SIM. === BODY
## Scam 1: the inactive SIM text

According to [Virgin Media O2](https://news.virginmediao2.co.uk/o2-warns-of-new-inactive-sim-scam-calling-on-customers-to-report-to-7726/):

- fraudsters text O2 customers saying their SIM will soon become inactive;
- the SIM will be deactivated unless they sign updated terms and conditions;
- a link leads to a fake O2 website asking you to log in;
- the aim is to capture My O2 usernames and passwords and take over accounts.

O2 says it has blocked **over 1 billion scam messages** to date, thousands of them linked to this scam.

## Why your phone account matters

A hijacked mobile account can be used to order new phones on your contract or move your number to a new SIM (a [[sim-swap-fraud-how-it-works-and-how-to|SIM swap]]), which then receives your bank's security codes. It's not about your phone bill; it's about everything your number unlocks.

## Scam 2: the bill discount call

Which? flagged [calls from spoofed UK mobile numbers](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) offering fake O2 bill discounts. The caller triggers a one-time passcode to your phone, then asks you to read it out. That code lets them into your account. A discount that requires your security code isn't a discount.

## How to tell what's real

- O2 **doesn't text** asking you to sign new terms or risk losing your SIM.
- Genuine O2 texts on many smartphones show under a verified **“O2”** sender, not a mobile number.
- Check anything in the **My O2 app** or by calling **202** from an O2 phone.
- Never read out a code sent to you.

## What to do

1. Forward scam texts to **7726** (works on all networks).
2. If you entered your login, **change your My O2 password** immediately and add a PIN to your account.
3. If you shared a code, call O2 and ask them to check for SIM or upgrade orders.

=== FAQ
Q: Will O2 deactivate my SIM if I don't sign new terms?
A: No. O2 says it doesn't text customers asking them to sign new terms or risk losing their SIM.
Q: How do I check if an O2 message is real?
A: Use the My O2 app or call 202 from an O2 device. Genuine texts often show a verified "O2" sender.
Q: Someone from O2 asked for a code to apply a discount. Is it real?
A: No. Never share a one-time passcode with a caller.
Q: How do I report an O2 scam text?
A: Forward it to 7726, which works on all UK networks.
