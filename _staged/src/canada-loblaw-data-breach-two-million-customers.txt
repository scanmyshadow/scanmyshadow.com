=== TLDR
Loblaw said on October 23, 2026 that it is starting to notify about two million Canadians affected by a breach it detected in March. Depending on the person, exposed data may include names, email and home addresses, dates of birth, loyalty card numbers, some order details and partial payment card information. Loblaw is offering free TransUnion credit monitoring and identity restoration. The bigger risk now is scam emails and texts dressed up as breach notices.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to the [Canadian Press report carried by CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/money/2026/10/23/loblaw-says-march-data-breach-affected-about-two-million-canadians/), Loblaw detected “suspicious activity” on its IT network in March 2026 and found that an unauthorized third party had accessed some basic customer information. It investigated, notified police and regulators, and says it strengthened its security.

Seven months later, on October 23, it began notifying about **two million Canadians**. The company hasn't said how the attackers got in.

## What may have been exposed

Not everyone had everything exposed. The possible data includes:

- names;
- email and home addresses;
- dates of birth;
- loyalty card numbers;
- some purchase order details;
- **partial** payment card information.

Partial card data can't be used to shop on its own, but it is gold for phishing: “We noticed a charge on your card ending 4417” sounds terrifyingly real when the last four digits are correct.

## The scam wave to expect

Every big Canadian breach is followed by fake notices. Watch for:

- emails or texts saying “Your Loblaw/PC account was compromised, verify here”;
- fake “compensation” or “gift card for the inconvenience” offers;
- calls from a “bank fraud team” that quote your partial card number and ask you to move money or read out a code;
- loyalty-points theft attempts, where scammers log in and redeem your points.

Small businesses are being targeted with fake breach notices too, as [Cybersecurity Canada notes](https://cybersecuritycanada.ca/news/posts/loblaw-data-breach-phishing-scams-how-canadian-smbs-should-respond/).

## What to do now

1. **Wait for the real notice**, then enroll in the free TransUnion credit monitoring using the code in it. Don't click enrollment links in unexpected texts.
2. **Change your loyalty account password** and any other account that shared it. A [[check-if-your-password-has-been-leaked|leaked password]] is the most common way points get stolen.
3. **Place a fraud alert** with Equifax and TransUnion Canada, or go further with a [[how-to-freeze-your-credit-canada|credit freeze in Canada]].
4. **Check your card statements** for small test charges.
5. **Report scam messages** to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/) and see our guide to [[canada-anti-fraud-centre-reporting-guide|reporting to the CAFC]].

## A breach rarely comes alone

If your email is on one breached list, it is usually on several. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows where it has turned up, which helps you guess what a scammer knows before they call.
