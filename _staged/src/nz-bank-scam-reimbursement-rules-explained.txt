=== TLDR
Under changes to the **Code of Banking Practice** that took effect **30 November 2025**, NZ banks can reimburse victims of **authorised payment scams** (where you made the payment yourself, like romance or investment scams) **up to $500,000**, if your bank or the receiving bank **failed to meet its commitments**. How much you get depends on whether you took **"reasonable care."** Report to your **bank and Police within 3 months** of discovering it. Purchases on **Trade Me or social media** aren't covered. Disagree? Go to the **Banking Ombudsman**.

=== BODY
## What changed

On 23 April 2025, the NZ Banking Association announced Code changes, effective **30 November 2025**, per [Bell Gully](https://www.bellgully.com/insights/fraud-and-scams-update-banks-commit-to-new-protections-and-reimbursement-for-scam-victims/):

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Authorised payment scams** | Reimbursement up to **$500,000** where your bank or the recipient bank failed its commitments |
| **How much** | Some or all, depending on whether **you took reasonable care** |
| **Above $500k or 3+ times** | At the bank's discretion |
| **Deadline** | Report to the bank **and Police** within **3 months** of discovering it, and within **12 months** of the last payment |
| **Which payments** | To **NZ bank accounts**, made **after 30 November 2025** |
| **Unauthorised payments** (scammer accessed your account) | Compensated if you weren't dishonest or negligent, took reasonable steps and cooperated |

## The five bank commitments

Banks must provide:

1. **Warnings** for high-risk or unusual payments, including asking what the payment is for.
2. **Confirmation of payee** in online and mobile banking.
3. **Policies to catch high-risk transactions**: questions, real-time warnings, delays or blocks.
4. A **24/7 scam reporting** line with timely investigations.
5. **Information sharing** between banks to freeze and recover funds and close mule accounts.

If your bank skipped one of these and you lost money, that's your strongest argument.

## The catches

[Consumer NZ](https://www.consumer.org.nz/articles/banks-introduce-new-compensation-measures-for-scam-victims) welcomed the scheme but flagged problems:

- **Voluntary**, with no regulator or penalties.
- **"Reasonable care"** is a lower bar for banks than the UK's gross-negligence test, so fewer victims may be paid.
- **Speed:** banks pledge payment within **30 business days** after gathering information; the UK typically takes five.
- **Exclusions:** scams buying goods on **social media or marketplaces like Trade Me** aren't covered.
- You may be ineligible if you acted dishonestly or paid through a **third-party service**.

## How to claim

1. **Call your bank immediately.** Every hour counts for freezing funds.
2. **Report to Police** (105.police.govt.nz or 105). The Code requires both reports.
3. Keep **records**: screenshots, messages, payment details.
4. Ask the bank in writing to consider **reimbursement under the Code**.
5. If you disagree with the outcome, complain to the **Banking Ombudsman**.

=== FAQ
Q: Will my NZ bank refund money I lost to a scam?
A: Possibly. Since 30 November 2025, banks can reimburse authorised payment scam losses up to $500,000 where they or the recipient bank failed to meet their commitments, depending on whether you took reasonable care.
Q: How long do I have to report a scam to claim?
A: Within three months of discovering it, and within 12 months of the last payment, to both your bank and Police.
Q: Are Trade Me scams covered?
A: Consumer NZ says scams involving purchases on social media or marketplaces like Trade Me are not covered.
Q: What if my bank refuses?
A: You can complain to the Banking Ombudsman.
