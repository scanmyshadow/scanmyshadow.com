=== TLDR
In **February 2026**, NZ Police arrested a **44-year-old Ellerslie woman** over an alleged **romance scam** that began **online in 2006** and ran for about **18 years**. Police allege she obtained **$525,537** from a **Dunedin man**. She faces a **representative charge of obtaining by deception**. Most romance scams are run **offshore**, which makes this local arrest unusual. The lesson: long relationships that are always about money are still scams.

=== BODY
## What Police allege

From [NZ Police](https://www.police.govt.nz/news/release/busted-woman-arrested-over-elaborate-18-year-scam) (11 February 2026):

- An "**elaborate**" romance scam starting **online in 2006**.
- **Dunedin Police**, with Auckland City Financial Crime Unit, executed a search warrant at an **Auckland** property.
- A **44-year-old Ellerslie woman** was arrested.
- Police allege she obtained **$525,537**.
- The charge: a **representative charge of obtaining by deception**, covering the whole period.

These are allegations; the case was due in Auckland District Court on 17 February 2026.

## Eighteen years

That's not a scam, that's a second career. And it shows how romance fraud really works: not a single big hit, but **years of small, emotional requests** that add up.

## Why romance scams last so long

- **Sunk cost:** the more someone has given, the harder it is to walk away.
- **Shame:** victims often hide it from family.
- **Plausible excuses:** there's always a reason they can't meet.
- **Isolation:** scammers encourage secrecy.

Police note romance scams are **often run by offshore offenders**, making prosecution hard, and stress the **emotional and financial harm**.

## Warning signs

- You've **never met in person** (or every meeting gets cancelled)
- Requests for money keep coming, each with a new crisis
- They discourage you from **telling friends or family**
- They avoid **live video calls**

## What to do

- Be very cautious meeting people online or on dating apps.
- **Talk to someone you trust**. A second opinion is the best defence.
- Read Netsafe's romance scam guidance.
- Call your bank and report to Police (105) if money's involved.
