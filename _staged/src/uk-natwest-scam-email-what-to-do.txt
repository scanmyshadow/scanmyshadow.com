=== BODY
## What the email looks like

According to [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC), the October 2026 version uses accurate NatWest branding and asks you to check your account information is up to date through a link. It looks routine, which is the point. “Please confirm your details” is the most boring sentence in banking, and boring is believable.

The link goes to a lookalike page that collects your customer number, PIN and password characters, and sometimes a one-time code.

## What NatWest says

From [NatWest's reporting page](https://www.natwest.com/support-centre/help-with-your-card/fraud/how-do-i-report-a-phishing-email-or-scam-email.html):

- NatWest **won't ask for personal information by email**.
- It would only ever ask for **random characters** from your PIN or password, never the full thing.
- If you received a suspicious email and haven't responded, forward it to **phishing@natwest.com**.

## Why this matters: the 2026 numbers

UK Finance's annual fraud report, [covered by The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5256106) in June 2026, put total UK payment fraud losses for 2025 at about **£1.28 billion**. **Unauthorised fraud**, where criminals use your stolen card or login, accounted for **£703.4 million**. Authorised push payment (APP) fraud, where you're tricked into sending money, rose **19%** to over **£576 million**, with impersonation scams among the types whose case numbers grew.

A phishing email is the front door to both kinds. Your login lets them spend; a follow-up "fraud team" call talks you into moving money yourself.

## Spot the fake

- a link to log in or “verify” (log in via the app or by typing natwest.com instead);
- a sender address that isn't a NatWest domain;
- requests for your full PIN or password;
- urgency: “your account will be restricted within 24 hours.”

## The follow-up call

Phishing emails are often step one. A day later, a "NatWest fraud team" may call to say your account was attacked and you need to move money to a "safe account". A genuine bank protecting your account freezes it; it doesn't ask you to move the money. Hang up and call the number on your card, ideally from another phone ([[uk-bank-impersonation-scams-what-to-know|how bank impersonation calls work]]).

## If you clicked and entered details

Call NatWest immediately: personal customers **03457 888444** (overseas +44 3457 888444), business **03457 114477**. Or dial **159** ([[uk-159-scam-hotline-how-it-works|how 159 works]]). Change your credentials and check recent payments. Report losses to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/).

## Report other phishing too

Forward scam emails to **report@phishing.gov.uk** (the NCSC's service) and scam texts to **7726**.

## Why you got it

NatWest phishing is sent to people who bank everywhere. Lists come from breached emails. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows which breaches include your address. See also [[uk-bank-impersonation-scams-what-to-know|UK bank impersonation scams]].

=== FAQ
Q: How do I report a NatWest phishing email?
A: Forward it to phishing@natwest.com if you haven't responded, then delete it.
Q: Does NatWest ask for my PIN or password by email?
A: No. NatWest says it won't ask for personal information by email and only ever asks for random characters from your PIN or password.
Q: I entered my details on a fake NatWest page. What now?
A: Call NatWest on 03457 888444 (personal) or 159 immediately and change your security details.
Q: Where else can I report phishing in the UK?
A: Forward emails to report@phishing.gov.uk and texts to 7726.
