=== TLDR
You list something on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist. A “buyer” says they've been scammed before and need to verify you're real, so they'll send a code to your phone. That code is a Google Voice verification code. If you share it, they get a Google Voice number linked to your phone number, which they use to scam others under your identity. Never share a code you didn't ask for; if you did, reclaim your number through Google.

=== BODY
## How it works

The [FTC has warned](https://www.ktvu.com/news/ftc-warns-of-google-voice-verification-scam) about this for years, and it's still everywhere:

1. You post an item for sale.
2. A “buyer” messages and asks for your phone number.
3. They say they want to make sure you're a real person, so they'll send a code.
4. A text arrives: “Your Google Voice verification code is 123456.”
5. They ask you to send it back.

If you do, they've set up a Google Voice number linked to your real number. They can now scam other people from a number that traces back to you, and may try to use your name and number to get into your accounts.

## Why it works

It flips the script. You're the cautious one, worried about scam buyers, and they appeal to that: “I've been scammed before, I just need to check you're legit.” It feels mutual and reasonable. It isn't.

## The rule

**Never share a verification code you didn't request yourself.** Not with buyers, not with “support,” not with anyone. Codes exist to prove that *you* are doing something. Handing one over lets someone else do it as you. See [[why-you-should-never-share-otp|why you should never share an OTP]].

## If you shared the code

Follow Google's steps to reclaim your number: sign up for Google Voice yourself, choose to link your number, and if it's already linked to another account, Google offers a way to reclaim it after verifying you own it. Then:

- watch for messages from people saying “you scammed me”;
- change passwords on accounts that use SMS codes and switch them to an authenticator app;
- report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

## Safer selling

Keep chats inside the marketplace app, meet in public (many police stations have safe exchange zones), take cash or an in-app payment, and ignore buyers who want codes, overpayments or courier pickups. Our guides on [[is-facebook-marketplace-safe-from-scams|Facebook Marketplace]] and [[is-craigslist-safe-from-scams|Craigslist]] cover the rest, and [[online-marketplace-scam-rules-craigslist-gumtree-kijiji-vinted|marketplace rules]] compares platforms.

## Check your number's exposure

Sellers publish their number and name constantly. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows whether your email is in breach data too, and [[where-do-phone-numbers-get-exposed|where phone numbers get exposed]] explains the phone side.

=== FAQ
Q: Why would a buyer ask me for a verification code?
A: To use it to create a Google Voice number linked to your phone number. It's a scam.
Q: What can a scammer do with my Google Voice code?
A: Set up a Google Voice number tied to your number and use it to scam others, and possibly target your accounts.
Q: How do I get my number back from Google Voice?
A: Follow Google's reclaim steps: try to link your number in Google Voice and choose to reclaim it if it's already in use.
Q: Where do I report this scam?
A: Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the marketplace where it happened.
