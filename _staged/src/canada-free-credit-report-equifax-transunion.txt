=== BODY
## Two bureaus, two reports

Canada has two credit reporting agencies, **Equifax** and **TransUnion**, and their reports can contain different information, as [Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/2015/06/how-to-get-a-free-copy-of-your-credit-report/) explains. A lender might check either, so fraud could appear on one and not the other.

## Four ways to get yours free

From the [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC)](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/order-credit-report.html), updated July 2026:

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Online** | Both bureaus offer free online access; you can download or print it. TransUnion calls its version the **Consumer Disclosure** |
| **By mail** | Send the bureau's request form with photocopies of **two valid IDs** |
| **By phone** | Call, answer identity questions and follow the instructions: Equifax **1-800-465-7166**, TransUnion **1-800-663-9980** |
| **In person** | Only in some provinces and territories, at a bureau office, with at least two IDs |

Go to **equifax.ca** and **transunion.ca** by typing the address yourself, and skip paid upsells you don't need. Checking your own report or score **doesn't affect** your credit rating.

## Is the credit score free too?

| | Equifax | TransUnion |
|---|---|---|
| Free score online | All provinces and territories | Quebec and Ontario only |
| Free score by phone or mail | Yes, with ID | Quebec and Ontario residents |

The FCAC says bureaus update your score **at least once a month**. Some banks also show a free score in their apps. If you use a third-party site for a score, the FCAC suggests checking it's legitimate, reading its privacy policy and finding out whether it **sells your data**.

## What to look for

- accounts or credit cards you didn't open;
- **credit inquiries** from lenders you never applied to;
- addresses you've never lived at;
- debts in **collections** you don't recognise;
- name spellings or employers you don't recognise.

## If you find fraud

1. **Contact the lender** to close the fraudulent account.
2. **Dispute** the entry with the bureau.
3. Add a **fraud alert** at both bureaus, free for six years ([[canada-equifax-transunion-fraud-alert-guide|how]]).
4. If you live in **Quebec or Ontario**, consider a free **credit lock (freeze)** as well ([[how-to-freeze-your-credit-canada|how freezes work]]).
5. Report to police and the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).
6. If your SIN was involved, follow [[canada-sin-stolen-what-to-do|Service Canada's steps]].

You can also add a short **explanation** to your report, which Consumer Protection BC puts at up to 100 words.

## How often to check

At least **once a year**, and right away after:

- a **data breach** notice, like [[canada-loblaw-data-breach-two-million-customers|Loblaw's]];
- a lost wallet or stolen mail;
- a scam call where you shared personal details.

Because online reports are free and update monthly, checking every few months costs nothing. The [FCAC](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2017/11/fcac_reminds_consumerscreditreportscanhelpprotectthemfromfinanci.html) has long reminded Canadians that checking credit reports helps protect against fraud. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] tells you whether your email has been in breaches, a good trigger for a credit check.

=== FAQ
Q: Is my credit report free in Canada?
A: Yes. Equifax and TransUnion both offer free online access, and will also send a free copy by mail or provide one by phone.
Q: Is my credit score free in Canada?
A: Equifax's score is free online in all provinces. TransUnion's is free online only for Quebec and Ontario residents.
Q: Does checking my own credit report lower my score?
A: No. The FCAC says checking your own report or score doesn't affect your credit rating.
Q: Why check both bureaus?
A: Their reports can contain different information, and lenders may use either.
Q: What are the phone numbers for Equifax and TransUnion Canada?
A: Equifax 1-800-465-7166 and TransUnion 1-800-663-9980.
Q: How often should I check my credit report?
A: At least once a year, and immediately after a breach or suspected identity theft.
