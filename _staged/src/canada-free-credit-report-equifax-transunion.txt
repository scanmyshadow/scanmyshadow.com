=== BODY
## Two bureaus, two reports

According to [Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/2015/06/how-to-get-a-free-copy-of-your-credit-report/), Canada has two credit reporting agencies, **Equifax** and **TransUnion**, and their reports can contain different information. A lender might check either, so fraud could appear on one and not the other.

## How to get yours

- **By mail**: both bureaus provide a free copy by mail; you'll need copies of **two pieces of ID**.
- **TransUnion**: request through its consumer disclosure page by mail, in person or by phone.
- **Equifax**: fill in its free credit report form; leave payment fields blank if you don't want to buy a score.
- **Online**: both bureaus also offer account-based online access on their own sites. Start from equifax.ca or transunion.ca, typed yourself, and skip paid upsells you don't need.

The [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2017/11/fcac_reminds_consumerscreditreportscanhelpprotectthemfromfinanci.html) has long reminded Canadians that checking credit reports helps protect against fraud.

## What to look for

- accounts or credit cards you didn't open;
- credit inquiries from lenders you never applied to;
- addresses you've never lived at;
- debts sent to collections you don't recognise.

## If you find fraud

1. Contact the lender to close the fraudulent account.
2. Dispute the entry with the bureau.
3. Add a fraud alert at both bureaus ([[canada-equifax-transunion-fraud-alert-guide|how]]).
4. Report to police and the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).
5. If your SIN was involved, follow [[canada-sin-stolen-what-to-do|Service Canada's steps]].

You can also add a short explanation to your report, which Consumer Protection BC puts at up to 100 words.

## How often

At least once a year, and right away after a breach like [[canada-loblaw-data-breach-two-million-customers|Loblaw's]]. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] tells you whether your email has been in breaches, a good trigger for a credit check.

=== FAQ
Q: Is my credit report free in Canada?
A: Yes. Both Equifax and TransUnion provide free reports, by mail with ID, and both offer online access.
Q: Why check both bureaus?
A: Their reports can contain different information, and lenders may use either.
Q: What should I look for on my credit report?
A: Unknown accounts, unfamiliar credit inquiries, addresses you never lived at and collections you don't recognise.
Q: How often should I check my credit report?
A: At least once a year, and immediately after a breach or suspected identity theft.
