=== TLDR
A CBC investigation reported in October 2026 that more than 1.5 million tips sent to Canadian Crime Stoppers programs over 25 years have leaked, after data was stolen in March from P3 Global Intel, the company that ran the tip database. Over 850,000 tips contained personal details such as addresses, phone numbers, emails and licence plates, and in 4,300+ cases tipsters gave their full names. If you ever left a tip, assume some details could be out there and check your exposure.

=== BODY
## What CBC found

[MobileSyrup's summary of the CBC investigation](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/10/21/canadian-crime-stoppers-tips-leak-report/) lays out the scale:

- **1.5 million+** tips from the past 25 years leaked;
- **850,000+** contained personally identifiable information about tipsters, people named in tips, staff or police;
- street addresses and phone numbers, **tens of thousands** of personal emails and licence plates;
- **hundreds** of banking details linked to cash rewards;
- **4,300+** tips where people gave their full names, even though Crime Stoppers tells tipsters not to.

CBC also reported that in hundreds of cases staff had flagged that a tipster's identity was compromised, yet the details stayed in the database unredacted.

## How it leaked

The data was stolen in March 2026 from P3 Global Intel, the Crime Stoppers affiliate that managed the tip database, by a hacktivist group calling itself Internet Yiff Machine. The non-profit Distributed Denial of Secrets later made it available to researchers and journalists. Back in June, [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/canada/article/crime-stoppers-cyber-breach-may-put-tipsters-lives-at-risk/) reported that the Canadian Crime Stoppers Association did not yet know whether tipsters' details had been exposed. An expert told CTV: “Individuals' lives could be at risk here.”

## Why this is different from a normal breach

Most breaches risk your money. This one risks retaliation. Tips include allegations about named people, sometimes in serious criminal cases. A leaked email or plate number linked to a tip can be enough for someone motivated to identify the person who reported them.

## If you ever left a Crime Stoppers tip

1. **Think about what you included.** Name, phone, email, address, licence plate or bank details for a reward are the key risks.
2. **If you feel at risk, contact your local police** and tell them you were a tipster affected by the leak.
3. **Reduce what's findable about you.** Remove yourself from people-search sites and check what Google shows for your name. Our guides on [[what-is-doxxing-what-to-do-if-your-info-is-posted|what to do if you're doxxed]] and [[canada-411-and-people-search-removal|people-search removal]] can help.
4. **Change the email you used** for sensitive things, or at least lock it down with two-factor authentication.
5. **Be wary of contact** that references a tip you made. Don't confirm anything to unexpected callers or messages.

## Data held for too long

CBC's core finding was that decades of sensitive records were kept longer than needed and left unredacted. That is a lesson for every organisation, and the reason privacy laws push [[data-minimisation-explained|data minimisation]]. In Canada you can ask an organisation what it holds on you under [[canada-pipeda-explained|PIPEDA]].
