=== TLDR
In **July 2025**, Qantas confirmed attackers reached a **third-party customer servicing platform** used by a call centre and took data on **5.7 million** customers. About **4 million** had names, emails and **Frequent Flyer details** exposed; about **1.7 million** also had some mix of **addresses (1.3M), birthdates (1.1M), phone numbers (900,000)**, gender and meal preferences. Qantas says **no cards, financial data, passports, passwords or PINs** were taken. Watch for Qantas-themed phishing.

=== BODY
## What happened

[Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/qantas-57m-data-breach/) reported that attackers accessed a third-party customer servicing platform via an attack on a **call centre**. The method bore hallmarks of the Scattered Spider group, known for talking helpdesk staff into resetting passwords, though attribution wasn't confirmed.

## What was taken

| Data | Customers |
|---|---|
| Name, email, Frequent Flyer details | ~4 million (1.2M of these name and email only) |
| Residential and/or business address | 1.3 million |
| Date of birth | 1.1 million |
| Phone number | 900,000 |
| Gender | 400,000 |
| Meal preferences | 10,000 |
| Cards, financial data, passports | **Not taken** |
| Passwords, PINs, logins | **Not taken** |

Ten thousand people's meal preferences are now in criminal hands. Vegetarians of Australia, stay alert. (Seriously, it's harmless on its own. The rest isn't.)

Qantas emailed affected customers with the specific data types taken.

## How to check

- Look for the Qantas email listing your data types.
- Check your email on our free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure checker]] to see this and other breaches.

## What Qantas advised

- Watch for **phishing emails, texts and calls**, especially ones claiming to be Qantas.
- Use **multi-factor authentication** on email and other accounts.
- **Never give passwords** or personal or financial information to anyone who contacts you claiming to be official.
- Check **Scamwatch** and the **Australian Cyber Security Centre** for current threats.
- See **IDCARE** and the **OAIC** for guidance.

## Scams to expect

With your name, email, Frequent Flyer number and maybe your birthday:

- "Your points are expiring, log in to redeem" emails (see [[australia-bank-rewards-points-expiring-sms-scam|rewards points scams]]);
- fake flight cancellation refunds;
- "Qantas security" calls asking you to verify your account.

Log in through the Qantas app or by typing qantas.com yourself. Our [[qantas-scam-what-to-know|Qantas scams guide]] covers more.

## Your rights

If you're unhappy with how Qantas handled your data, complain to Qantas first, then to the OAIC ([[australia-oaic-complaint-how-to|how]]).
