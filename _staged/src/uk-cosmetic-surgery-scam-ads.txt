=== BODY
## How it works

According to [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC), social media adverts lure people in with appealing prices for cosmetic procedures. Victims pay deposits. Then the procedure is cancelled and the company says it's in liquidation, so the money is gone.

Report Fraud received **49 reports between 2020 and 2025**, with losses of **£234,112**. That's nearly £4,800 per report, and many people never report at all because the topic feels private.

## Why it works

Cosmetic treatment is personal, often planned for months, and people compare prices online. A “limited slots” offer at half the usual price feels like luck. And shame keeps victims quiet, which is good news for the scammers.

## Checks before you pay a deposit

- **Check the surgeon**: in the UK, surgeons should be registered with the General Medical Council (GMC); check the GMC register yourself.
- **Check the clinic's regulator**: in England, clinics providing surgery should be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Other UK nations have their own regulators.
- **Check the company** on Companies House: how old is it, are accounts filed, any liquidations? ([[uk-companies-house-check-before-paying|how]])
- **Search reviews** beyond the clinic's own site and social media.
- **Pay by credit card** where possible; it gives more protection than a bank transfer.
- **Avoid pressure**: “book today” discounts and deposits by bank transfer to individuals are red flags.

## Overseas packages

Package deals abroad add more risk: aftercare, complications and getting money back across borders. Take extra care, and read [[investment-trading-app-scam-recovery|why cross-border recovery is hard]].

## If you've lost a deposit

Contact your card provider about a chargeback or Section 75 claim, then report to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/). Report the advert to the platform.
