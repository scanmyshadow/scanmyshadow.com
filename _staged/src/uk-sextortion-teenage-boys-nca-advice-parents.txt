=== TLDR
The National Crime Agency says financially motivated sextortion, mainly run by organised gangs in parts of West Africa and South East Asia, is targeting boys aged 15 to 17. Some cases go from first contact to blackmail in under an hour. In NCA research, 74% of boys didn't see requests for nude images as a warning sign and 73% didn't know how to report. === BODY
## What the NCA found

From the [NCA's campaign launch](https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/news/national-crime-agency-launches-online-campaign-to-tackle-sextortion-among-young-teenage-boys):

- **74%** of boys didn't fully understand sextortion;
- **74%** didn't see requests for nude images as a warning sign;
- **73%** didn't know how to report it;
- only **12%** thought they might be at risk;
- the CEOP Safety Centre received **380** sextortion reports in 2024;
- from January to May 2024, police averaged **117 reports a month** from under-18s, likely an undercount.

The NCA also alerted teachers in April 2024, reaching an estimated two-thirds of UK teaching staff.

## How it happens

- A friendly, flirty account (often posing as a girl of similar age) makes contact on social media or a game.
- The chat moves fast to swapping images, or images are faked.
- Then the threat: pay, or the images go to your friends and family.
- The NCA says some cases go from first message to blackmail **in under an hour**.

These gangs want money, not images. That's grimly useful to know: it means there's a business script, and scripts can be recognised.

## What to tell your teenager

Say it before anything happens, calmly:

- **It's never your fault**, even if you sent something. The NCA is explicit about this.
- **Don't pay.** Paying usually leads to more demands.
- **Stop replying, but don't delete everything**: screenshots help police.
- **Tell an adult you trust**, today.
- **Report** to the CEOP Safety Centre or police.

For removing images, the NSPCC and Childline's Report Remove tool exists for under-18s in the UK.
