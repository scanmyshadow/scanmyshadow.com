=== TLDR
In **June 2025**, AUSTRAC imposed a **$5,000 limit on cash deposits and withdrawals** at crypto ATMs, plus **scam warning signs**, tighter **transaction monitoring** and **enhanced customer checks**. Why: Australia's crypto ATMs exploded from **67 (Aug 2022)** to **1,819**, the third-largest fleet in the world. AUSTRAC found most users are **over 50**, making up almost **72% of transactions by value**, and many people aged 60–70 were scam victims. If anyone tells you to go to a crypto ATM, it's a scam.

=== BODY
## The new rules

From [Cointelegraph's report on AUSTRAC](https://cointelegraph.com/news/australia-new-crypto-atm-rules-amid-rising-scams):

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Cash limit** | A$5,000 per deposit or withdrawal |
| **Scam warnings** | Warning signs on machines |
| **Monitoring** | Stronger transaction monitoring |
| **Customer checks** | Enhanced due diligence |

The limits apply to crypto ATM providers now; AUSTRAC expects exchanges taking cash to consider similar limits.

## The numbers behind it

- **1,819** crypto ATMs in Australia, up from **67** in August 2022 (Coin ATM Radar).
- Australia is the **third-largest** crypto ATM hub after the US and Canada.
- The AFP received **150 reports** of crypto ATM scams from January 2024 to January 2025, with losses over **A$3.1 million**, which it called "the tip of the iceberg."
- AUSTRAC's taskforce found most users are **over 50**, making up nearly **72% of transactions by value**, and a "large number" of 60–70-year-olds were scam victims.

From 67 to 1,819 in under three years. Those machines aren't popping up in shopping centres because grandma suddenly got into Bitcoin.

## What AUSTRAC said

CEO **Brendan Thomas** called the rules "absolutely necessary" and said they aim to deter criminals from directing victims to crypto ATMs. The rules will be reviewed and adjusted if needed.

## How the scams use crypto ATMs

Scammers posing as **police, banks, government or a romantic partner** tell victims to withdraw cash and feed it into a crypto ATM, scanning a QR code that sends the money to the scammer's wallet. Once sent, it's almost impossible to get back. We've covered real cases in [[australia-tasmania-crypto-atm-scam-losses-1-5-million|Tasmania]] and [[australia-sydney-widow-crypto-atm-dating-app-430000-afp-austrac|Sydney]].

## The simple rule

**No legitimate organisation will ever ask you to pay with a crypto ATM.** Not the bank, not the ATO, not the police, not your online sweetheart.

## If you've paid

1. Call your bank (if cash came from your account) and police.
2. Report to ReportCyber and Scamwatch.
3. Beware **recovery scams** promising to get the crypto back for a fee.
