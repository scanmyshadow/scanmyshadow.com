=== TLDR
On **23 June 2026**, the **National Anti-Scam Centre (NASC)** emailed about **1,500 victims** after the director of **Blue Star Exchange Pty Ltd** was successfully prosecuted for **money laundering** following an **AFP** investigation. The AFP had obtained victims' contact details during the case. Genuine NASC contact exists, but the NASC says it will **never** text you **a number to call, links or attachments**. Fake "scam recovery" contacts are common, so verify everything.

=== BODY
## What happened

From [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/national-anti-scam-centre-contacted-victims-following-successful-money-laundering-prosecution):

- The **AFP** investigated, and the director of **Blue Star Exchange Pty Ltd** was **successfully prosecuted for money laundering**.
- The AFP obtained victims' **contact details** during the investigation and passed them to the NASC.
- The NASC emailed around **1,500 victims** on 23 June 2026.

This follows a similar effort where the [[australia-nasc-warns-10000-after-uk-crypto-wallet-investigation|NASC contacted more than 10,000 Australians]] after an international crypto investigation.

## How to tell real NASC contact from fake

The NASC says it **will never send you a text message with a number to call, or with links or attachments**.

Scammers know victims are desperate to recover money, and they copy official agencies. Fake "recovery" contacts often:

- say they can **get your money back**, for a **fee**;
- ask for **ID documents** or **bank details** to "release funds";
- pressure you with **deadlines**;
- claim to be from the AFP, NASC, ASIC or a "recovery firm."

**Government agencies don't charge you to recover scam losses.** That one rule kills most recovery scams.

## Stop, Check, Protect

- **Stop:** pause before giving money or personal information.
- **Check:** contact the organisation using details **you find yourself**, like scamwatch.gov.au.
- **Protect:** if you think you've been scammed, contact your bank and report to **ReportCyber**.

## Why victims get targeted twice

Victim lists are valuable. Once you've lost money, scammers may sell or reuse your details for a "second wave." We wrote about the same pattern in Canada: [[canada-fake-cafc-recovery-scam-54-million|fake CAFC recovery scams]].

## If you were a victim

- Keep records of your original loss and reports.
- Get free support from **IDCARE** (1800 595 160).
- See our [[australia-scamwatch-idcare-recovery-process|recovery process guide]].
