In **July 2026**, Which? warned about Facebook and Instagram ads promoting **fake apps** posing as **Google Authenticator, Disney+, Duolingo and Airbnb** that lead to **unlicensed online casinos** asking for a deposit. === BODY
## What Which? found

[Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) reported on **29 July 2026** that NordVPN had found a criminal network running Facebook and Instagram ads that impersonate **Google Authenticator, Disney+, Duolingo and Airbnb**. The "apps" lead to **unlicensed online casinos** that ask for a deposit, and Which? says some victims only realise after they've paid into a casino they've never heard of. You came for language lessons and left with a slot machine.

## How the trick works

[NordVPN's research](https://nordvpn.com/blog/nordvpn-pwa-scams-research/) (published 2 September 2026) walks through the steps:

1. A **Facebook or Instagram ad** leads to a **fake app-store page**, often on an unrelated throwaway domain.
2. A tracking system checks who's visiting. Real people see the fake install page; scanners and ad reviewers may see a harmless page instead.
3. "Install" adds a **progressive web app (PWA)**, a web shortcut on your home screen that opens a website rather than a real app.
4. The page may ask for **notification permission**, so it can send alerts later, and some interfere with the **Back** button.
5. The shortcut opens a **casino sign-up page** asking for an email, password, country, age confirmation and a first deposit, often with a big welcome bonus.
6. **Affiliates get paid** for every registration or deposit.

NordVPN says the network impersonated **more than 400 brands**, including Google Authenticator, Google Translate, Gemini and YouTube Kids, plus banks, lotteries, travel, streaming and VPN apps. NordVPN chief technology officer **Marijus Briedis** called it "essentially trust laundering."

[Malwarebytes' March 2026 report](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/scams/2026/03/friendlydealer-mimics-official-app-stores-to-push-unvetted-gambling-apps) on a similar kit, **FriendlyDealer**, found **1,500+ domains** and **20+ casino brands** generated from one configuration file, with fake reviews on the fake store pages. Malwarebytes says this campaign doesn't steal passwords or install traditional malware, but pushes people into **unregulated gambling** with no age checks, deposit limits or consumer protection.

## Why "unlicensed" matters

UK-licensed gambling sites must verify age, offer safer-gambling tools and follow complaint rules. NordVPN lists what you risk on an unlicensed site: **poor age checks, unclear deposit rules, unfair bonus terms, refused payouts** and aggressive marketing.

The Gambling Commission calls the illegal market "unsafe, unfair and criminal." According to [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/featurednews/2025/09/19/ukgc-black-market/), its research found:

- some people use illegal sites to **get around GAMSTOP self-exclusion** or spending limits, and those people are more likely to experience gambling harm;
- some offshore sites openly advertise as **"non-Gamstop casinos"**;
- many use **Gambling Commission and GambleAware logos** to look regulated.

So a logo on the page proves nothing. Check the operator's name on the **Gambling Commission's public register** before depositing anywhere.

## How to remove a fake app

| If you... | Do this |
|---|---|
| Only opened the page | Close it; don't install or allow notifications; report the ad |
| Added the shortcut | Delete it, revoke notification permission and clear the site's data in your browser |
| Registered at the casino | Don't deposit more; change any reused password; save evidence |
| Deposited money | Contact your bank or card provider promptly |
| Entered real logins (e.g. Google) | Change the password, turn on two-step verification, sign out other sessions |

That advice comes from NordVPN. On **Android**, clear site data in Chrome's site settings; on **iPhone**, delete the home-screen icon and clear the site's data in Safari settings.

**Report the ad** on Facebook or Instagram using the three dots in the top-right corner of the ad, then **Report**.

## Spot the fake next time

- **Real installs open the Play Store or App Store app**, not a web page that looks like it.
- **Check the address bar:** NordVPN says scammers count on people checking the logo, not the URL.
- **Search for the app yourself** in the official store and check the developer name.
- **A notification request during "install"** is a warning sign.
- If an "authenticator" app starts talking about free spins, you have your answer.

Fake apps are a wider problem: in October 2026 Which? also reported McAfee finding **970 fake apps** on Google Play targeting UK users. See also [[uk-copycat-apps-fake-websites-65-brands|fake apps on copycat sites]] and [[scam-online-casino-websites-australia|scam casino websites]].

A: Which? reported fakes posing as Google Authenticator, Disney+, Duolingo and Airbnb. NordVPN found one network impersonating more than 400 brands.
Q: Are these real apps?
A: Usually not. They're web shortcuts (PWAs) installed from fake store pages, not apps from Google Play or the App Store.
Q: Is it dangerous to gamble on an unlicensed site?
A: Unlicensed sites can lack age checks, deposit limits and consumer protection, and may refuse payouts.

A: Not necessarily. The Gambling Commission says many offshore sites copy its logo. Check the operator on its public register.
