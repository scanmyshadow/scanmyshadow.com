=== TLDR
Canada Computers & Electronics says **guest checkouts made online between December 29 and January 22** were exposed to unauthorized access, including personal details and **credit card information**. Customers who logged in with a member account and in-store shoppers were not affected. Affected customers are being offered **two years of credit monitoring**. If you checked out as a guest in that window, call your card issuer and ask for a new card.

=== BODY
## What happened

Canada Computers is a Canadian electronics retailer with about 30 stores, mainly in Ontario, Quebec and BC. According to [TEISS](https://www.teiss.co.uk/cyber-threats/canada-computers-confirms-data-breach-exposing-customer-personal-and-credit-card-information-17028), the company learned of the breach on **January 22** and started notifying customers on **January 25**, after revising dates it had first given.

## Who is affected

| Shopper | Affected? |
|---|---|
| Online **guest checkout**, Dec 29 to Jan 22 | Yes |
| Online, logged into a member account | No, per the company |
| In-store purchase | No, per the company |

The company describes the number affected as "small" or "limited," without a figure. An independent forensic firm is investigating how it happened.

Yes, this is the rare case where being lazy and not making an account was the wrong call. Usually it's the opposite.

## What was exposed

Personal details entered during guest checkout (think name, address, email, phone) **plus the credit card details** used. That combination is what card fraudsters want.

## What to do

1. **Call your card issuer** using the number on the back of your card. Tell them about the breach and ask for a replacement card.
2. **Check statements** from late December onward for small "test" charges, which often come before big ones.
3. **Sign up for the two years of credit monitoring** offered in the notification.
4. **Watch for phishing.** Fake "refund for the breach" emails are a classic follow-up. Canada Computers won't need your card number again to send you anything.
5. **Report card fraud** to your issuer first, then to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).

## Card fraud is usually reversible

Unauthorized credit card charges reported promptly are generally reversed by issuers. The bigger risk is the personal data that came with the card. Run a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] to see where else your email turns up.

=== FAQ
Q: Who was affected by the Canada Computers data breach?
A: Customers who used online guest checkout between December 29 and January 22.
Q: Were in-store customers affected?
A: No. The company says in-store purchases and logged-in member accounts were not affected.
Q: Was credit card information stolen?
A: Yes. Personal details and associated credit card information from guest checkouts were exposed.
Q: Is Canada Computers offering credit monitoring?
A: Yes, two years of credit monitoring and identity protection for impacted customers.
