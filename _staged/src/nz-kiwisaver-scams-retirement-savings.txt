=== TLDR
At **65**, Kiwis can access their **KiwiSaver**, and scammers know it. They pose as **investment firms, banks or brokers**, use **fake comparison tools** to harvest contact details, and push **quick, high, low-risk returns** with a request to **keep it secret**. One **72-year-old** Auckland man lost **$209,000** to fake "British brokers." Before moving any money, check the provider on the **FSPR** and **FMA warnings**, and call your existing provider.

=== BODY
## How KiwiSaver scams work

From [BNZ](https://verify.bnz.co.nz/personal-banking/life-moments/protecting-your-retirement-nest-egg-from-scammers):

- Scammers pose as **reputable investment firms or banks**, targeting people **approaching 65**.
- **Fake online comparison tools** collect your contact details; then a "**broker**" calls.
- Offers promise **quick, high returns with minimal risk**, and ask you to **keep it private**.

The [Retirement Commission](https://retirement.govt.nz/news/latest-news/kiwisaver-retirement-accounts-prime-target-for-scammers) noted fraudsters target people who suddenly have **access to large sums**, and pressure them to move KiwiSaver to **unverified** new providers.

## A real case

The Retirement Commission described a **72-year-old Auckland man** who lost **$209,000** to two men posing as **British brokers** for a Hong Kong asset manager. He only realised after seeing an **FMA warning**.

Lifetime of saving, one phone call. That's why this one deserves ten minutes of your attention.

## Red flags

- Pressure to **commit fast** ("limited spots")
- Returns **too good to be true**
- Requests to **keep it secret** (even from family)
- Payment by **gift card, money transfer or crypto**
- Email addresses with **look-alike characters** ("1" for "l")

## How to check before you move a cent

1. **Don't use** contact details from emails, links or messages. Find the firm's official **NZ website** yourself.
2. Check the firm on the **FMA** website and the **Financial Service Providers Register** ([[nz-fma-fake-nz-registered-investment-firms|how]]).
3. Check the FMA's **warnings and alerts**.
4. Make sure **email domains match** the official website.
5. **Talk to your current KiwiSaver provider** or a registered financial adviser first.

## Before a big withdrawal

BNZ suggests considering **regular withdrawals or smaller lump sums**, and reviewing your fund with your provider. A big balance sitting in a bank account makes you more visible to scammers.

## If you've paid

Call your bank immediately, then report to Police (105) and the FMA. Beware **recovery scams**.

Q: Why do scammers target people turning 65 in NZ?
A: At 65 people can access KiwiSaver savings, so scammers target those with sudden access to large sums.
Q: How do I check a KiwiSaver or investment provider is real?
A: Find its official NZ website yourself, check the FMA and Financial Service Providers Register, and review FMA warnings.
