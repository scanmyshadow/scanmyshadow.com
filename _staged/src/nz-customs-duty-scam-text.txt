=== TLDR
Scam texts and emails say your **package has been in storage** and will be **returned to sender** unless you pay a small **"customs fee"** through a link. NZ Customs says it **does not contact individuals or businesses about paying customs duty**, and it **doesn't physically hold** imported goods: freight forwarders do. Don't click. Forward the text to **7726**, then delete it.

=== BODY
## The scam

From [NZ Customs](https://www.customs.govt.nz/about-us/news/important-notices/customs-duty-scam-returns):

- A text or email impersonating NZ Customs says: "Urgent reminder: Dear, your package has been in our storage since last week. Resolve or we will return it to the sender."
- Act now or it'll be **returned to the sender**.
- A link leads to a **multi-step** process to pay a small **"customs fee."**

The "multi-step" part is deliberate. By step three, you've invested enough effort that typing your card number feels like the natural finish line.

## What Customs actually does

- "New Zealand Customs do not contact individuals or businesses about paying customs duty."
- Customs **doesn't physically receive** imported goods. They're held at warehouses run by **licensed freight forwarders** (Customs-controlled Areas).

So a message from "Customs" asking you to pay to release a parcel fails on both counts.

## It keeps coming back

This isn't new. In November 2021, [Customs warned](https://www.customs.govt.nz/about-us/news/media-releases/customs-warns-of-scam-text-messages-in-circulation/) about texts saying "there is an unpaid Customs charge" and "Customs Duty is still pending... we will send your order back." Its chief security officer Jamie Bamford was blunt: "Customs will never text people to inform them that they owe charges," and "Customs does not text!" The wording changes; the trick doesn't.

[Netsafe](https://netsafe.org.nz/scams/courier-delivery-scams) says delivery scams spike around **Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas**, when people are expecting parcels. It lists the common versions:

| Version | What happens |
|---|---|
| "Customs fee" | Pay a small duty to release a parcel |
| "Missed delivery" | Fake NZ Post or DHL message with a link to rearrange |
| "Approve your pickup time" | Followed by a "small" fee that can lead to **large unauthorised withdrawals** |
| Prize or survey | Claim a parcel prize or answer a survey |
| Malicious app | A link that installs an app on your phone |

Netsafe also notes scammers can get your name and address from **public records and data breaches**, so a message that knows where you live still isn't proof it's real.

## If you're genuinely expecting an import

Check tracking with the **courier or retailer** directly, through their official app or website. If a real fee is due, it comes from the courier or freight forwarder, not a random text.

## What to do

1. **Don't respond** or click any links.
2. **Forward texts to 7726** (free) ([[nz-report-scam-text-7726|how]]), then delete.
3. **Entered card details?** Call your bank now.
4. **Tapped a link on your phone?** Netsafe suggests running a malware scan and contacting your phone company; in some cases you may need a factory reset.
5. Report losses to **Police** (105) and **Netsafe**.

=== FAQ
Q: Does NZ Customs text people about fees?
A: NZ Customs says it does not contact individuals or businesses about paying customs duty.
Q: Does NZ Customs hold my parcel?
A: No. Imported goods are held at warehouses run by licensed freight forwarders, not by Customs itself.
Q: When are delivery scam texts most common?
A: Netsafe says they spike around Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Christmas, when people are expecting parcels.
Q: What should I do with a fake Customs text?
A: Don't click, forward it to 7726 and delete it. If you paid, call your bank.
