=== TLDR
On **10 July 2025**, Associate Justice Minister **Nicole McKee** announced New Zealand would **ban crypto ATMs** through anti-money laundering reforms. She called them a "**sound form of money laundering**" and cited **$107 million** laundered through criminal organisations. Provider **CoinFlip**, which runs about **120 machines**, called it "a step backward." For scam victims, the lesson is the same either way: **no legitimate person ever asks you to pay at a crypto ATM.**

=== BODY
## The announcement

According to [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/07/10/crypto-atms-to-be-banned-in-money-laundering-crackdown):

- Crypto ATMs will be banned under proposed changes to NZ's **AML/CFT** regime.
- Minister McKee said the machines are a "sound form of money laundering."
- She cited a case where crypto bought meth sent to New Zealand, and said she was "alarmed" by crypto financing crime and weapons.
- She pointed to **$107 million** laundered through criminal groups.

At the time of the report, the ban was proposed, and no start date was given.

## The industry's view

**CoinFlip**, one of the largest operators with around **120** NZ machines, called the ban a "step backward" that doesn't fix the underlying problem. It suggested alternatives like **wallet pinning**, **pre-transaction monitoring** and **real-time scam warnings**.

## Why scammers love crypto ATMs

Crypto ATMs let a victim turn cash into crypto in minutes and send it to a wallet controlled by a scammer, often by scanning a QR code the scammer sends. There's no bank in the middle to ask "are you sure?" and no way to reverse it.

We've seen the playbook across the Tasman: Australia went from 67 crypto ATMs to over 1,800 in three years, and AUSTRAC found most heavy users were over 50, many of them scam victims ([[australia-crypto-atm-rules-austrac-5000-limit|Australia's crackdown]]).

## Other ways crypto scams reach Kiwis

Banning machines doesn't stop crypto scams. Watch for:

- **Fake police** asking about your crypto, then leading you to enter your seed phrase on a fake site ([[nz-fake-police-crypto-seed-phrase-scam|NZ police crypto scam]]);
- **Deepfake politician ads** pushing crypto platforms ([[nz-deepfake-luxon-investment-scam-ads|deepfake Luxon scam]]);
- **Investment chat groups** promising guaranteed returns.

## The simple rule

If anyone, whether a "bank investigator", a "police officer" or an online romance, tells you to buy crypto to protect or grow your money: it's a scam. Hang up, call your bank, report to Police on 105.
