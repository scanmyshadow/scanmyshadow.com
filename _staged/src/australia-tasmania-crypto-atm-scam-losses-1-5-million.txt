=== TLDR
Tasmania Police reported in late September 2026 that Tasmanians lost $1.5 million through crypto ATMs in just three months, deposited under pressure from scammers. A 2025 police review of the state's 15 biggest crypto ATM users found every one was a scam victim. AUSTRAC brought in cash limits and warnings in September 2025, but losses continue. If someone you met online or by phone asks you to put cash into a crypto ATM, it's a scam.

=== BODY
## The numbers

According to [Crypto News Australia](https://cryptonews.com.au/news/crypto-atm-scam-losses-hit-1-5m-in-tasmania-despite-nationwide-crackdown-135151/):

- Tasmanians lost **$1.5 million** to crypto ATM scams in three months (per a late-September police report);
- Tasmania has fewer than **30** crypto ATMs, up from one in 2021; Australia has close to **2,000**;
- a 2025 review of the state's **15 highest-value** crypto ATM users found **no legitimate activity**. All 15 were linked to scams, with $900,000 put through ATMs and $2.5 million lost in total.

That last point is remarkable. Police looked at the heaviest users of these machines in one state and found only victims.

## Which scams send people to the machines

Fake investments, romance scams, “recovery” scams, fake government or utility bills, tech support scams and job scams. Different stories, same ending: a stranger walks you to a crypto ATM, sometimes staying on the phone the whole time.

## Why the crackdown hasn't worked yet

AUSTRAC introduced cash limits, mandatory scam warnings and tougher customer checks for crypto ATM operators in September 2025. Warnings on a screen don't do much when a “bank investigator” or online partner is in your ear telling you to ignore them. Overseas, some places have gone further: see how [[us-wisconsin-crypto-atm-law-1000-cap-refunds|Wisconsin capped daily transactions]] and the [[us-crypto-kiosk-complaints-by-state-ic3-2025-supplement|FBI's crypto kiosk data]].

## The red flag

Detective Sergeant Paul Turner of Tasmania Police named the biggest one: being “asked to deposit cash into a cryptocurrency ATM by someone you have only met online or spoken to over the phone.”

No bank, government department, utility, employer or genuine partner will ask that. Ever.

## If you or someone you know is mid-scam

- Stop. Don't make another deposit, even if they say one more payment releases everything.
- Call your bank and report it to [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover/report) and [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/).
- Beware of “recovery” offers that follow: see [[crypto-scam-recovery-realistic-expectations|realistic crypto recovery]].
