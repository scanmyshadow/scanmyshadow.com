=== TLDR
407 ETR warned in November 2025 of rising scam texts and websites impersonating it, using urgent language and links to collect personal and card details. 407 ETR says it will never ask for passwords, PINs or credit card details in texts or emails. Check your account only at 407etr.com or in the official 407 ETR app, and report scams to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

=== BODY
## The text

It usually goes something like: “407 ETR: You have an outstanding toll balance of $6.99. Pay today to avoid a late fee of $48.” A link leads to a convincing copy of the 407 ETR site asking for your card.

According to [407 ETR's November 2025 release](https://newsroom.407etr.com/2025-11-07-407-ETR-urges-customers-to-beware-of-messages-impersonating-the-Company-amid-increasing-reports-of-scams), the fakes use:

- urgent language;
- links to malicious sites;
- convincing branding and wording.

## Why it works

Hundreds of thousands of Ontario drivers use the 407, often without a transponder, and bills arrive later. So an unpaid-toll message is plausible for a lot of people. The small amount lowers your guard; the card details are what they're after.

## What 407 ETR says

- **“407 ETR will never ask for passwords, PINs or credit card details in text messages or emails.”**
- Official website: **407etr.com**. Official app: the **407 ETR mobile app**.
- Log in to My Account only through those.
- Be wary of any link to a domain other than 407etr.com.

## What to do

- Don't click. Open the app or type 407etr.com to check your balance.
- Forward the text to **7726**.
- Report it to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).
- If you paid, call your card issuer right away.
