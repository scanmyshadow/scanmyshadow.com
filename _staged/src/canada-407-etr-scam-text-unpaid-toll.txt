=== TLDR
Fake 407 ETR texts claim a toll is **overdue**, threaten a **penalty** and link to a site that isn't **407etr.com**. In 2025, 407 ETR's security teams took down **more than 1,100 fraudulent domains** impersonating it. Real payment reminders come from an **official six-digit short code** and include your **first name**, and 407 ETR says it "will never ask for passwords, PINs or credit card details through any communication channel." Check your balance only at **407etr.com** or in the **407 ETR app**, forward fakes to **7726** and report to the **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre**.

=== BODY
## What the fake looks like

407 ETR's [March 2026 Fraud Prevention Month release](https://newsroom.407etr.com/2026-03-05-407-ETR-highlights-Fraud-Prevention-Month-with-tips-to-help-customers-spot-and-avoid-scams) lists the common themes:

- a claim that a **toll payment is overdue**;
- a threat of a **penalty** if you don't act right away;
- **urgent or threatening** language;
- a **link to a website that isn't 407etr.com**.

[MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/blogs/407-etr-unpaid-toll-text-scam/) recorded one version headed "407 ETR: Unpaid Toll Reminder," warning that unpaid tolls "may result in the revocation of your driver's license," with a link to an address beginning **407etr.com-** followed by a different domain. That's the trick: the real brand name sits at the front of the link, and the actual website is whatever comes after the dash.

## How big is this?

Big enough that 407 ETR runs a takedown operation. In 2025 its cybersecurity teams identified and took down **more than 1,100 fraudulent domains** impersonating the company, according to the March 2026 release. That's roughly three fake 407 websites a day, every day, for a year. In November 2025 the company had already warned of a "surge" of impersonation messages ([407 ETR, Nov 2025](https://newsroom.407etr.com/2025-11-07-407-ETR-urges-customers-to-beware-of-messages-impersonating-the-Company-amid-increasing-reports-of-scams); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/beware-407-etr-seeing-increasing-reports-of-phishing-scams/)).

## Real reminder or fake? The quick test

| Clue | Real 407 ETR | Fake |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | Official **six-digit short code** | A random mobile number, email or long number |
| Greeting | Uses your **first name** | Generic or none |
| Tone | No threats | "Pay today or lose your licence" |
| Link | **407etr.com** only | 407etr.com-something, or another domain |
| Asks for | Never passwords, PINs or card details | Card number, login, personal details |

407 ETR says it does contact customers by text, email, phone and mailed letter, so a message from 407 isn't automatically fake. What it won't do is ask for sensitive details or use threatening language to rush you.

## Why it works

407 ETR does send real payment reminders by text, so a toll message doesn't sound absurd, especially if you've driven the 407 recently. The scammers aren't after the toll; they're after your card details.

## What to do

1. **Don't click.** Open the 407 ETR app or type **407etr.com** and log in to **My Account** to check your balance.
2. **Forward the text to 7726** so your carrier can block it.
3. **Report it** to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).
4. **If you paid or typed your card number**, call your card issuer right away and ask for a new card.
5. **If you entered a password** that you use elsewhere, change it everywhere.

407 ETR also keeps a fraud awareness page at **407etr.com/fraudawareness**. Its CIO, Ali Razavi, put the rule simply: "customers should always verify communications through official 407 ETR channels."

## Same scam, other places

Toll texts are a global favourite: see [[ez-pass-unpaid-toll-text-scam|E-ZPass in the US]]. Peel police also investigated fake company plates used to dodge 407 tolls ([[canada-project-octane-peel-police-fake-company-plates-407-tolls|Project Octane]]).
