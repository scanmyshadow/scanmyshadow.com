=== TLDR
On **10 June 2026**, MBIE published the **New Zealand Anti-Scam Alliance**'s 2026 work programme. Scams cost Kiwis **$265 million (gross)** in the year to **October 2025**. The 2026 plan: **expand bank Confirmation of Payee**, **review the telco Scam Prevention Code**, and **build better scam reporting tools**, plus smarter data sharing and clearer public guidance. The Alliance started in **July 2025** and brings together banks, telcos, digital platforms, consumer groups and government.

=== BODY
## The numbers

From [MBIE](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/about/news/new-actions-to-protect-kiwis-from-scams):

- **$265 million** gross scam losses in the year to October 2025.
- Alliance formed **July 2025**.

(Netsafe and GASA put the broader estimate at around **$3 billion** for 2025 once unreported losses are included; see [[nz-scam-statistics-2026-netsafe-data|NZ scam statistics 2026]].)

## What's in the 2026 programme

| Action | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Expand Confirmation of Payee** | More payments checked for name–account mismatches before money leaves |
| **Review the telco Scam Prevention Code** | Potentially more blocking of scam calls and texts |
| **Better scam reporting tools** | Easier, joined-up reporting |
| **Disruption tools and data sharing** | Faster takedowns and freezes |
| **Clearer public guidance** | One set of advice instead of ten |

## Who's in the Alliance

Banks, telecommunications providers, digital platforms, consumer groups and government agencies. That matters, because a scam usually touches all of them: a social media ad, a phone call, a text, and a bank transfer.

## What already exists

- **Bank reimbursement rules** since November 2025 ([[nz-bank-scam-reimbursement-rules-explained|explained]]).
- The **telco Scam Prevention Code**, with blocking and in-store ID checks for SIM swaps ([[nz-telco-scam-prevention-code-explained|explained]]).
- **7726** for scam texts ([[nz-report-scam-text-7726|how to use it]]).

## What you can do now

1. **Use Confirmation of Payee** prompts; don't skip a mismatch warning.
2. **Report every scam** you see; reports drive blocking.
3. **Verify before you pay**, using contact details you already trust.

A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] shows which breaches your email is in, which is often how scammers find you in the first place.
