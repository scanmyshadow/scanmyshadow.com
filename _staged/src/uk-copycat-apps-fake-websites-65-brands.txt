=== TLDR
Which? warned in August 2026 about scam texts offering airline discounts, job offers or document renewals that lead to copycat websites pushing fake apps. === BODY
## How it works

According to [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC):

1. A text promises something useful: an airline discount, a job, a document renewal.
2. The link goes to a copycat website for a well-known brand.
3. The site pushes you to download “the app” directly, outside the official app store.
4. The fake app can **spy on your screen** and **activate your microphone and camera**.

More than **65 brands** were impersonated, including airlines such as Ryanair, government services and retailers.

## Why it's dangerous

A fake website steals what you type once. A fake app with screen access can watch everything: banking logins, one-time codes, private messages. It's the difference between someone reading your postcard and moving into your spare room.

## How to protect yourself

- **Install apps only from Google Play or Apple's App Store**, and preferably via a link on the company's own website.
- **Never install an app from a link in a text.**
- On Android, **keep “Install unknown apps” turned off** for your browser and messaging apps.
- Check the developer name before installing.
- Be suspicious of any “app” that asks for Accessibility or screen-recording permissions it doesn't need.

Even official stores aren't perfect: McAfee found 1,900+ fake apps on Google Play in 2026 ([[uk-fake-android-apps-google-play-rewafake-subscriptions|details]]).

## If you installed one

1. Disconnect from the internet.
2. Uninstall the app (on Android, check Settings > Apps and remove device admin rights first if needed).
3. Run a security scan; see [[how-to-remove-spyware-from-android|removing spyware from Android]].
4. From a different device, change your banking and email passwords and call your bank or **159**.
5. Forward the original text to **7726** and report the site to the NCSC.
