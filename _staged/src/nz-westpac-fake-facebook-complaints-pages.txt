=== TLDR
In **August 2026**, Westpac NZ warned that scammers built **fake Facebook complaints pages** that reply to people **posting publicly about service problems**. Posing as Westpac, they offer a **quick resolution**, collect your **name, email and contact details**, then contact you about **"compensation"** and ask for **banking or card info**. Westpac had the page removed, but expects **more to pop up**, including for other businesses.

=== BODY
## How it works

From [MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/nz/specialty/residential/fake-facebook-complaints-pages-used-to-scam-westpac-nz-customers/586447) (18 August 2026):

1. You post publicly that your bank (or any business) let you down.
2. A page that looks like **Westpac NZ** replies: "Sorry to hear that, let's sort it fast."
3. They ask for your **full name, email and contact details**.
4. Later, someone contacts you to **"process compensation"** and asks for **banking or card information**, sometimes asking you to call an **overseas number**.

Venting online is human. Scammers are counting on it. The angrier the post, the faster people accept a helping hand.

## What Westpac said

- Peter Barnes, head of customer care operations: some customers "may have been tricked by the fake page."
- "As known sites are shut down, others are likely to pop up."
- Westpac's financial crime team knows of **similar fake pages targeting other businesses**.

## How to spot a fake complaints page

- **Not verified**, recently created, or few followers
- Replies **within minutes** to public posts, asking you to DM details
- Offers **compensation** before anyone's looked at your account
- Moves you to **email, phone or WhatsApp**
- Asks for **card details, PINs or passwords**

## What to do instead

- Complain through the bank's **app, website or published phone number**.
- Never share account or card details in reply to an **unsolicited offer**.
- Westpac never asks for your **online banking password or PIN**.
- Be suspicious of any request to call an **overseas number**.

## If you shared details

Contact Westpac **immediately**; early reporting improves recovery chances. Report the page to Facebook and the scam to Netsafe. See [[nz-how-to-report-a-scam|how to report a scam in NZ]].
