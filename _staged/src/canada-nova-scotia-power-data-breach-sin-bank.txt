=== TLDR
Hackers had access to Nova Scotia Power systems from **March 19 to April 25, 2025**. About **280,000** people were notified. Stolen data includes **names, addresses, birth dates, driver's licence numbers, bank account numbers and, for some, Social Insurance Numbers**. The company confirmed the data was **published on the dark web**. The federal Privacy Commissioner opened an investigation. If you're a customer, treat this as a long-term identity risk, not a two-year one.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [The Record](https://therecord.media/thousands-impacted-cyber-nova-scotia), attackers were inside Nova Scotia Power's systems for about five weeks, from **March 19 to April 25, 2025**. The utility isolated servers to stop further intrusion; its customer care line and MyAccount portal were affected, but the power stayed on.

[Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11201691/federal-privacy-czar-nova-scotia-power-data-breach) reported the company confirmed the stolen data was **published on the dark web**, and that federal Privacy Commissioner **Philippe Dufresne** opened an investigation on **May 28, 2025** after receiving complaints.

## What data was exposed

Exposure varied by customer, but the list includes:

| Data | Risk |
|---|---|
| Names, addresses, birth dates, emails | Targeted phishing, impersonation |
| Driver's licence numbers | ID fraud |
| **Bank account numbers** (some customers) | Fraudulent debits, payment scams |
| **Social Insurance Numbers** (some customers) | Credit fraud, tax and benefit fraud |
| Billing, payment and credit history | Convincing "account problem" scams |
| Power use, service requests, correspondence | Social engineering detail |

## The criticism

Experts quoted by Global News said the notification letters lacked detail about what could go wrong, and that **two years** of TransUnion credit monitoring may not be long enough since criminals can use data well beyond 24 months. A SIN doesn't expire, after all.

## What customers should do

1. **Sign up for the free credit monitoring** offered in your letter, through the official link.
2. **Place a fraud alert** with Equifax and TransUnion. See our [[canada-equifax-transunion-fraud-alert-guide|fraud alert guide]].
3. **Tell your bank** your account number was exposed and watch for unfamiliar debits.
4. **If your SIN was exposed**, follow our [[canada-sin-stolen-what-to-do|stolen SIN guide]] and check your CRA My Account for changes.
5. **Distrust "Nova Scotia Power" calls and texts** about overdue bills or refunds. Scammers now know your billing history. Call the number on your real bill.
6. **Keep checking for years**, not months. Order your [[canada-free-credit-report-equifax-transunion|free credit report]] regularly.

## Your rights

Under PIPEDA, you can complain to the Privacy Commissioner if you think a company mishandled your data. See [[canada-breach-notification-letter-what-to-do|breach notification rights in Canada]].
