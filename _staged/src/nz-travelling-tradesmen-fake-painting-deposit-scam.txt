=== TLDR
In **Auckland**, two **UK nationals** allegedly dropped flyers for a fake painting company, "**Everlast Home Improvements**." A **Mt Albert** homeowner paid a **$27,000 deposit**; someone did **two days of poor sanding** and never came back. One man pleaded guilty; the other **left NZ** and faces arrest if he returns. Auckland City Financial Crime Unit says it's prosecuted **14 foreign nationals** in **three years** for scams including **roof and paint work**. If you paid a deposit for work never finished, **report it**.

=== BODY
## What happened

From [NZ Police](https://www.police.govt.nz/news/release/%E2%80%98think-again%E2%80%99-police-send-strong-message-visitors-scamming-kiwis) (21 May 2026):

- A flyer in a **Mt Albert** mailbox advertised "**Everlast Home Improvements**."
- The homeowner paid a **$27,000 deposit**.
- A man did a **poor sanding job** on the exterior over **two days**, then vanished.
- **Andrew Yeshua Glauberman** (21, UK) pleaded guilty to two fraud offences and several shoplifting offences; he was held in custody until sentencing.
- **Thomas Jay Taylor** (29, UK) has **left New Zealand**; a warrant is out.

Police say most offenders face **jail** and, ultimately, **removal from the country**, working with Immigration NZ.

## A bigger pattern

Auckland's Financial Crime Unit has prosecuted **14 foreign nationals in three years** for scams including:

- fake **Police** calls and **text** scams
- **foreign exchange** and **"blessing"** scams
- **term deposit** scams
- stolen credit card "**ghost taping**"
- **travelling trade** scams: **roofing and painting**

## Red flags of a travelling tradesman scam

- **Flyers** or door-knocks offering a deal "because we're in the area"
- **Big deposits** up front
- No **verifiable business address**, website or reviews
- **Cash or bank transfer only**, no proper invoice
- Pressure to **book today**

$27,000 up front for a paint job is a lot of trust to hand to a flyer. Legit tradies are usually happy to show you previous work and a real address.

## Protect yourself

1. **Get multiple quotes** from local, established businesses.
2. **Check** company details on the Companies Register and online reviews.
3. **Pay small deposits** and stage payments to completed work.
4. **Get a written contract** with names, addresses and timelines.

## Report it

Paid a deposit for work that wasn't done? Police want to hear, **even if you're not sure it was a scam**: **105.police.govt.nz** or **105**. More: [[nz-how-to-report-a-scam|how to report a scam in NZ]].
