=== TLDR
On **Sunday 22 February 2026**, **MediMap**, a privately owned medication portal used by **aged care, disability, hospice and community care** providers, detected "unauthorised entry" and went offline. Attackers had **altered patient records**, **marking living patients as deceased** and changing names. The Aged Care Association said up to **60%** of aged care facilities could be using it. PM **Christopher Luxon** called it "concerning, particularly when it follows Manage My Health," and the government's cyber action plan now asks the Ministry of Justice to look at **civil penalties** for privacy failures.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/alert-nat/587773/patient-data-changed-as-major-nz-health-app-medimap-hacked) (24 February 2026):

- MediMap found **"unauthorised entry"**; the problem began around **1:30pm on Sunday** and the platform was put into maintenance mode as a precaution.
- Some patient information was **altered**, including records marking **living patients as deceased**. Some patients were relabelled with a stranger's name.
- MediMap said the data involved included **names, dates of birth, prescribers, location of care and resident status**.
- It notified the **Privacy Commissioner** and **NZ Police**, brought in external cyber experts, and was being helped by the **Ministry of Health**. Health NZ activated its Cyber Incident Management Team.

[Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/national/medimap-hack-investigation-after-patients-wrongly-marked-dead-names-changed) also reported patients being wrongly marked dead and names being changed.

## Who uses MediMap

MediMap records medication doses, handles e-prescribing, holds medication histories and connects care providers with pharmacies. RNZ reported that:

- the **Aged Care Association** said as many as **60%** of aged care facilities could be using it;
- **Hospice NZ** said a number of hospices were affected and had switched to **paper systems**;
- the **Nurses Organisation** said Christchurch aged care sites were affected.

Hospice NZ chief executive Wayne Naylor said: "As far as we are aware, patient care has continued safely and has not been impacted greatly by the outage."

## Why tampering is scarier than theft

A stolen record is a privacy problem. A **changed** medication record is a **safety** problem. Staff rely on these systems to give the right dose to the right person. Marking someone as dead in a care system is, to put it mildly, not a clerical inconvenience.

## Who's responsible?

Health NZ was clear about where the responsibility sits. Its spokesperson Darren Douglass said: "As a private company, MediMap is solely responsible for ensuring the security of its platform." MediMap director Geoffrey Sayer apologised for the disruption and concern.

## The political fallout

- **PM Christopher Luxon**: "It's concerning, particularly when it follows Manage My Health." He said he had been pushing for stronger cybersecurity laws.
- **Associate Health Minister David Seymour**: "New Zealand cannot afford to be the weakest link."
- The government's **Cyber Security Strategy 2026–2030** and **Action Plan** ask the Ministry of Justice to advise on **civil penalties** under the Privacy Act, which currently has no civil fines for poor security, and on a new offence for handling illegally obtained data ([MinterEllison](https://minterellison.co.nz/insights/government-releases-new-zealand-cyber-security-action-plan); [[nz-cyber-security-action-plan-stolen-data-offence|our explainer]]).
- The Privacy Commissioner has also reported a **43% rise** in serious breach notifications ([[nz-privacy-breach-notifications-rise-43-percent|more]]).

## Context: a bad run for NZ health data

MediMap followed the **Manage My Health** breach, where hundreds of thousands of documents were stolen and the Privacy Commissioner later issued compliance notices ([[nz-manage-my-health-data-breach-privacy-commissioner-findings|our guide]]).

## If you or a family member uses aged care, hospice or disability services

- Ask the provider whether **records were checked and corrected** after the incident.
- Double-check **medications and personal details** at the next review.
- Be wary of **calls or messages** that mention the incident; verify through the provider directly.
- You can ask for a copy of health records to check they're accurate ([[nz-health-records-access-request|how]]).
