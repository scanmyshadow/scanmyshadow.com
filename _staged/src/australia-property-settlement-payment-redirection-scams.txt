=== TLDR
**Payment redirection scams**, including property settlement scams, cost Australians **$166.8 million in 2025**, up more than 9%. Criminals hack or impersonate **conveyancers, agents or settlement agents** and send **fake bank details** that look genuine. In **August 2026**, a Westpac banker stopped a **$1.2 million** first-home transfer after spotting a hacked settlement agent email. The fix: **call to confirm bank details** on a number you already saved, every time.

=== BODY
## The $1.2 million near miss

Per [Westpac](https://www.westpac.com.au/news/money-matters/2026/08/westpac-bankers-instinct-saves-customer-from-1-million-scam/) (6 August 2026):

- A customer was about to send **$1.2 million**, savings plus loan, for their first home.
- Criminals had **hacked the settlement agent's email** and sent fake instructions.
- Banker **Neet Kukreja** noticed: the **tone** was different; the **urgency** didn't fit; the staff member named was **on leave**; the sender's address had **one extra letter**; and the payment route didn't match normal practice.
- She paused, called the real settlement agent, who confirmed no such request existed.

"Scammers rely on urgency and fear," she said.

## How these scams work

From [CommBank's September 2026 warning](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2026/09/property-settlement-scam-risk-rising.html):

- Fraudsters **intercept emails** with bank details and send **fake payment instructions**.
- They **research buyers and sellers on social media** and use **AI** to write messages and even **clone voices**.
- In one case, a scammer posing as the seller had first **phoned the agency**, mentioning the property address and local landmarks to sound legit, then emailed "new" bank details.
- A conveyancer intercepted another attempt that could have diverted nearly **$200,000**.

A PEXA survey found **99%** of Australians couldn't identify a fraudulent email address even when told it was a scam. So "I'd spot it" isn't a plan.

## Warning signs

- **Changed bank details**, especially last minute
- **Urgency**: "settlement fails today unless you pay now"
- Payment details arriving **without the client knowing**
- Small changes in **tone, signature or email address**

PEXA's Graham Fairley: "Property professionals won't be injecting a sense of urgency."

## The rules

1. **Call to confirm bank details** using a number you **saved yourself** or found independently. Never use numbers in the email.
2. **Agree on communication rules** with your conveyancer at the start ("we'll never change bank details by email").
3. **Question any last-minute change.**
4. **Send a small test amount** and confirm by phone before the big transfer.
5. **Contact your bank immediately** if anything feels off.

## If you've sent money

Call your bank **immediately**, then your conveyancer, and report to ReportCyber and Scamwatch. Speed is everything with recalls.
