=== BODY
## Why “temporary lift” is the right choice

Freezing your credit is one of the best free protections against identity theft (here's [[how-to-freeze-your-credit-us|how to set one up]]). But you'll need to open it for a mortgage, car loan, new card, apartment application or some phone contracts. A **temporary lift** (Experian calls it a thaw) opens your file for a date range, then refreezes it automatically. Removing the freeze entirely means you have to remember to put it back, and most of us won't.

## The rules, briefly

According to [Credit Karma's guide](https://www.creditkarma.com/credit-cards/i/lift-credit-freeze):

- freezing and unfreezing is **free** under federal law;
- requests online or by phone must be honoured **within one hour** (often minutes);
- mail requests have **three business days** from receipt;
- each bureau is separate, so a lift at one doesn't touch the others;
- when a temporary lift ends, the freeze comes back automatically.

## Only thaw what the lender checks

Ask the lender, landlord or carrier which bureau they pull. Many use just one or two. If they don't know, lifting all three for a week is fine, but a one-bureau lift keeps the other two locked. It's like opening one door for a guest instead of taking the house off its hinges.

## Pick sensible dates

- **Credit card or phone contract:** a few days.
- **Car loan:** a week, as dealers sometimes shop several lenders.
- **Mortgage:** talk to your loan officer; underwriting can pull again near closing.

## “I lost my freeze PIN”

If you froze your credit years ago, you may have been given a PIN. The bureaus have mostly moved to online accounts. [Experian says](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/replacing-a-lost-security-freeze-pin-number/) you no longer need a PIN: create or log in to an account in its Security Freeze Center and toggle the freeze there. For Equifax and TransUnion, set up an online account with the same personal details, or call the numbers above and verify your identity. Expect questions about past addresses and loans.

## Don't confuse freezes with locks

Credit **locks** are bureau products, sometimes paid, with app toggles. Freezes are the legal right with guaranteed timelines. Our guide to [[credit-lock-vs-credit-freeze|lock vs freeze]] explains why we prefer the freeze. === FAQ
Q: How long does it take to unfreeze my credit?
A: Within one hour for online or phone requests, often a few minutes. Mail requests can take up to three business days.
Q: Does it cost money to lift a credit freeze?
A: No. Federal law makes freezing and unfreezing free.
Q: Do I need to unfreeze all three bureaus?
A: Only the ones your lender checks. Ask which bureau they use.
Q: What if I lost my credit freeze PIN?
A: Experian no longer requires a PIN; manage the freeze through an online account. For Equifax and TransUnion, create an online account or call to verify your identity.
