=== BODY
## The usual Amazon scam texts

- “Amazon: Your account is suspended due to a billing problem. Update payment: [link]”
- “Did you order an iPhone 17 Pro for $1,199? Reply 1 to cancel.”
- “You are owed a $89.99 refund. Confirm your card to receive it.”
- “Your package couldn't be delivered. Reschedule here.”

The order version is the most effective, because a big purchase you didn't make creates instant panic, and the “helpful” agent on the other end of the phone then asks for remote access or a code. Our [[uk-amazon-scam-call-whatsapp-code-iphone-order|UK version of this scam]] even asks for your WhatsApp code.

## The three things Amazon never does

According to [Amazon's own guidance](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/how-to-avoid-amazon-scams):

1. **It won't ask for payment by phone or email.** Payment happens only in the app, on the website or in its physical stores.
2. **It won't ask you to buy gift cards.** No legitimate Amazon transaction needs gift card payment.
3. **It won't ask you to install software or give remote access** to talk to customer service.

If a message breaks any of those, you can stop reading it.

## How big the problem is

Amazon says that in 2025, **28%** of reported impersonation scams were attempts to get into customer accounts, and it initiated takedowns of **more than 70,000 phishing websites and 14,000 phone numbers**. It says reported phishing sites can now be taken down in a few hours. That's a lot of whack-a-mole.

## How to check and report

- **Check:** open the Amazon app or type amazon.com yourself and look at Your Orders and your account. If it isn't there, it isn't real.
- **Don't call numbers** from texts, emails or search results; contact Amazon through the app or site.
- **Report:** use Amazon's self-service scam report tool (customers), or email **reportascam@amazon.com** (non-customers).
- **Forward texts** to 7726 for your carrier, and report to the [FTC](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
- **Search before you trust:** Amazon partners with the BBB's Scam Tracker, where you can search a phone number, URL or email.

## If you gave card details or remote access

Call your card issuer, uninstall any remote-access app, run a security scan, and change your Amazon and email passwords. See [[tech-support-scam-remote-access-recovery|remote-access scam recovery]] and [[how-to-cancel-a-compromised-debit-card|cancelling a compromised card]].

## Why Amazon is the favourite disguise

Almost everyone in the US has an Amazon account, so a fake Amazon text has a near-100% chance of matching someone's real life. Scammers get numbers from breaches and brokers; check your exposure with a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]].
