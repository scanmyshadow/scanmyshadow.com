=== TLDR
If having your address on the **public electoral roll** could put **you or your family's safety at risk**, you can apply to the AEC to become a **silent elector**. You'll need a **Commonwealth Statutory Declaration** explaining the risk, and one form of ID. Once approved, your address won't appear on **future** public rolls (masking can take up to 7 days). Wanting fewer junk mail or a silent phone number isn't enough on its own.

=== BODY
## Why it matters

The public electoral roll can be inspected, and it lists names and addresses. For most people that's fine. For anyone escaping violence, stalking or harassment, it's a real risk.

## Who can apply

According to the [AEC](https://www.aec.gov.au/Enrolling_to_vote/Special_Category/silent-electors.htm):

- Australian citizens **18 or over** (16- and 17-year-olds can enrol and register now, ready for 18);
- who have lived at their address for at least **one month**;
- who believe having their address on the public roll **could put them or their family's safety at risk**.

Not accepted as grounds on their own: a silent phone number, or not wanting junk mail.

## What you need

1. **The silent elector form**: online, or the paper version for your state or territory.
2. **A Commonwealth Statutory Declaration**, witnessed by an approved person, explaining **in detail** the risk to you or your family. State or territory declarations aren't accepted (the Attorney-General's Department lists alternatives, including myGov and Digital ID options).
3. **One form of ID**: Australian driver licence, passport, Medicare card, citizenship number, or confirmation from a currently enrolled person. Proof of age cards don't count.

**Tip:** family members at the same address with the same surname should each apply separately.

## What it hides (and doesn't)

- Your address **won't be shown on future editions** of the public roll.
- At a **new address**, only your name and division appear.
- At your **current address**, it stays on rolls **already** published. That's why applying before or as you move is best.
- Masking can take up to **7 days** after approval.
- You **don't need to reapply** after moving or changing your name. Just update your enrolment.

## State and local rolls

Paper forms exist for each state and territory. In **Victoria**, silent status on the state roll also suppresses your address on the local government roll. Non-resident ratepayers may need to apply separately to their council.

## How the application works in practice

Service NSW's [referral page](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/apply-register-silent-elector) shows how it plays out for NSW residents (the process runs through the AEC for everyone):

- You must **already be enrolled** at your **current address**.
- Your application must **explain in detail** what the risk is and why your or your family's safety is at risk.
- You'll need the AEC form, a **statutory declaration** and one ID: an **Australian driver licence or passport**.
- A **Divisional Returning Officer** decides and tells you **in writing**. The information in your application stays **strictly confidential**.
- Once approved, only your **name and electoral division** appear on future rolls.

One easily missed tip: **family members with the same surname** enrolled at your address should consider applying too. Otherwise their entry can link your surname to the address anyway.

=== FAQ
Q: Should my family also apply to be silent electors?
A: Service NSW suggests family members with the same surname enrolled at your address consider applying, so the address can't be linked to your surname through them.
Q: Who can become a silent elector in Australia?
A: Australian citizens 18 or over who believe having their address on the public roll could put them or their family's safety at risk.
Q: What documents do I need to become a silent elector?
A: The silent elector form, a Commonwealth Statutory Declaration explaining the risk, and one form of ID such as a driver licence or passport.
Q: Does silent enrolment remove my address from old rolls?
A: No. It hides your address on future public rolls. Rolls already published at your current address still show it.
Q: Do I need to reapply after moving?
A: No. Update your enrolment and you remain a silent elector.
