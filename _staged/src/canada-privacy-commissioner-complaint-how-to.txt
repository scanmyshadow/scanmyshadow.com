=== TLDR
If a business mishandled your personal information, you can file a free complaint with the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) under PIPEDA, without a lawyer. First try to resolve it with the company's privacy officer. PIPEDA covers personal information handled in commercial activity; Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec have their own laws that may apply instead. File online or by mail; the OPC's line is 1-800-282-1376.

=== BODY
## Does PIPEDA cover your issue?

According to the [OPC](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/file-a-formal-privacy-complaint/file-a-complaint-about-a-business):

- PIPEDA covers many private-sector organisations when they handle personal information **during commercial activity**.
- Not-for-profits and charities are generally excluded unless doing commercial work (fundraising doesn't count).
- **Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec** have their own similar laws, which may apply if the information is handled entirely within that province. Quebec's rules have been strengthened in recent years ([[canada-pipeda-explained|our PIPEDA explainer]] covers the basics).

## You won't be the only one

The OPC's 2025-26 annual report, [tabled on 4 June 2026](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/nr-c_260604/), recorded **3,044 PIPEDA complaints** against businesses, up **109%** on the year before. Businesses reported **almost 700 breaches** affecting **more than 20 million Canadians**. The OPC said the jump in complaints was likely linked to greater awareness of the process and easier access to it. It also closed **302 PIPEDA complaints through early resolution**, a faster, informal route.

## Step 1: talk to the company

The OPC encourages you to try to resolve it directly first, starting with the organisation's **Privacy Officer**. Put your concern in writing: what happened, when, and what you want (access, correction, deletion, an explanation). Keep copies.

## Step 2: file with the OPC

- **It's free**, and you don't need a lawyer.
- File **online** (preferred) or by downloading the form and mailing it.
- Filing for someone else? You need their written approval.
- Your name and complaint details will generally be shared with the business.
- Complaint files are kept for 10 years after completion.
- Questions: **1-800-282-1376**.

An incomplete form can stop your complaint being processed, so include dates, names and copies of correspondence.

## What happens next

Some complaints are closed through **early resolution**, an informal route where the issue is settled without a full investigation; the OPC closed 302 PIPEDA complaints that way in 2025-26. Others are investigated. Either way, your written record of what you asked the company and when is what moves things along, so keep every email.

## Complaints about the government are different

If your complaint is about a **federal institution** (such as the CRA or IRCC), it falls under the **Privacy Act**, not PIPEDA. The OPC handled **3,146** Privacy Act complaints in 2025-26, and federal institutions reported about **450 breaches** affecting **more than 48,000** people.

## Common reasons to complain

- a company won't give you access to your personal information;
- a breach exposed your data and the company didn't notify you properly;
- your information was used or shared without consent;
- a company keeps marketing to you after you withdrew consent.

## After a breach

If you were caught in a breach like [[canada-loblaw-data-breach-two-million-customers|Loblaw]] or [[canada-crime-stoppers-tips-leak-1-5-million|Crime Stoppers]], you can ask the organisation what was exposed and how it's protecting you, then complain to the OPC if you're not satisfied. === FAQ
Q: How many privacy complaints does the OPC get?
A: The OPC's 2025-26 annual report recorded 3,044 PIPEDA complaints against businesses, up 109%, and almost 700 business breach reports affecting more than 20 million Canadians.
Q: Can I complain about a government department?
A: Yes, but complaints about federal institutions fall under the Privacy Act, not PIPEDA. The same OPC handles both.
Q: How do I file a privacy complaint in Canada?
A: Try to resolve it with the company's privacy officer first, then file online or by mail with the Office of the Privacy Commissioner.
Q: Does it cost anything to complain to the OPC?
A: No. Filing is free and you don't need a lawyer.
Q: Does PIPEDA apply in Quebec, BC and Alberta?
A: Those provinces have their own similar laws, which may apply when information is handled entirely within the province.
Q: What is the OPC's phone number?
A: 1-800-282-1376 (toll-free).
