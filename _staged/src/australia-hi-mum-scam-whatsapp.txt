=== TLDR
"**Hi Mum, I dropped my phone, this is my new number.**" After some friendly chat, "your kid" needs money for a bill, a replacement phone or a contractor, and can't use their banking app. When the scam spiked in 2022, the ACCC said **more than 1,150 Australians** lost **$2.6 million** in seven months. **Women over 55** made over two-thirds of reports, and **over-55s** bore **95% of losses**. Beat it in seconds: **call the old number**.

=== BODY
## How it goes

From the [ACCC's warning](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-warning-of-suspicious-messages-as-hi-mum-scams-spike):

1. A WhatsApp message from an **unknown number**: "Hi Mum" or "It's me."
2. They say their phone was **lost or broken**, so this is a **new number**.
3. A bit of chat to build rapport.
4. Then the ask: money for an **urgent bill**, a **contractor**, or a **new phone**.
5. The excuse: they **can't access their online banking** right now.

Some ask you to pay a "friend's" account, because the child's own account is "frozen". That detail alone should stop you: a real kid's bank problem is never solved by paying a stranger.

## A real Australian case

SBS [reported](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/dateline/article/the-scam-targeting-australian-women-by-preying-on-a-mothers-love/40lydym5r) on a Perth mother whose "daughter", supposedly overseas, messaged from a new number after "water damage" to her phone. The scammer knew details that fitted the daughter's life and said her bank account was **frozen for 48 hours**. Over three transfers she sent **just under $10,000** before a family member noticed something was off. After SBS contacted her bank, she was told the money would be refunded.

Notice how ordinary each step was. No threats, no prize. Just a child who needed help.

## Who it hits

| ACCC figure (Jan–Jul 2022) | Number |
|---|---|
| Victims | 1,150+ |
| Losses | $2.6 million |
| Reports from women over 55 | Over two-thirds (losses over $1.4m) |
| Share of reports from over-55s | 82% |
| Share of losses from over-55s | 95% |

Most reports landed in **June and July 2022**, which is when the scam took off in Australia. It hasn't gone away; the same script turns up as "Hi Dad", "Hi Nan" and messages from a "friend".

The scam works because it's built on love, not greed. You're not chasing a prize. You're helping your kid. That's a much harder instinct to override. As the ACCC's then-Deputy Chair put it: "It's designed to pull your heartstrings."

## The 30-second check

- **Call the number already saved** in your phone. If the old number still works, the "new number" story is false.
- No answer? Try **another way**: a partner, sibling, flatmate, work.
- Ask a **personal question** only they'd know (not "what's your birthday", which is in data breaches; try "what did we eat last Christmas?").
- The ACCC's rule: **never send money unless you're absolutely sure** who it's going to.

## A family code word

Agree on a silly word or phrase with your family. If "you" text asking for money, the real you will know it. The scammer won't. It costs nothing and it's oddly fun.

## If you've already paid

1. **Call your bank immediately** and ask them to stop or recall the payment. Speed matters.
2. Report to **Scamwatch**, even if you didn't lose money.
3. If you shared personal info or photos, contact **IDCARE** on 1800 595 160.
4. Block and report the number in WhatsApp.
5. Don't feel stupid. These scams are built by professionals to beat good people.

=== FAQ
Q: What is the Hi Mum scam?
A: A scammer messages pretending to be your child on a new number, then asks for money for an urgent bill or new phone, often to a "friend's" account.
Q: How much have Australians lost to Hi Mum scams?
A: The ACCC reported more than 1,150 victims lost $2.6 million in the first seven months of 2022.
Q: How do I check if a Hi Mum message is real?
A: Call your child on the number already saved in your phone, or contact them another way. If the old number works, it's a scam.
Q: What should I do if I paid a Hi Mum scammer?
A: Call your bank immediately, report it to Scamwatch and contact IDCARE if you shared personal information.
