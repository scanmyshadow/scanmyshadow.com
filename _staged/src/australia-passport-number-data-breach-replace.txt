=== TLDR
If your **passport number** leaked in a breach, the **Australian Passport Office** says your passport is **still valid for travel**: someone would need the **physical passport** to travel on it. Compromised passports can be **blocked in the Document Verification Service (DVS)** so they can't be used for **online ID checks**, and a passport number **can't be used to get a new passport**. You usually **don't need to replace it**. But if you're asked to use it online and it fails, that's why.

=== BODY
## What the APO says

This guidance from the [Australian Passport Office](https://passports.gov.au/node/305) was written after the **Optus** breach, but the principles apply to any breach exposing passport numbers:

- **Travel:** "Your passport is still safe to use for international travel." A number alone can't be used to travel.
- **Online ID checks:** compromised passports are **blocked in the DVS**, so they can't be used to verify identity online.
- **In person:** you can still show your physical passport.
- **New passports:** a passport number **can't be used to get a new passport**, and **facial recognition** guards against identity takeover.
- **Replacement:** usually **not required**. If you're **travelling soon**, don't apply now, as processing could take longer than your trip.

## When replacing might make sense

- You **lost the physical passport** as well (then you must report and cancel it).
- You need to use your passport for **online ID checks** and it's now blocked.
- The breaching company **offers to pay** for a replacement (Optus did).

## Cancelling a lost or stolen passport

Call **131 232**, or if you're overseas, contact the nearest Australian embassy or consulate.

## The real risk from a leaked passport number

The passport itself is fairly well protected. The danger is that the number is often leaked **alongside** your name, birthday and address, which helps scammers:

- pass weaker ID checks at smaller lenders;
- sound convincing on the phone;
- run "your passport is compromised, pay to replace it" scams.

The APO's advice: don't click links or share details with **unsolicited** contacts.

## What to do

1. Keep the breach notice.
2. Consider a [[australia-credit-ban-how-to-request|credit ban]] if your birthday and address also leaked.

4. Get help from IDCARE (1800 595 160) if you see misuse.
