=== TLDR
Hamilton police warned on October 20, 2026 about a three-stage scam targeting seniors: a caller from your “phone or internet provider” offers a discount, then a “bank” caller says your account is compromised, then a courier comes to collect your bank cards while they ask for your PIN. Banks never send couriers for cards or ask for PINs. Hang up and call your bank on a number you've checked yourself.

=== BODY
## The three stages

From [CHCH News](https://www.chch.com/chch-news/hamilton-police-warns-public-of-new-scam-targeting-seniors/), after several reports to Hamilton police in a week:

1. **The discount call.** Someone claiming to be your phone or internet provider offers a discount. This warms you up and often collects personal details.
2. **The bank call.** A second caller, claiming to be from your bank, says your account has been compromised.
3. **The courier.** They arrange for a courier to pick up your bank cards, and ask for your PIN so the card can be “cancelled” or “investigated.”

The first call is the clever bit. It's friendly and pleasant, so by the time the “bank” rings, you're already in a cooperative mood, and the scammer may know your provider, your address and your name.

## What police say

- Keep PINs, passwords and banking details private; never share them by phone.
- Don't trust a caller just because they know your personal information. It can come from breaches, stolen mail or other sources.
- **Banks don't send couriers** to collect cards or PINs.
- If unsure, contact your bank or provider on a number you've verified yourself, or visit a branch.
- Report suspicious calls to police.

## A pattern across Canada

The same card pick-up scam has been reported in [[canada-maple-ridge-rcmp-card-pick-up-fraud-bank-impersonation|Maple Ridge]] and elsewhere, and the UK calls it [[uk-courier-fraud-fake-bank-investigators-explained|courier fraud]]. Variations swap cards for gold ([[canada-gold-bar-safekeeping-scam-fake-rcmp|the gold scam]]) or cash.

## Help an older relative

Agree one rule: nobody from a bank ever comes to the door for cards, and nobody needs your PIN. If a courier is coming, call family first. See [[protect-elderly-parents-from-phone-scams|protecting elderly parents]].

If cards were handed over, call the bank immediately to cancel them, then report to police and the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/). A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] can show what's leaked about a parent's email.
