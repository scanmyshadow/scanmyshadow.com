=== TLDR
Waterloo Regional Police arrested three men in July over linked home renovation scams that took more than $500,000 from seniors across the country, Money.ca reported in August 2026. They used high-pressure fear tactics, offered work they never meant to finish, and one posed as a police officer. Red flags: urgent “damage,” payment before work, no written contract, and pressure to decide on the spot.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [Money.ca](https://money.ca/news/ontario-seniors-home-renovation-fraud-scam), Waterloo Regional Police Services arrested three men on July 8 in connection with multiple linked home renovation scams. Seniors across Canada lost **more than $500,000**.

Police said the men used “high-pressure, urgent fear tactics” and offered services they had no intention of completing. One suspect **posed as a police officer**, exploiting the trust people place in officers. Police said such schemes are often tied to organised networks that cross jurisdictions, with some participants travelling from outside the country.

## The usual pattern

Investigators describe it like this:

1. A knock at the door: “We noticed your roof/chimney/driveway is damaged.”
2. Fear: it's dangerous, it'll get worse, it'll cost a fortune if you wait.
3. Payment before work starts.
4. They vanish, or do a token job and ask for more.

Some use fake business names or move between provinces, which makes them hard to trace.

## Red flags

- unsolicited door-to-door offers;
- urgency and fear (“it could collapse”);
- demands for full or large payment upfront;
- no written contract, no business address, cash only;
- a “police officer” vouching for a contractor (that's not a thing).

## Protect yourself and older relatives

- Get at least three quotes from contractors you contacted yourself.
- Check reviews and business registration.
- Never pay the full amount upfront.
- Don't sign on the spot; a real contractor can wait a day.
- Keep in regular touch with older relatives and talk about doorstep scams.
