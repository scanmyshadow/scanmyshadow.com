=== TLDR
Cybertip.ca, Canada's tipline for online child sexual exploitation, processed 29,505 reports in 2024, up nearly 8%, and says financial sextortion reports rose significantly, with males more often targeted for money. If your teen is being blackmailed: don't pay, stop replying, keep screenshots, report to Cybertip.ca and police, and reassure them it isn't their fault.

=== BODY
## What the data shows

From [Cybertip.ca's 2024 report summary](https://cybertip.ca/en/about/reports-from-public-processed-2024/):

| Measure (2024) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Reports processed | 29,505 (up nearly 8%) |
| Online luring reports | 2,717 |
| Non-consensual distribution of intimate images | 271 |
| Victims' gender | 36.2% female, 34.3% male, 29.3% unknown |

Cybertip.ca says **financial sextortion reports increased significantly**, and **males are more often targeted for money**.

## How it usually unfolds

- A friendly account, often claiming to be a teen girl, starts chatting.
- It moves quickly to exchanging images, or the scammer creates fakes.
- The threat arrives: pay, or images go to friends, family and school.

The UK's National Crime Agency has seen cases go from first contact to blackmail in under an hour ([[uk-sextortion-teenage-boys-nca-advice-parents|the UK picture]]). The scammers run it like a business, which means the script is predictable.

## What to tell your teen

- **It's not your fault.** Shame is the scammer's main tool.
- **Don't pay.** Paying rarely ends it.
- **Stop replying, but keep evidence**: screenshots of messages, usernames and payment requests.
- **Tell an adult now.**

## Where to report in Canada

- **Cybertip.ca**, Canada's national tipline for online sexual exploitation of children.
- **NeedHelpNow.ca**, from the same organisation, helps teens get images removed and deal with the situation.
- **Local police**.
- The platform where it happened.

## Prevention that actually helps

Calm, early conversations beat parental-control apps. Make accounts private, hide friends lists, and agree that a weird request from a stranger is something to share, not hide. See [[protect-your-teen-from-online-scams|protecting teens online]] and [[how-to-make-instagram-private|making Instagram private]]. Avoid paid “sextortion removal” services that promise miracles; the US FTC sued one ([[us-ftc-sextortion-help-service-digital-forensics-lawsuit|the case]]).
