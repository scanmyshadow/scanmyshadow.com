=== TLDR
Between **2021 and 2022**, Telstra disclosed details of **more than 140,000** customers who'd asked for **unlisted or silent numbers**. More than **24,000** silent numbers appeared **with names and addresses in the White Pages**, and **139,402** unlisted numbers went into the directory assistance database operators use. ACMA found **163,000+** breaches of Telstra's licence conditions and ordered audits, training and twice-yearly data checks. For people who chose silent numbers for safety, this was serious.

=== BODY
## What happened

From [The Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/8690789/telstra-put-silent-numbers-addresses-in-phone-book/) (July 2024):

- Telstra disclosed details of customers who had requested **silent or unlisted** numbers.
- **24,000+** silent numbers were **published with names and addresses** in the **White Pages**.
- **139,402** unlisted numbers were published in the **directory assistance database**.
- Telstra blamed **systems issues and process failures** and reported itself to the regulator.

## What ACMA did

- Found Telstra **breached its carrier licence conditions on more than 163,000 occasions**.
- Said the breach put **privacy and safety at risk**, though it wasn't aware of resulting harm.
- Required Telstra to **reconcile customer data** between the White Pages and its own database **every six months**, run **staff training**, and get an **independent audit**.
- No fine was reported. If Telstra doesn't comply, ACMA could go to court, where penalties could run into millions.

## Why it matters

People ask for silent numbers for real reasons: escaping family violence, stalking, harassment, sensitive jobs. A silent number showing up in the phone book is the opposite of what they paid attention to set up.

It's like putting up a "do not disturb" sign and having it printed in the local paper with your address.

## What to do if you rely on a silent number

1. **Check the White Pages** yourself for your name and address.
2. **Confirm your listing status** in My Telstra ([[australia-white-pages-remove-number-unlisted|how]]).
3. If your safety depends on it, also become a [[australia-silent-elector-how-to-hide-address|silent elector]].
4. If you were affected, you can complain to Telstra and then the telco ombudsman or OAIC ([[australia-oaic-complaint-how-to|how]]).
5. Serious invasions of privacy may now also be actionable under the [[australia-privacy-statutory-tort-explained|privacy tort]].
