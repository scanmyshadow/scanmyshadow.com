=== TLDR
From late 2023 into January 2024, attackers took over about **15,000 Australian online shopping accounts** at brands including **The Iconic, Dan Murphy's, Guzman y Gomez and Event Cinemas**. No company was hacked in the usual sense: attackers **reused usernames and passwords from older breaches** and spent **saved cards and store credit**. This is **credential stuffing**. In **March 2025** the same method hit Australia's biggest **super funds**: more than **20,000** accounts were accessed and four AustralianSuper members lost a combined **$500,000**. The fix is boring and effective: **unique passwords plus MFA**.

=== BODY
## What happened

[Information Age (ACS)](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2024/coordinated-cyberattack-hits-thousands-of-aussies.html) reported in January 2024:

- Security firm **Kasada** counted about **15,000** Australian accounts affected since late November, rising daily.
- **The Iconic** spotted it first when customers reported unexpected card transactions; it promised refunds.
- **Dan Murphy's** owner Endeavour said some accounts were accessed using logins from **unrelated third-party breaches**.
- **Guzman y Gomez** and **Event Cinemas** were also reported as affected.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called scams a "scourge." Cyber Security Minister Clare O'Neil urged "strong and unique passphrases" and MFA.

## How credential stuffing works

1. A site you used years ago gets breached. Your email and password leak.
2. Attackers load millions of leaked logins into automated tools.
3. The tools try each one on **other** sites.
4. Wherever you **reused that password**, they get in.
5. Inside, they use **saved cards, store credit or points**, and grab personal info.

The company wasn't "hacked" so much as you were, years ago, somewhere else. Annoying, but also fixable.

## Then it hit retirement savings

Over the weekend of **29-30 March 2025**, attackers used stolen passwords against several of Australia's largest super funds, [RNZ reported](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/top/557223/hackers-strike-australia-s-largest-pension-funds-in-coordinated-attacks):

| Fund | What happened |
|---|---|
| **AustralianSuper** | Up to **600** member passwords stolen; four members lost a combined **$500,000**, according to an unnamed source |
| **Rest** | About **20,000** accounts, roughly 1% of members, affected by unauthorised activity |
| **Australian Retirement Trust** | Several hundred accounts locked as a precaution |
| **Insignia (MLC)** | 100 Expand Wrap accounts accessed; no financial impact reported |
| **Hostplus** | Targeted; no losses reported |

AustralianSuper said **stolen passwords** were used to get in. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese described such attacks as a "regular issue." The difference from the shopping wave: instead of designer clothes and alcohol, the prize was a retirement balance. ## Why your old passwords are still worth money

According to [Telstra](https://www.telstra.com.au/exchange/what-is-credential-stuffing), batches of stolen usernames and passwords can contain thousands of logins and sell on the dark web **for a few dollars**. Once inside a shopping account, attackers use saved payment details to buy goods such as **designer clothing, high-end alcohol, gift cards and experiences**, shipped to themselves. As Telstra puts it, "a breach of your password on one platform can potentially expose you to a breach on many others."

## How to protect yourself

1. **Unique password for every account.** A [[password-manager-mfa-security-key-guide|password manager]] makes it painless.
2. **Turn on MFA** wherever offered, especially email, banking, super and shopping apps with saved cards.
3. **Remove saved cards** from sites you rarely use. Telstra says saving card details on shopping sites is "akin to" the risk of reusing passwords.
4. **Use long passphrases** for anything you have to remember. Telstra's example, "CookSteakToTempOf48C," would take centuries to brute-force.
5. **Check breaches**: our free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure checker]] shows which breaches your email appears in, so you know which passwords to change first.
6. **Watch for login alerts** and act on them.

## If your account was taken over

- Reset the password (and anywhere else you used it).
- Remove saved cards; call your bank about any charges.
- Check the account's email and phone haven't been changed.
- Report to the retailer and ReportCyber.

=== FAQ
Q: What happened to Dan Murphy's and The Iconic accounts?
A: In a wave of credential stuffing reported in January 2024, attackers used passwords from other breaches to log in and use saved payment details.
Q: How many Australian accounts were affected?
A: About 15,000 since late November 2023, according to security firm Kasada.
Q: What is credential stuffing?
A: Using usernames and passwords stolen in one breach to log into other sites where people reused them.
Q: Were Australian super funds hit by credential stuffing?
A: Yes. In March 2025, stolen passwords were used against AustralianSuper, Rest and other funds. More than 20,000 accounts were affected and four AustralianSuper members lost a combined $500,000.
Q: How do I stop credential stuffing?
A: Use a unique password for each account and turn on multi-factor authentication.
