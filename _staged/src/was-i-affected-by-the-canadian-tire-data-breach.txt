=== TLDR
Canadian Tire discovered unauthorized access to an e-commerce database on **October 2, 2025**. Have I Been Pwned later loaded about **42 million records, including 38.3 million email addresses**. Accounts with Canadian Tire, SportChek, Mark's/L'Équipeur and Party City were involved. Exposed data includes names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, hashed passwords and, for a small subset, dates of birth and partial card details. If you shopped online with any of these brands, assume you're in it: change the password, watch for targeted phishing and ignore "Triangle points" texts.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/canadian-tire-data-breach-impacts-38-million-accounts/amp/), Canadian Tire found that someone had accessed an **e-commerce database** on October 2, 2025. The company notified affected customers by email. It hasn't publicly confirmed a total number of victims, but when the data reached Have I Been Pwned in late February 2026, it contained about **42 million records and 38.3 million unique email addresses**.

That's roughly one record for every person in Canada. Not every one is a separate human, of course. Some of us have a SportChek account from 2014 we've completely forgotten about, which is precisely the problem.

## Which brands were included

The breach covered online accounts with:

- **Canadian Tire**
- **SportChek**
- **Mark's / L'Équipeur**
- **Party City** (Canadian stores)

## What data was exposed

| Data | Status |
|---|---|
| Names | Exposed |
| Email addresses | Exposed |
| Phone numbers and physical addresses | Exposed (per HIBP) |
| Gender | Exposed (per HIBP) |
| Passwords | Exposed as PBKDF2 hashes |
| Date of birth | Fewer than 150,000 accounts |
| Partial card data (type, expiry, masked number) | A subset of records |
| Canadian Tire Bank data | Not involved, per the company |
| Triangle Rewards loyalty data | Not involved, per the company |

Canadian Tire says the password and card information can't be used to get into accounts or make purchases. PBKDF2 is a slow, deliberate hashing method, which is good news. "Hard to crack" still isn't "impossible to crack" for a weak password, though.

## How to check if you're in it

1. Search your inbox for an email from Canadian Tire about the incident.
2. Enter your email on [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com/).
3. Run our free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] to see this breach alongside any others your address appears in.

## What to do now

- **Change your password** on any of the four brands' sites, and anywhere else you reused it.
- **Turn on two-factor authentication** wherever you can. Our [[how-to-set-up-two-factor-authentication|2FA setup guide]] walks through it.
- **Expect tailored phishing.** Scammers now know your name, address and that you shop at Canadian Tire. Fake "order problem", "Triangle points expiring" or "refund" messages will look very plausible.
- **Check card statements** if you saved a card. The data was partial, but it's still worth a look.
- **If your date of birth was included**, consider a [[canada-equifax-transunion-fraud-alert-guide|fraud alert with Equifax and TransUnion]].

## The phishing to expect

With name, email, phone and address in one file, the likely follow-ups are:

- texts about a "failed delivery" of a Canadian Tire order;
- emails offering "compensation" for the breach via a link;
- calls from someone "in Canadian Tire security" who needs to "verify" your card.

Real breach notices don't ask you to log in through a link or read out card numbers. When in doubt, go to the company's site yourself.

## Part of a bigger pattern

This was one of several large Canadian retail breaches in 2025. See also the [[canada-loblaw-data-breach-two-million-customers|Loblaw breach]] and the [[was-i-affected-by-the-canada-goose-data-breach|Canada Goose breach]].

A: The company hasn't confirmed a total. Have I Been Pwned lists about 42 million records with 38.3 million unique email addresses.
