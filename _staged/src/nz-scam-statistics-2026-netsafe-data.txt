=== TLDR
New Zealanders lost an estimated NZ$3 billion to scams in 2025, up 30% from $2.3 billion in 2024, according to Netsafe and the Global Anti-Scam Alliance. 72% of Kiwis experienced a scam in a year and 23% lost money. Only 10% of victims got money back before it reached the scammer. Netsafe's April–June 2026 quarter logged 3,463 scam and fraud reports and $1.56 million in reported losses, with fake shops at the top.

=== BODY
## The big number: $3 billion

Netsafe's [State of Scams in New Zealand report](https://resource.netsafe.org.nz/New-Zealand’s-Scam-Epidemic.-Kiwis-Lose-Record-$3-Billion-as-Fraud-Hits-New-Highs_For-Release.pdf), produced with the Global Anti-Scam Alliance (GASA), estimates:

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Total lost to scams, 2025 | NZ$3 billion |
| Total lost, 2024 | NZ$2.3 billion |
| Change | +30% |
| Average loss per victim | $3,352 |
| Adults who lost money | 23% |
| Experienced a scam in 12 months | 72% |
| Average scam encounters per person per year | 152 |

That last figure means the average Kiwi meets a scam roughly three times a week. Most of us just don't notice, which is the good news, and occasionally do notice too late, which is the bad news.

## Where scams find people

The top channels were **Gmail (48%)** and **Facebook (47%)**. The most common scam types by share of victims were **shopping (50%)**, **unexpected money (47%)** and **investment (40%)**.

## Getting money back is rare

- 78% of victims who lost money reported it to their bank.
- 36% of those got at least some money back.
- Only **10%** recovered funds before they reached the scammer.

Speed is everything. The minutes after you realise are worth more than the weeks after. See our [[already-got-scammed-international-recovery-guide|international recovery guide]].

## Netsafe's April–June 2026 quarter

Netsafe's [Q4 FY26 summary](https://resource.netsafe.org.nz/Netsafe-Q4-April---Jun-FY26R.pdf) shows what people reported directly:

| Measure | April–June 2026 |
|---|---|
| Total reports | 6,606 |
| Scam and fraud reports | 3,463 |
| Personal harm reports | 2,032 |
| Scam reports with a loss | 324 |
| Reported losses | about $1,558,504 |

The top report categories overall were **worthless or non-existent products** (fake shops), harassment, **friends or relatives impostor** scams, sextortion and false allegations.

Reporters by age: 22–40 made up 42%, 41–64 made 24%, 13–17 made 17% and 65+ only 4%. Low numbers from older Kiwis likely reflect under-reporting rather than safety. Auckland accounted for 41% of reports.

## How people feel about it

61% of scam victims reported stress and 34% moderate to significant effects on their mental wellbeing. 48% think banks should always reimburse victims. New Zealand banks have since moved towards reimbursement rules and confirmation of payee, which we'll cover separately.

## What the data suggests you do

1. **Shop on known sites** and search for reviews before buying from new ones; fake shops lead the reports.
2. **Verify “it's me, new number” messages** with a call to the old number; family impostor scams are top three.
3. **Report fast** to your bank, then to [Netsafe](https://netsafe.org.nz/) or the NCSC.
4. **Check your exposure**: many scams start with leaked contact details. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows which breaches include your email.
