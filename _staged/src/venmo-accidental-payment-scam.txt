=== TLDR
A stranger sends you money on Venmo (or Cash App), then messages: “So sorry, wrong person, can you send it back?” The money often came from a stolen card. If you send it back from your balance, the original charge gets reversed later and you lose the amount you returned. Don't send it back yourself. Ask the sender to cancel it, or contact the app's support.

=== BODY
## How it works

[Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/venmo-scam/), citing the Dayton Daily News, describes the play:

1. A scammer links a **stolen credit card** to Venmo.
2. They send you, say, $300 “by accident.”
3. They message, very politely, asking you to send it back.
4. You send $300 to them from your balance or bank.
5. The stolen-card owner disputes the charge, the original $300 is pulled back from you, and the scammer keeps the $300 you sent, now cleanly theirs.

Some versions skip the money entirely and send a fake screenshot of a payment.

## Why it works

Because you're honest. Returning money that isn't yours feels like the decent thing to do, and the scammer is counting on it. Fraud that exploits kindness is particularly irritating.

## What to do instead

- **Don't send money back yourself.** Ask the sender to cancel or reverse the payment through the app. A real person who made a mistake can contact support. A scammer will push you to “just send it back quickly.”
- **Contact Venmo or Cash App support** through the app and explain.
- **Don't accept payments from strangers** at all if you can avoid it. Venmo says it's designed for payments between people who trust each other.
- **Link a credit card rather than a debit card or bank account** where possible; the BBB notes charges are easier to dispute.
