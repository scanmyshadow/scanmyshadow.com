=== TLDR
Scam texts from spoofed numbers claim a standing order or direct debit was set up on your Nationwide account (Which? flagged versions in August 2026) and give a number to call if you didn't set it up. Nationwide says genuine texts about suspicious transactions include the last four digits of your card, and it will never ask you to move money to a safe account or give your PIN or card reader codes by phone. Hang up and call 159.

=== BODY
## The texts

Which? logged two in its [August 2026 scam alerts](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC):

- a **spoofed text** saying a standing order has been set up, with a number to call if it wasn't you;
- an email saying a monthly **direct debit to a dental practice** was set up, with a link to click if you don't recognise it.

The dental detail is a nice touch from a scammer's point of view: specific, mundane and slightly alarming.

## What Nationwide will never do

From [Nationwide's fraud page](https://nationwide.co.uk/help/fraud-and-security/reporting-fraud-or-a-scam), Nationwide will never ask you to:

- give your PIN or a one-off card reader code over the phone;
- log in to Internet Banking through a link in an email, text or social media message;
- transfer money into a safe account;
- use, re-enable or re-sync your card reader over the phone.

And a useful tell: if Nationwide texts you about a suspicious transaction, **the message includes the last four digits of your card**.

## How to check a call

Hang up and dial **159** (24/7), or stay on the line and use **Call Checker** in the Nationwide app. Fraud helpline: **0800 055 66 22**.

## Report it

- Forward scam emails claiming to be Nationwide to **phishing@nationwide.co.uk**.
- Forward scam texts to **7726**.
- Report losses to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/) (England, Wales, NI) or Police Scotland on 101.

## If you called and shared details

Call 0800 055 66 22 or 159 straight away. Change your passcode and check payments and payees. See [[uk-159-scam-hotline-how-it-works|how 159 works]] and [[uk-action-fraud-crm-reimbursement-code|reimbursement for scam victims]].

## Can you get money back?

If a fake caller talks you into transferring money, you're usually covered by the UK's mandatory APP scam reimbursement rules, which took effect in **October 2024** and cover claims up to **£85,000**, with some exceptions. In July 2026 the Payment Systems Regulator reported that the share of money reimbursed had risen from **54% to 65%** since the rules began ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/07/payment-fraud-falls-by-73m-following-psr-reimbursement-scheme/)). If Nationwide says no, complain, then go to the [[uk-financial-ombudsman-scam-complaint-guide|Financial Ombudsman]].

## Why they target building society members

Older, loyal customers with savings are attractive targets, and Nationwide is the UK's biggest building society. Scammers don't need to know you bank there; they text everyone. And it pays: UK Finance's 2026 fraud report, [reported by The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5256106), found APP fraud losses rose **19% to over £576 million** in 2025, with **impersonation** cases up by double digits. **17%** of fraud incidents started through telecoms: calls and texts like this one.
