=== BODY
## The call

[Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) reported on August 28, 2026 that callers claiming to be from Three say your number will stop working within 24 hours or your SIM will be blocked. They ask for personal and payment details to “keep it active.”

It's a close cousin of O2's [[uk-o2-inactive-sim-scam-text|inactive SIM text]], and the same script turns up with EE and Vodafone branding.

## What they're really after

- **Payment details** for a fake “reactivation fee.”
- **Account access**: your date of birth, account PIN or a one-time code. With those, criminals can order a new handset on your account or move your number to their SIM.
- A SIM swap then lets them receive your bank's security codes. Cifas recorded a **402% rise** in unauthorised SIM swaps in the first half of 2026 ([[uk-cifas-fraudscape-2026-identity-fraud-sim-swap-data|the data]]).

## How to check

- Hang up. Networks don't cut numbers off with a 24-hour phone warning.
- Open your network's app, or call the number on your bill or the network's website.
- Never give a PIN, password or code to an incoming caller.

## Report it

Text “call” followed by the number to **7726** (on Android, text “call” and follow the prompts), as Which? advises. See [[uk-7726-how-to-report-scam-texts-calls|how 7726 works]].

## If you shared details

Call your network immediately and ask them to check for upgrades, SIM changes or porting requests, and add a password or PIN to your account. If your phone loses signal unexpectedly, act fast: [[how-to-block-a-swapped-sim-card|how to block a swapped SIM]]. Tell your bank too, or dial **159**.

Scammers often already know your name and number from leaks. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows what's circulating about your email.
