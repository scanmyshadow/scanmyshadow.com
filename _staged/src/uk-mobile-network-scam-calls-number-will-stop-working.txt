=== TLDR
Which? warned in August 2026 about calls impersonating Three, claiming your number will stop working within 24 hours or your SIM will be blocked, to extract personal and payment details. Similar calls and texts use O2, EE and Vodafone. The goal is often your account access, which can lead to SIM swaps or phones ordered on your contract. Hang up and check in your network's app or by calling the number on your bill. Report by texting “call” and the number to 7726.

=== BODY
## The call

[Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) reported on August 28, 2026 that callers claiming to be from Three say your number will stop working within 24 hours or your SIM will be blocked. They ask for personal and payment details to “keep it active.”

It's a close cousin of O2's [[uk-o2-inactive-sim-scam-text|inactive SIM text]], and the same script turns up with EE and Vodafone branding.

## What they're really after

- **Payment details** for a fake “reactivation fee.”
- **Account access**: your date of birth, account PIN or a one-time code. With those, criminals can order a new handset on your account or move your number to their SIM.
- A SIM swap then lets them receive your bank's security codes. Cifas recorded a **402% rise** in unauthorised SIM swaps in the first half of 2026 ([[uk-cifas-fraudscape-2026-identity-fraud-sim-swap-data|the data]]).

## Why your phone account is worth stealing

Cifas's Fraudscape report, [published in May 2025](https://www.cifas.org.uk/newsroom/huge-surge-see-sim-swaps-hit-telco-and-mobile), found unauthorised **SIM swaps rose 1,055% in 2024**, with nearly **3,000** cases filed to the National Fraud Database. **48%** of account takeover cases involved mobile phone accounts, **unauthorised mobile upgrades rose 96%**, and people aged **61 and over** made up **29%** of account takeover victims.

[Which? reported in April 2026](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/mobile-networks-must-step-up-to-prevent-sim-swap-fraud-aSsLN6p0CMRq) on victims who lost around **£3,000 to £3,500** after SIM swaps, including one Vodafone customer who waited **10 days** to get her number back. Ofcom told Which? networks would consider customers' location before allowing email-based swaps.

## How to check

- Hang up. Networks don't cut numbers off with a 24-hour phone warning.
- Open your network's app, or call the number on your bill or the network's website.
- Never give a PIN, password or code to an incoming caller.

## Report it

Text “call” followed by the number to **7726** (on Android, text “call” and follow the prompts), as Which? advises. See [[uk-7726-how-to-report-scam-texts-calls|how 7726 works]].

## If you shared details

Call your network immediately and ask them to check for upgrades, SIM changes or porting requests, and add a password or PIN to your account. If your own phone has lost signal, contact the network by **webchat or from another device**, as Which? advises, and call your banks to freeze accounts. If your phone loses signal unexpectedly, act fast: [[how-to-block-a-swapped-sim-card|how to block a swapped SIM]]. Tell your bank too, or dial **159**.

## Lock your account before anyone calls

Which?'s advice: use a **strong password and two-factor authentication** on your mobile account, ask your network for an **extra PIN or password** for sensitive changes, protect your **email** (fraudsters have used email access to request swaps), and prefer **passkeys** over SMS codes where you can. Keep your date of birth off public social media profiles.

Scammers often already know your name and number from leaks. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows what's circulating about your email.
