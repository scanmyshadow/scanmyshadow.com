=== TLDR
Toys "R" Us Canada learned on **July 30, 2025** that customer records had been posted online, and later emailed customers. Third-party experts confirmed an unauthorized party copied records that may include **names, addresses, email addresses and phone numbers**. The company says no passwords or credit card details were involved. That's still enough for convincing scam texts and emails, especially around the holidays when parents expect toy deliveries.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to a [Canadian Press report carried by Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35637985/toys-r-us-canada-customers-notified-of-breach-of-personal-information), someone posted information on the deep web claiming it came from Toys "R" Us Canada's databases. The company learned about it on **July 30**, brought in outside cybersecurity experts, and they confirmed an unauthorized party had copied customer records. Customers started getting notification emails in October 2025.

## What data was involved

| Data | Status |
|---|---|
| Names | May be included |
| Mailing addresses | May be included |
| Email addresses | May be included |
| Phone numbers | May be included |
| Passwords | Not involved, per the company |
| Credit card details | Not involved, per the company |

The company didn't say how many customers were affected.

## Why "just contact details" still matters

A file with your name, home address, email and phone number is a scammer's starter kit. Add the fact that you buy from a toy store, and they can guess you have kids. Expect:

- "Your Toys R Us order is delayed, pay a redelivery fee" texts;
- fake holiday giveaways and "loyalty rewards" emails;
- parcel scams that line up suspiciously well with real orders.

None of this needs a password. It just needs you to click once while juggling a toddler.

## What to do

1. **Look for the notification email** from Toys "R" Us Canada and keep it.
2. **Check your email** on [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com/) or our free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure checker]].
3. **Treat delivery texts with suspicion** through the holiday season. Track orders by logging in yourself, not through links.
4. **Forward scam texts to 7726** so your carrier can block them. See our [[canada-7726-spam-text-reporting-guide|7726 and Spam Reporting Centre guide]].
5. **Report losses** to police and the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).

## Your rights

Under PIPEDA, organizations must notify you when a breach creates a real risk of significant harm. If you think a notice was inadequate, you can file a complaint. Our guide to [[canada-privacy-commissioner-complaint-how-to|complaining to the Privacy Commissioner]] explains how.
