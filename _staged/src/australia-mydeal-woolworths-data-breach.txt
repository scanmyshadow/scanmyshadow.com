=== TLDR
In **October 2022**, an attacker used a **compromised user credential** to access the customer system of **MyDeal**, Woolworths' majority-owned online marketplace. About **2.2 million** customers were affected; **1.2 million** had only their email exposed. Others had **names, phone numbers and delivery addresses**, plus **birthdates** for people who verified their age to buy alcohol. No passwords, payment data or ID documents. The data is old, but contact details don't expire.

=== BODY
## What happened

[Information Age (ACS)](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2022/data-of-2-2m-australians-exposed-in-mydeal-breach.html) reported that an attacker used a **compromised user credential** to get into MyDeal's **customer relationship management system**. MyDeal said it found the breach the same day, notified the OAIC and worked with security experts.

## What was exposed

| Data | Who |
|---|---|
| Email address only | ~1.2 million customers |
| Names, emails, phone numbers, delivery addresses | The rest of the ~2.2 million |
| Birthdates | Customers who verified age to buy alcohol |
| Passwords | Not compromised |
| Payment details | Not compromised |
| Passport or licence details | Not compromised |

Fun fact nobody asked for: buying a bottle of wine online is how some customers ended up with their birthday in a breach.

## Why a 2022 breach still matters

Phone numbers, addresses and birthdays rarely change. Data from older breaches gets combined with newer ones to build fuller profiles. Your MyDeal phone number plus your [[was-i-affected-by-the-qantas-data-breach|Qantas]] Frequent Flyer number plus your [[australia-origin-energy-data-breach-900000|Origin]] partial bank number starts to look like a very convincing scam call.
