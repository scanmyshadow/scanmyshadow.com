=== TLDR
If you're **concerned about your safety or your family's**, you can apply to go on New Zealand's confidential **unpublished roll**. Your name and address **won't appear on the public roll** or the printed roll at voting places. You'll need **supporting information**, such as a **protection order**, a **restraining order**, or a **letter** from police, an employer, lawyer or advocate. Apply via **vote.nz/unpublished** or call **0800 36 76 56**. You can still vote.

=== BODY
## Who can apply

According to the [Electoral Commission](https://vote.nz/assets/Accessible-formats/2026/ROE47b-Concerned-for-your-personal-safety-Large-Print-GE26.pdf), **anyone concerned about their own or their family's personal safety** can apply. You "don't lose the right to vote" by not wanting your details public.

## Evidence that helps

You don't need all of these, but include something:

- A **letter** explaining why your work or circumstances put you at risk, from an **employer, lawyer, social worker, advocate** or someone of standing in your community.
- A copy of a **protection order** under the **Family Violence Act 2018**.
- A copy of a **restraining order** under the **Harassment Act 1997**.
- Information from a **police or corrections officer** about the risk.

## How to apply

1. Get the form at **vote.nz/unpublished**, or call **0800 36 76 56** to have one posted.
2. Fill it in and return it:
   - **Email:** unpublished.roll@elections.govt.nz (a scan or photo is fine)
   - **Post:** Electoral Commission, PO Box 190, Wellington 6140
3. Not enrolled or need to update? Also complete an **enrolment form**.

## What's hidden

Your details are kept secure, aren't given to anyone, and **don't appear on the public roll** or the **printed roll used at voting places**.

## Voting on the unpublished roll

You can vote anywhere in a general election or by-election, but you'll cast a **special vote** with an extra form confirming your electorate. For local elections, ask your council's electoral officer to send voting papers.

## Keep it current

Tell the Electoral Commission about name or address changes by submitting a new enrolment form. You can ask to return to the public roll if things change.

## Background from the Privacy Commissioner

A Privacy Commissioner [blog on the unpublished roll](https://www.privacy.org.nz/blog/electoral-roll) explains the legal basis: under **section 115 of the Electoral Act**, the Electoral Commission can put you on the unpublished roll if publishing your details would be **prejudicial to your safety or your family's**. Points worth knowing:

- Only the **Registrar of Electors** can see the unpublished roll.
- It's used mainly by people with **protection or restraining orders**, and by **Police** staff and their families.
- Nearly **16,000 people** were on it in **2014**, according to Ministry of Justice figures cited then.
- You stay on it until your circumstances change; the Registrar **periodically confirms** your details.
- Because you're not on the printed roll, you cast a **special vote**, before or on election day.

The blog dates from 2014, so check vote.nz for current forms and rules, but the principles haven't changed.

=== FAQ
Q: Who can see the unpublished electoral roll in NZ?
A: Only the Registrar of Electors, according to the Privacy Commissioner. You cast a special vote because you're not on the printed roll.
Q: Who can go on the unpublished electoral roll in NZ?
A: Anyone concerned about their own or their family's personal safety.
Q: What evidence do I need?
A: Supporting information such as a protection order, restraining order, or a letter from police, an employer, lawyer, social worker or advocate.
Q: How do I apply for the unpublished roll?
A: Get the form at vote.nz/unpublished or call 0800 36 76 56, then email it to unpublished.roll@elections.govt.nz or post it.
Q: Can I still vote if I'm on the unpublished roll?
A: Yes. You'll cast a special vote and complete an extra form.
