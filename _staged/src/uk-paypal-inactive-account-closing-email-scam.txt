=== BODY
## The email

According to [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) (September 9, 2026), the message says your PayPal account is restricted because of inactivity and will close in 90 days. A link leads to a fake page to “create a new account.”

It's a clever twist. Most PayPal scams claim suspicious activity. This one targets people who *haven't* used PayPal in a while, who are less likely to remember what a real PayPal email looks like and more likely to think “oh, I'd better keep that.”

## What the fake page wants

- your PayPal email and password;
- card and bank details for the “new account”;
- sometimes a one-time code to “verify.”

## How to check

Open the PayPal app or type paypal.com and log in. If there's a real problem, it will show in your account. If you don't use PayPal any more, close it properly from inside your account rather than via an email.

## How to report

PayPal's [reporting page](https://history.paypal.com/us/security/report-suspicious-messages) says to forward suspicious emails to **phishing@paypal.com**, then delete them. In the UK, also forward to **report@phishing.gov.uk** (the NCSC).

## If you entered details

1. Change your PayPal password, and your email password if they were the same.
2. Turn on two-step verification in PayPal.
3. Check for unknown payments and linked cards.
4. Call your bank or **159** if you entered card details.

Old dormant accounts are a security risk in general. === FAQ
Q: Will PayPal close my account for inactivity by email link?
A: Check by logging in through the app or paypal.com yourself. Don't use links in the email.
Q: How do I report a fake PayPal email?
A: Forward it to phishing@paypal.com and, in the UK, to report@phishing.gov.uk.
Q: What does the fake "create a new account" page steal?
A: Your login, card and bank details, and sometimes a one-time code.
Q: I don't use PayPal any more. What should I do?
A: Log in directly and close the account from your settings, so it can't be misused.
