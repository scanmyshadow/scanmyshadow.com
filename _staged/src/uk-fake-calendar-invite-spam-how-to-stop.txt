=== TLDR
On **12 October 2026**, Which? warned of a **resurgence in scam calendar invites**: events that appear in your calendar with a fake payment warning, a "support" number or a meeting link. **Responding, even with "Decline," confirms your account is active.** Delete them without replying, then change one setting: in **Google Calendar** set "Add invitations to my calendar" to **"Only if the sender is known"**; on **iPhone** delete unknown **subscribed calendars**; in **Outlook** turn off automatic adding of events from email. Forward the original email to **report@phishing.gov.uk**.

=== BODY
## What these invites look like

You open your calendar and there's an event you never made: "Payment of £399 approved, call to cancel," "Your subscription renews today" or a "billing update meeting." According to [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/11/fake-calendar-invites-are-spreading-heres-how-to-remove-them-and-prevent-more), common lures include:

- warnings about an **impending payment**;
- prompts to **call a phone number**;
- fake **meeting invites**, such as a fake "Geek Squad" billing update;
- subject lines with words like **investment, invoice, bonus payout** or **urgent meeting**.

The goal is usually money: a fake tech-support call, getting you to install **remote-access software**, selling you something overpriced, or collecting bank details.

It's phishing in a smarter costume. People have learned to ignore spam emails, but a calendar alert pops up on your phone like a reminder from your own life.

## How they get into your calendar

Malwarebytes says the invites arrive as **email attachments** or as **download links in messaging apps**. Many calendar apps add invitations automatically as soon as the email lands, so the event appears before you've opened anything. And because calendars **sync across devices**, one invite can show up on your phone, laptop and watch at once.

## Why "Decline" is the wrong button

The [Which? alert](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) is clear: responding to the invite confirms to the scammer that your email account is active. "Decline" is still a response; it sends an email back. Delete instead, and in Outlook pick **"Do not send a response"** if offered.

There's a second reason. In Google Calendar, [replying to an invitation](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-calendar-spam-protection-default/) makes the sender "known," so future invitations from them can be added automatically.

## How to stop them

### Google Calendar (computer)

1. Open Google Calendar, click the **gear icon**, then **Settings**.
2. Under **Event settings**, change "Add invitations to my calendar" to **"Only if the sender is known"** or **"When I respond to the invitation in email."**
3. Untick **"Show events automatically created by Gmail"** if you don't use that feature.
4. Delete the fake events and remove any calendars you don't recognise.

Android Police reported Google testing "Only if the sender is known" as the new default, replacing "From everyone." Check yours anyway: your account may still be on the old setting.

### Android

In the Calendar app, tap **Menu > Settings > General > Adding invitations**, then choose **"Only if the sender is known."**

### iPhone and iPad

1. Open **Settings**, then **Calendar** (on newer iOS: Settings, Apps, Calendar).
2. Go to **Accounts**, then **Subscribed Calendars**.
3. Delete anything you didn't add on purpose.

Spam "subscribed calendars" often come from tapping a pop-up on a dodgy website, so clear your browser history too if they keep coming back. The same sites often show fake virus warnings ([[uk-fake-apple-virus-pop-up-scareware|how to close those]]).

### Mac

Go to **Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security > Calendars** and check which apps have access. Remove access for anything you don't recognise.

### Outlook

1. Delete the invite **without opening links or attachments**.
2. Choose **"Do not send a response"** if asked.
3. Mark the sender as **junk** and report it to Microsoft as phishing.
4. Turn off **automatic adding of events from email** in your calendar settings.
5. Remove any extra or subscribed calendars you didn't add.

**Delete the event on every synced device**, or it can reappear. Malwarebytes says reappearing entries are almost always a sync issue.

## If you called the number or joined the "meeting"

- **Installed something?** Disconnect from the internet, uninstall it and run a security scan.
- **Shared card or bank details?** Call your bank on the number on your card, or dial **159** to reach your bank's fraud team.
- **Gave remote access?** Follow our [[tech-support-scam-remote-access-recovery|remote-access scam recovery guide]].
- **Lost money?** Report it: see [[how-to-report-a-scam-to-action-fraud-uk|how to report a scam in the UK]].

Forward the original invitation email to **report@phishing.gov.uk**.

## Why your address was targeted

Calendar spam goes to email addresses scraped from breaches and old sign-ups. If you get a lot of it, run a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] to see which breaches include your address, and consider an [[email-alias-temporary-phone-number-privacy|email alias]] for shopping sign-ups.

=== FAQ
Q: Should I decline a spam calendar invite?
A: No. Declining sends a reply and confirms your account is active. Delete it without responding.
Q: How do I stop spam invites in Google Calendar?
A: In Settings, under Event settings, set "Add invitations to my calendar" to "Only if the sender is known."
Q: How do I remove spam calendars on iPhone?
A: Go to Settings, Calendar, Accounts, Subscribed Calendars and delete the ones you don't recognise.
Q: Why do deleted spam events keep coming back?
A: Usually because of syncing. Delete the event or calendar on every device and account where it appears.
Q: Where do I report a scam calendar invite in the UK?
A: Forward the email to report@phishing.gov.uk.
