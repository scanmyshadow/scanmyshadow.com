Which? warned on October 12, 2026 about scam calendar invites. They appear in your calendar with a fake payment warning, a support number or a meeting link. === BODY
## What these invites look like

You open your calendar and there's an event you never made: “Payment of £399 approved, call to cancel”, “Your subscription renews today” or a “billing update meeting.” According to [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/11/fake-calendar-invites-are-spreading-heres-how-to-remove-them-and-prevent-more), most warn of an impending payment, and the goal is usually money: fake tech support, remote-access software or stealing bank details.

It is phishing in a smarter costume. People ignore spam emails, but a calendar alert pops up on your phone like a reminder from your own life.

## Why “decline” is the wrong button

The [Which? alert](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) is clear: responding to the invite confirms to the scammer that your account is real and active. “Decline” is still a response. It sends an email back. Delete instead, and in Outlook pick “Do not send a response” if offered.

## How to stop them

### Google Calendar

1. Open Google Calendar on a computer, click the gear icon, then Settings.
2. Under Event settings, change “Add invitations to my calendar” to **“Only if the sender is known”** or **“When I respond to the invitation email.”**
3. Untick “Show events automatically created by Gmail” if you don't use that feature.
4. Delete the fake events and remove any calendars you don't recognise.

### iPhone and iPad

1. Open Settings, then Calendar (on newer iOS: Settings, Apps, Calendar).
2. Go to Accounts, then Subscribed Calendars.
3. Delete anything you didn't add on purpose.

Spam “subscribed calendars” often came from tapping a pop-up on a dodgy website, so also clear your browser history if they keep returning.

### Outlook

1. Delete the invite without opening links or attachments.
2. Mark the sender as junk.
3. Turn off automatic adding of events from email in your calendar settings.
4. Remove any extra calendars you didn't add.

Delete the event on every synced device, or it can reappear.

## If you called the number or joined the “meeting”

If you installed anything they asked you to, disconnect from the internet, uninstall it and run a security scan. If you shared bank or card details, call your bank on the number on your card or dial 159. Our guide on [[tech-support-scam-remote-access-recovery|recovering from a remote-access scam]] covers the steps. Forward the original email to report@phishing.gov.uk.

## Why your address was targeted

Calendar spam goes to email addresses scraped from breaches and old sign-ups. If your inbox gets a lot of this, run a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] to see which breaches your address appears in, and consider an [[email-alias-temporary-phone-number-privacy|email alias]] for shopping sign-ups.

=== FAQ
Q: Should I decline a spam calendar invite?
A: No. Declining sends a reply and confirms your account is active. Delete it without responding.
Q: How do I stop spam invites in Google Calendar?
A: In Settings, Event settings, set "Add invitations to my calendar" to "Only if the sender is known."
Q: How do I remove spam calendars on iPhone?
A: Go to Settings, Calendar, Accounts, Subscribed Calendars and delete the ones you don't recognise.
Q: Where do I report a scam calendar invite in the UK?
A: Forward the email to report@phishing.gov.uk.
