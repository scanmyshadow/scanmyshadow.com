=== TLDR
To complain to the **Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC)**: (1) complain **in writing to the organisation first**; (2) give it **30 days**; (3) check whether an **external dispute resolution scheme** applies (e.g. **AFCA** for banks, telco, energy schemes); (4) if you're still unhappy, lodge **in writing** with the OAIC online or at **oaicintake@oaic.gov.au**. The OAIC can't take complaints over the phone.

=== BODY
## Step 1: Complain to the organisation

The OAIC says you [first need to complain](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints/before-you-lodge-a-privacy-complaint-with-us) to the organisation or agency you think mishandled your information. For breaches specifically, [ask them](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/data-breaches/make-a-data-breach-complaint) whether your information was affected.

Do it in writing. Keep it short:

- what happened and when;
- what personal information was involved;
- what you want (an explanation, correction, deletion, an apology, compensation).

## Step 2: Wait 30 days

The OAIC considers **30 days** a reasonable time to respond. If they ignore you or the response is poor, move on.

## Step 3: Check for another scheme

Some complaints go to an **external dispute resolution (EDR) scheme** first:

| Sector | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Banks and financial services | **AFCA** |
| Telecommunications | Telco EDR scheme |
| Some electricity, gas and water | Energy and water ombudsman |
| Victorian public transport, some tolling | Relevant scheme |

## Step 4: Lodge with the OAIC

- **Online form**, or download the form and email **oaicintake@oaic.gov.au**, or post to **GPO Box 5288, Sydney NSW 2001**.
- Must be **in writing**. No phone complaints.

**Include:**

- your name and contact details;
- the organisation's name (and ABN if known);
- what happened and when;
- your complaint to them and their response;
- reference numbers;
- what they've done about it;
- the outcome you want;
- for credit reporting complaints, a **copy of your credit report**.

You can nominate a representative, such as a lawyer, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty.

## Data breach grounds

You can complain if:

- you weren't **notified quickly enough** about a breach that the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme required them to tell you about; or
- the breach raises **other privacy issues** (like poor security).

## What happens next

The OAIC reviews it and may decline or investigate. It's not a fast process, so keep copies of everything.

## Other options

For serious invasions of privacy, you can now also go to court under the [[australia-privacy-statutory-tort-explained|statutory privacy tort]]. That needs legal advice.

=== FAQ
Q: Do I have to complain to the company before the OAIC?
A: Yes. The OAIC says you first need to complain to the organisation or agency.
Q: How long should I wait for a response?
A: The OAIC considers 30 days a reasonable period.
Q: Can I complain to the OAIC by phone?
A: No. Complaints must be in writing, online, by email or by post.
Q: Where do bank privacy complaints go?
A: Usually to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) first.
