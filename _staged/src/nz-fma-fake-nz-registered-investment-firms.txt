=== TLDR
The **Financial Markets Authority** warned on **30 October 2025** about scam platforms that **falsely claim to be registered in New Zealand**, often targeting **overseas investors**. They show **fake NZ incorporation certificates**, **copy details from real NZ companies**, and contact people via **social media and messaging apps**, using **Gmail/Hotmail** or **look-alike domains**. Before investing, check the **Companies Register** and the **Financial Service Providers Register**, and make sure the website and contact details match.

=== BODY
## The tactics

From the [FMA's alert](https://www.fma.govt.nz/library/warnings-and-alerts/entities-falsely-claiming-nz-registration/):

- **Fake NZ company incorporation certificates**
- **Copying information** from genuine NZ-registered companies
- Contact through **social media or messaging apps**
- **Overseas phone numbers**, **Gmail or Hotmail** addresses, or **look-alike domains**

The FMA lists many suspected sites, including **RS Finance (rs-fin.com)**, **JRBKR (jrbkr.com)**, **Zennith Vista (zennithvista.com)**, **Allied Holdings Limited Investments (alliedholdingslimited.com)** and **BitsStrategy (bitsstrategy.com)**. Many are flagged as **impersonating legitimate firms**.

## Why "registered in New Zealand" is the hook

NZ has a clean reputation, and it's cheap and quick to register a company. Scammers borrow that reputation. A registration number on a website sounds official, but it might belong to a completely different, real company that has no idea it's being used.

Being on a register isn't the same as being licensed to give investment advice, either.

## How to check in two minutes

1. Search the **Companies Register** for the company name and number.
2. Search the **Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR)**.
3. Check the **website and contact details match** what's on the register exactly.
4. Check the FMA's **warnings and alerts** list.
5. Contact the firm using details **from the register**, not the website or chat.

## Red flags

- **Guaranteed** or **risk-free** returns
- "NZ-based" firm asking you to pay into **overseas bank accounts** or in **crypto**
- Approaches via **social media**, Gmail/Hotmail, or overseas numbers
- Pressure to decide fast

## If you've invested

- Stop paying, including "fees" or "taxes" to withdraw.
- Call your bank immediately.
- Report to the FMA and Police (105).
- Watch for **recovery scams** offering to get your money back for a fee.

=== FAQ
Q: How do I check if a company is really registered in New Zealand?
A: Search the Companies Register and the Financial Service Providers Register, and make sure the website and contact details match.
Q: What did the FMA warn about in October 2025?
A: Scam entities and websites falsely claiming NZ registration, often using fake certificates and copied company details.
Q: Is a company on the FSPR safe to invest with?
A: Not necessarily. Registration isn't an endorsement. Also check the FMA's warnings and confirm contact details match the register.
Q: What should I do if I invested with a fake NZ firm?
A: Stop paying, call your bank, report to the FMA and Police, and beware of recovery scams.
