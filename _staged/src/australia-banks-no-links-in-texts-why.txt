=== TLDR
Australia's big banks have made text scams easier to spot. **NAB removed links from its text messages** in 2023, when about **40 million of its 112 million texts** that year contained one. **CommBank** says it never includes a link in an SMS or email to log in. **Westpac** says it never asks you to verify your identity via a link. **ANZ** says it never asks you to click a link to log in. And since **1 July 2026**, texts from unregistered brand names show as **"Unverified."** The rule is simple: **a "bank" text with a link is a scam.** Open the app instead.

## What each bank says

| Bank | Policy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **NAB** | Removed links from text messages; texts point you to the app, website or phone line instead | [NAB](https://www.nab.com.au/about-us/security/latest-fraud-scam-alerts), [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/nab-removes-links-from-text-messages-597719) |
| **CommBank** | Never includes a link in an SMS or email to log in directly | [CommBank](https://www.commbank.com.au/security-privacy/report-hoaxes.html) |
| **Westpac** | Never asks you to verify identity by clicking a link in an email or SMS | [Westpac](https://www.westpac.com.au/security/latest-scams/) |
| **ANZ** | Never asks you to click a link to log in | [ANZ](https://www.anz.com.au/content/anzcomau/en/personal/security/latest-scams-australia.html) |

## Why NAB did it

When NAB announced the change in July 2023, [iTnews reported](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/nab-removes-links-from-text-messages-597719) that the bank had sent **112 million messages** that year, about **40 million** with a link. Chief executive **Ross McEwan** said the aim was "to make it as simple as we can for customers to know whether a message from NAB is legitimate," and his advice was blunt: "don't click on a link."

Two exceptions are worth knowing: at the time, subsidiaries **Ubank, JBWere and Citi Consumer Bank** were excluded, and a text sent by a NAB representative **during a phone call you're already on** may still contain a link.

## Did it work?

Removing links was one of several measures, so no single number proves it. But the trend is clear:

- NAB said reports of **NAB-branded spoofing scams fell 29%** (January to May 2023 compared with August to December 2022), as it worked with telcos to stop criminals getting into legitimate text threads ([NAB](https://www.nab.com.au/news/personal-finance/scam-strategy-update)).
- Between **October 2024 and March 2025**, NAB said it stopped or recovered **more than $48 million** heading to scammers, almost $2 million a week, and customers **abandoned more than $195 million** in payments after real-time alerts ([NAB](https://www.nab.com.au/news/technology-ai/nab-scam-stop-and-recover)).

NAB's **Chris Sheehan** described scammers as "experts in socially engineering people to make the payment themselves," which is why friction like alerts and no-link texts matters.

## The new "Unverified" label

From **1 July 2026**, texts that use a brand name as the sender but aren't on the **SMS Sender ID Register** show as **"Unverified"** instead, grouped with other unregistered senders, according to the [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-05/unregistered-branded-sms-be-labelled-unverified-1-july-acma-warns). ACMA member Samantha Yorke: "Scammers rely on familiar names to make fake messages look real." More in our guide to the [[australia-acma-sms-sender-id-register-unverified-label-1-july|Sender ID Register]].

A verified name doesn't make a message safe, but an "Unverified" text claiming to be your bank is a strong warning sign.

## The one rule

**If a text says it's from your bank and has a link, it's a scam.** You don't have to judge the wording, the logo or the sender name. Spoofed messages have even landed in the same thread as real ones.

It's like a restaurant announcing it never asks for your PIN at the table. Easy test, very hard to fake around.

## But watch for the workarounds

Scammers adapted. Instead of links, they now say **"call this number"**: see [[australia-bank-new-login-call-this-number-email-scam|the callback scam]]. And they ask you to **read out a code**: see [[australia-bank-code-read-back-scam|read-back code scams]].

So the full rule set:

1. **No links** from banks in texts. Ignore any that have them.
2. **No calling numbers** from unexpected messages. Use the number on your card or in the app.
3. **No reading out codes** to anyone.
4. **No "safe accounts."** Ever.

## What to do with a fake

Forward it to your bank and to the ACMA's scam text line (**0429 999 888**), then delete it. Full list in our [[australia-report-spam-sms-acma-0429-999-888|reporting guide]]. If you already tapped a link and entered details, call your bank straight away using the number on your card.

Q: Do Australian banks send links in text messages?
A: NAB removed links from its texts in 2023, and CommBank, Westpac and ANZ say they don't send links to log in or verify identity.
Q: Is a text from my bank with a link a scam?
A: Based on the banks' own policies, treat any bank text with a login or verification link as a scam.
Q: What does "Unverified" mean on a text in Australia?
A: Since 1 July 2026, texts using a brand name that isn't on the SMS Sender ID Register are labelled "Unverified."
Q: What should I do instead of clicking?
A: Open your banking app or type the bank's website yourself.
