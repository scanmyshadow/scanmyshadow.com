=== TLDR
Australians can get a **free credit report every 3 months** from each credit reporting body, and Moneysmart suggests checking **at least once a year**. Since **1 April 2026** there are only **two**: **Equifax** and **Experian**, which now includes the former **illion** data. They hold different information, so check both. Online reports usually arrive in a day or two; by email or post, up to 10 days. **Fixing errors is free**: ignore companies that charge to "repair" your credit. Unknown loans or enquiries on your report can be a sign of identity theft.

=== BODY
## The rules

From [Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/credit-scores-and-credit-reports):

- **Free every 3 months** from each credit reporting agency.
- Check **at least once a year**, and more often after a breach.
- **Different agencies hold different information**, so one report may not show everything.
- **Online:** usually a day or two. **Email or post:** up to 10 days.

The OAIC [adds](https://oaic.gov.au/privacy/credit-reporting/access-your-credit-report) two more free triggers: you can get a free copy if you've been **refused credit in the last 90 days**, or after your information has been **corrected**. Outside those, a fee can apply, but it must not be excessive.

## Two bodies now, not three: what happened to illion

Australia used to have three credit reporting bodies. From **1 April 2026**, illion and Experian became a **single Experian** body, according to the government-backed [CreditSmart](https://www.creditsmart.org.au/otherhelpfulservices/credit-reporting-bodies/changes-to-the-experian-and-illion-credit-reporting-bodies) service. What that means for you:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where's my illion report? | It's part of the **new Experian report** |
| Why does a lender letter still say "illion"? | Usually it means the new Experian report |
| My score changed. Is that fraud? | Not necessarily: the calculation changed. Pull the new report to check |
| I had a ban on my old Experian file | Probably no longer needed; bans on other files continue as normal |

| Body | Phone (OAIC) |
|---|---|
| **Equifax** | 138 332 |
| **Experian** (incl. former illion) | 1300 783 684 |

Go to each website directly and look for the **free** report option. Some will steer you toward paid products first. Polite, but no.

## What's on your report

- Personal details: name, date of birth, address history
- Credit products and **repayment history** (last two years); a payment counts as missed if it's **14 days** late
- Financial hardship arrangements (these don't affect your score, and are deleted after 12 months)
- **Defaults**: a debt of **$150 or more**, **60+ days** overdue, where the lender couldn't reach you. They stay **five years**, or seven for a "clearout"
- **Credit applications**, bankruptcies and court judgments
- **Who has requested your report**

## Credit scores

Scores run from 0 to 1,000 or 1,200 depending on the agency, with bands like low, fair, good, very good and excellent. Some agencies show your score free; free score services exist too. Moneysmart warns to **avoid providers that ask you to pay** or want your card details.

## Spotting identity theft

Look for:

- **loans or cards you don't recognise**;
- **credit enquiries** from lenders you never applied with;
- **addresses** you've never lived at;
- **defaults** on accounts you never opened.

Moneysmart says unknown loans or debts may mean you're a victim of identity theft. Next steps:

1. Contact the lender and the agency.
2. Place a [[australia-credit-ban-how-to-request|credit ban]].
3. Report to police and ReportCyber; get help from IDCARE (1800 595 160).

## Fixing errors

Ask the agency to correct anything wrong or out of date. It's **free**. Moneysmart warns against companies that charge to remove negative information: only **incorrect** information can be removed, and you can request that yourself.

## When to check

- every few months as routine;
- after any big breach you're in ([[australia-biggest-data-breaches-list|see the list]]);
- before applying for a home loan;
- after losing your wallet or ID.

A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] tells you which breaches your email is in, a good prompt to pull a report.

=== FAQ
Q: How often can I get a free credit report in Australia?
A: Every three months from each credit reporting body, plus after a credit refusal in the last 90 days or a correction.
Q: Does illion still exist for credit reports?
A: From 1 April 2026, illion and Experian operate as a single Experian credit reporting body. Your former illion information is in the new Experian report.
Q: How long does it take to get my credit report?
A: Online usually a day or two; by email or post up to 10 days.
Q: Do I have to pay to fix errors on my credit report?
A: No. Fixing incorrect information is free. Only incorrect information can be removed.
Q: How can my credit report show identity theft?
A: Look for loans, enquiries, addresses or defaults you don't recognise.
