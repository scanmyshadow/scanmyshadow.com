=== TLDR
An IRS **Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)** is a six-digit number that blocks anyone else from filing a federal tax return with your **Social Security number or ITIN**. Anyone who can verify their identity can get one, and parents can get one for **dependents**. The fastest route is your **IRS online account**; **Form 15227** works if your AGI is under **$84,000** (single) or **$168,000** (married filing jointly); or visit a **Taxpayer Assistance Center**. You get a **new PIN every year**, viewable online from **mid-January to mid-November**. The IRS **never** asks for your IP PIN by phone, email or text.

=== BODY
## Why bother

Tax identity theft is simple: a criminal with your name, date of birth and SSN files a return before you and pockets the refund. You find out when your real return is rejected. If your SSN has been in a breach (and after [[was-i-affected-by-the-national-public-data-breach|National Public Data]], that's a lot of people), an IP PIN is the free fix. According to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin), a return filed with a wrong or missing IP PIN is **rejected** if e-filed and **delayed** if filed on paper. That's the protection: a fraudster has your SSN but not this year's PIN.

## Who can get one

- Anyone with an **SSN or ITIN** who can verify their identity.
- **Parents and legal guardians** can request one for **dependents**, which protects a child's SSN from being claimed fraudulently.
- **Non-filers** can get one too.

## Three ways to apply

| Route | Who it's for | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **IRS online account** (IP PIN section of your profile) | Anyone 18+ who can verify identity online | The fastest way |
| **Form 15227** (submitted online; the IRS calls you to verify) | Can't verify online, last AGI under **$84,000** single or **$168,000** married filing jointly, valid SSN or ITIN, and a phone | PIN by mail, usually **4 to 6 weeks** |
| **In person** at a Taxpayer Assistance Center (by appointment) | Anyone who can't use the other two | PIN by mail, usually about **3 weeks** |

Under-18s must use one of the alternative options. For an in-person visit, bring **one federal or state photo ID plus one other ID**. For a dependent, bring your photo ID and one more ID for yourself, plus **two IDs for the dependent**, such as a birth certificate and Social Security card.

## The yearly routine

- You get a **new IP PIN every calendar year**, valid for that year only.
- If you enrolled **online**, the IRS **doesn't mail it**. Log in each year to get it from your **Profile** tab (generally mid-January to mid-November).
- If the IRS enrolled you after a confirmed identity theft, you'll get a **CP01A notice** by mail each year.
- Give it to your tax software or preparer when prompted. It's needed on **Forms 1040, 1040-NR, 1040-PR, 1040-SR and 1040-SS**, including **prior-year returns** filed during the year.

**Set a reminder for January.** Tax season is when you'll need it, and logging in before you sit down with your software saves a rejected return.

## Lost your PIN?

Check your online account, or request a reissue by mail through the IRS's lost IP PIN process. **Don't** file a new Form 15227 just to replace a lost PIN.

## The scam to watch for

The IRS says it will **never ask for your IP PIN**, and calls, emails or texts asking for it are scams. The only people who should know it are you, your tax professional when you're ready to file, and the IRS. Our guide to [[irs-scam-what-to-know|IRS scams]] covers the usual tricks, and [[tax-season-scams-2026|tax season scams]] the seasonal ones.

## Who should get one first

- anyone whose SSN appeared in a **data breach**;
- anyone who's had **mail stolen** or a wallet lost;
- parents of **children** whose SSNs are rarely watched;
- **retirees and non-filers**, whose SSNs can be used for a fake return without them noticing.

## If someone already filed in your name

See [[how-to-report-tax-identity-theft-irs|how to report tax identity theft]] and file **Form 14039**. After a confirmed identity theft, the IRS can enrol you and mail a **CP01A notice** with your PIN each year. Also freeze your credit ([[how-to-freeze-your-credit-us|how]]) and check how exposed your details are with a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]].

=== FAQ
Q: What is an IRS IP PIN?
A: A six-digit Identity Protection PIN that must be on any federal tax return filed with your SSN or ITIN. Without it, an e-filed return is rejected.
Q: How do I get an IP PIN?
A: The fastest way is through your IRS online account. If you can't verify online, use Form 15227 (if your AGI is under the limit) or visit a Taxpayer Assistance Center.
Q: Can I get an IP PIN for my child?
A: Yes. Parents and legal guardians can request an IP PIN for dependents, using the alternative options for under-18s.
Q: Do I get a new IP PIN every year?
A: Yes. A new IP PIN is generated each calendar year. If you enrolled online, log in to get it; it isn't mailed.
Q: What if I lose my IP PIN?
A: Check your online account or request a reissue by mail. Don't file a new Form 15227.
Q: Will the IRS ever ask for my IP PIN?
A: No. The IRS never asks for it by phone, email or text. Any request is a scam.
