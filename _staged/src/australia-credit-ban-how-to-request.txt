=== TLDR
A **credit ban** is Australia's version of a credit freeze. Credit reporting agencies can't share your credit file with lenders **without your written consent**, so a fraudster can't easily open credit in your name. It lasts **21 days** at first, and you can extend it to **12 months** if you're at risk of fraud. You can apply through **one agency and ask it to place bans with the others**. Existing cards and loans keep working.

=== BODY
## What a credit ban does

According to [IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/fact-sheets/credit-bans-australia), a ban "freezes" access to your credit file. Credit reporting agencies can't disclose your consumer credit information to credit providers without your **written consent**, unless the law requires it.

Since most lenders check your file before approving credit, a ban makes it very hard for someone using your stolen details to get a loan, card or phone plan in your name.

## What it doesn't affect

- Your **existing** credit cards and loans keep working.
- You can still **make repayments**.
- **Exception:** if credit is due for renewal (say, a card expiring), the ban can get in the way.

## How long it lasts

| Stage | Length |
|---|---|
| Initial ban | **21 days** |
| Extension | **12 months**, if you're a victim of or at risk of credit fraud |

Apply for an extension **before the ban expires**, ideally at least **10 days** before. You may be asked for a police report number or a cyber.gov.au report number.

## How to request one

1. Go to one credit reporting agency's ban request page. **Equifax** has an online form (it emails you once the ban is in place); **Experian** has an online ban request form.
2. **Ask that agency to place bans with the other agencies** too. IDCARE says one request can cover both.
3. Keep the confirmation.
4. Diary the end date, so you can extend if needed.

## When to use one

- Your **driver licence, passport or Medicare details** were in a breach (think [[was-i-affected-by-the-optus-data-breach|Optus]] or [[was-i-affected-by-the-latitude-financial-data-breach|Latitude]]).
- Your **wallet or ID** was stolen.
- You **gave ID to a scammer**.
- You see **credit enquiries** you don't recognise ([[australia-free-credit-report-equifax-experian-illion|check your report]]).

## Need credit while banned?

You'll need to give written consent for a lender to access your file, or wait for the ban to end. Plan big applications (like a mortgage) around it.

## Get help

**IDCARE** offers free support on **1800 595 160** (Australia) or **0800 121 068** (NZ). See our [[australia-scamwatch-idcare-recovery-process|IDCARE recovery guide]].

=== FAQ
Q: How long does a credit ban last in Australia?
A: An initial ban lasts 21 days. You can extend it to 12 months if you're a victim of or at risk of credit fraud.
Q: Does a credit ban affect my existing credit cards?
A: No. You can still use existing cards and repay loans, unless credit is due for renewal.
Q: Do I need to contact every credit agency?
A: IDCARE says you can apply through one agency and ask it to place bans with the others.
Q: When should I extend my credit ban?
A: Before it expires, ideally at least 10 days beforehand.
