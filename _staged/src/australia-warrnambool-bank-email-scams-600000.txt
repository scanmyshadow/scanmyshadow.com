=== TLDR
Warrnambool police said on October 26, 2026 that four people in Victoria's south-west lost about $600,000 in a week to the same trick: an email saying their bank account had been compromised, a phone number to call, and a “bank” employee who talked them through moving their money. One woman transferred $270,000 in four payments. Police say there is “zero chance” of getting it back. Banks don't email you to say your account is compromised and ask you to call.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [The Standard](https://www.standard.net.au/story/9379442/warrnambool-police-issue-scam-alert-after-600000-lost/), four local victims reported losing about $600,000 between them in the space of a week. The pattern was the same each time:

1. An email arrives saying your bank account has been compromised.
2. It gives a phone number to call.
3. The person who answers sounds like a bank fraud officer and tells you what to do to “protect” your money.
4. You move the money yourself.

The largest loss: a middle-aged woman who transferred **$270,000 in four separate transactions** in September. Police say the money went to an account in Australia, apparently via a hijacked account, then overseas.

Detective Sergeant Derek Verity didn't sugar-coat it: “There is zero chance of those funds being recovered.”

## Why calling the number feels safe

Most of us have learned not to click links. Calling a phone number feels like the cautious option, because you are the one making the call. That's the trick. If the number came from the email, you are calling the scammer, and everything they say afterwards sounds reasonable because you started the conversation.

## The rule that stops this

Warrnambool police were clear that banks don't contact customers this way by email. If you get a worrying message about your account:

- **don't use any number or link in it;**
- call the number on the back of your card, or visit a branch;
- treat any instruction to move money to a “safe account” as a scam, every time.

No genuine bank fraud team asks you to transfer your own money to protect it. We explain the script in [[australia-bank-impersonation-scams-what-to-know|bank impersonation scams in Australia]].

## If you've sent money

Call your bank immediately using the number on your card and ask them to try to stop or recall the payments. Then report it at [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover), to [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/), and contact [[australia-scamwatch-idcare-recovery-process|IDCARE]] for free support. Speed matters: once money leaves Australia it is much harder to trace.

## Why they targeted these people

Scam emails like this go to huge lists, and a convincing one only needs a few replies. Lists come from breaches, which is why checking your own exposure helps. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows if your email has been in known breaches.
