=== TLDR
Campbell River RCMP warned on October 20, 2026 about an email that looks like it's from FedEx, says a package for you was stopped “south of Campbell River,” and demands $1,200 to release it. It even carries a crude RCMP logo. Police don't collect fees by email, and no courier or agency charges you to “release” an intercepted parcel.

=== BODY
## What the email says

According to [Oak Bay News](https://oakbaynews.com/2026/10/20/campbell-river-rcmp-warns-about-fraudulent-intercepted-package-scam/), the message claimed a package addressed to the recipient had been stopped south of Campbell River and would only be released once a fee was paid: **$1,200**. A Vancouver Island resident spotted it, reported it and didn't click anything. Good instincts.

The twist is the mash-up of authority: a courier brand plus a police logo. Delivery scams usually threaten you with a missed parcel. This one hints you might be in trouble too.

## Red flags the RCMP pointed out

- lots of spelling and grammar mistakes;
- badly written, oddly formal content;
- an unauthorized, crude RCMP logo meant to look official;
- a demand for immediate payment.

Campbell River's officer in charge, Jeff Preston, said scammers “frequently attempt to create a sense of urgency or authority.” This email tried both at once.

## The rules that make it obvious

- **Police don't collect fees or fines by unsolicited email.**
- **No legitimate agency charges a fee to release a package.** Real customs duties on imports are handled by the carrier on its own site and are usually modest, not a round $1,200.
- **A parcel you aren't expecting can't be “yours” in trouble.** If you didn't order anything, there's nothing to release.

## What to do with one

1. Don't click links, open attachments or reply.
2. If you are expecting a parcel, check it on FedEx's site by typing the address yourself.
3. Delete it and report it to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/).

=== FAQ
Q: Does FedEx charge a fee to release an intercepted package?
A: Not like this. Legitimate customs charges are handled through the carrier's own site, and police never collect fees by email.
Q: Why does the email have an RCMP logo?
A: To scare you into paying. The RCMP says the logo was used without authorization.
Q: How much did the scam email demand?
A: $1,200, according to Campbell River RCMP.
Q: Where do I report a fake delivery email in Canada?
A: Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, then delete it.
