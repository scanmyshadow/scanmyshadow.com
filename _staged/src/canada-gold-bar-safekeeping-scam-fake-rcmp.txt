=== TLDR
Nova Scotia RCMP warned on October 5, 2026 about a scam where callers pose as a big bank's fraud department, say foreign hackers are after your savings, and tell you to convert the money into gold from a real Canadian dealer. Then a fake “police officer” with a fake badge comes to collect the gold for “safekeeping.” Police never hold your valuables for safekeeping.

=== BODY
## How the scam works

The [RCMP's release](https://rcmp.ca/en/nova-scotia/news/2026/10/4357534) describes a two-act scam:

1. **The bank call.** Someone posing as the fraud department of a major Canadian bank says your money is at risk from foreign hackers.
2. **The gold plan.** You're told to protect your savings by buying gold, from a genuine online dealer, using savings and investments.
3. **The pickup.** A “police officer” meets you in person, shows a badge and takes the gold to keep it safe.

In the Nova Scotia case under investigation, the scammers said they were RCMP investigators. Their badges did not match real RCMP badges, and they weren't in uniform or in police vehicles. Similar cases have been reported elsewhere in Canada.

## Why gold?

Because the old routes are getting harder. Banks now question large wire transfers and cash withdrawals, and gift cards and crypto ATMs draw attention. Buying gold from a legitimate dealer looks like a normal investment, so it raises fewer flags. And unlike a bank transfer, a gold bar handed over on your doorstep can't be recalled. It's the [[uk-card-cleaning-courier-scam|courier scam]] with a shinier prop.

## Not the first gold scam in Canada

Gold has become a favourite prop for fraudsters targeting older Canadians:

| When | Where | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| April 2025 | Ottawa and Norfolk County, Ont. | Ottawa Police and the OPP warned that seniors were told by **pop-up messages** that their online financial information had been stolen, then persuaded to **buy gold** that was picked up from their homes. Two Ottawa victims each bought **over $500,000** in gold; a Norfolk County victim lost nearly **$350,000** ([Ottawa Police](https://www.ottawapolice.ca/en/news/warning-ops-and-opp-alert-public-to-new-scam-targeting-seniors-with-fake-gold-investment-schemes.aspx)) |
| Early 2026 | Vancouver Island and across B.C. | A different trick: strangers in parking lots sell **fake gold jewellery** at a "discount," claiming they need money for gas or a hotel. West Shore RCMP reported at least **nine** incidents in Langford since January ([Victoria Buzz](https://victoriabuzz.com/2026/03/police-warn-of-gold-scam-as-cases-surface-on-vancouver-island-and-across-bc)) |
| October 2026 | Nova Scotia | The fake bank call plus fake RCMP pickup described above |

Ottawa and OPP police called the 2025 version a new twist on the **grandparent scam**: where scammers once demanded "bail money," they now say buying gold is the only way to protect your savings. In every case, victims were told **not to tell family or their bank**.

## The line to remember

Cpl. Mitch Thompson of the RCMP: “Police will not take financial instruments from people for safekeeping.”

That covers gold, cash, bank cards, jewellery and anything else. If someone in a suit with a badge wants to hold your valuables, they are not police.

## Red flags

- A bank “fraud team” that wants you to move money out of the bank.
- Instructions to keep it secret from family or branch staff.
- Any plan where a stranger collects money, gold or cards from your home.
- A badge shown at the door without uniform, marked car or a call-back you can verify.

## What to do

If you get a call like this, hang up before following any directions, the RCMP says. Then call your branch or the number on the back of your card, or talk to someone you trust. Report it to local police, or anonymously through Crime Stoppers, and to the [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/). Ottawa Police's advice is simple: don't buy gold or gift cards in response to unexpected pop-ups, emails or calls, and if someone asks you to, talk to a trusted family member or friend first. Fraud Detective Shaun Wahbeh: "If something feels suspicious, always ask for help."

Older adults are the main targets of in-person pickup scams. Our guides on [[protect-elderly-parents-from-phone-scams|protecting elderly parents from phone scams]] and [[talk-to-parents-about-scam-prevention|talking to parents about scams]] help start the conversation.

## How they knew which bank you use

Often they guess, because a handful of banks serve most Canadians. Sometimes they have leaked data. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows if your email has been in a breach, which is one way scammers build their call lists.

=== FAQ
Q: Will police ever hold gold or money for safekeeping?
A: No. The RCMP says police will not take financial instruments from people for safekeeping.
Q: Was the gold dealer part of the scam?
A: In the case described, victims bought gold from a legitimate online dealer. The scam was the calls and the fake officers who collected it.
Q: What should I do if my bank's fraud team calls?
A: Hang up and call the number on the back of your card or your local branch before doing anything.
Q: Have gold scams happened elsewhere in Canada?
A: Yes. In 2025, Ottawa Police and the OPP warned about seniors who bought gold after fake pop-up alerts; two Ottawa victims each lost over $500,000.
Q: Where do I report this scam in Canada?
A: Report to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
