=== TLDR
Scam texts claiming to be from a state DMV say you have an unpaid traffic ticket and threaten prosecution, registration suspension or loss of your license unless you pay through a link. State DMVs, including Colorado's, say they don't send unsolicited texts demanding payment or threatening suspension. Don't click; check your record on your state's official DMV site and report the text to the FBI's IC3 and the FTC.

=== BODY
## What the text looks like

Versions have spread across many states since 2025. Typically:

- “[State] DMV Final Notice: You have an outstanding traffic ticket. Pay by [date] to avoid suspension of your driving privileges and prosecution.”
- a link to a site that looks like a state DMV page;
- sometimes a list of “penalties” and a countdown.

The [Colorado DMV](https://dmv.colorado.gov/press-release/colorado-dmv-issues-urgent-warning-on-new-text-scam-threatening-prosecution-and) described messages from a “Colorado DMV” or similar official-sounding sender threatening prosecution, registration suspension and revocation of driving privileges. Nevada and others have issued similar warnings.

## What DMVs actually do

The Colorado DMV says plainly that these messages are not from it, and that it doesn't contact people by unsolicited text or email to demand ticket payments or threaten prosecution or suspension. Most states handle tickets through the court or agency that issued them, by mail.

If you haven't done anything that could result in a ticket, Colorado says, it's almost certainly a scam. And if you have, the text is still almost certainly a scam: real tickets don't arrive as a link from a random number.

## Don't give them

- your driver's license number;
- your Social Security number;
- card or bank details.

Those three together are an identity thief's starter pack.

## How to check

Go to your state DMV's website by typing the address yourself (it will end in .gov), or call the number listed there. Check your license, registration and any tickets. Don't use any contact details from the text.

## Report it

- [FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/), including the number that texted you and the website linked;
- [FTC ReportFraud](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/);
- forward the text to 7726.

=== FAQ
Q: Does the DMV text about unpaid tickets?
A: State DMVs such as Colorado's say they don't send unsolicited texts demanding ticket payment or threatening suspension.
Q: What happens if I clicked the DMV text link?
A: If you entered card details, call your bank immediately. If you entered your license or SSN, consider a credit freeze.
Q: How do I check if I really have a ticket?
A: Use your state DMV's official .gov website or phone number, not the text.
Q: Where do I report a fake DMV text?
A: Report it to the FBI's IC3 and the FTC, and forward it to 7726.
