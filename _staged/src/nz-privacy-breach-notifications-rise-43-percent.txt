=== TLDR
In its **2024/25** annual report, New Zealand's Privacy Commissioner reported **serious privacy breach notifications up 43%** and **privacy complaints up 21%** (on a previous record year). Commissioner **Michael Webster** wants a **right to erasure**, a **much stronger penalty regime**, proof of privacy compliance, protections around **automated decisions**, and **more powers**. Survey: **82%** of Kiwis want more control over their personal information.

=== BODY
## The numbers

From the [Privacy Commissioner's November 2025 release](https://www.privacy.org.nz/tuhono-connect/statements-media-releases/new-zealand-needs-privacy-act-modernisation-and-nbsp/):

| Measure | 2024/25 |
|---|---|
| Serious privacy breach notifications | **+43%** |
| Privacy complaints | **+21%** (on a record 2023/24) |

**2025 Privacy Survey:**

| Concern | Share |
|---|---|
| Protecting personal privacy is a major concern | 66% |
| Concerned about children's privacy | 67% |
| Concerned about AI decisions using their data | 62% |
| Want more control and choice | **82%** |

## What the Commissioner is asking for

1. A **right to erasure**: ask organisations to delete your personal information.
2. A **significantly stronger penalty regime**.
3. Organisations must **demonstrate** how they meet privacy obligations.
4. Stronger protections for people affected by **automated decision-making**.
5. **Stronger powers** for the Commissioner.

Webster: "The Privacy Act currently provides insufficient incentives for many organisations."

## Why it matters right now

Today, the Privacy Act has **no civil fines** for failing to protect data; the only fines are for not notifying a breach. After the [[nz-manage-my-health-data-breach-privacy-commissioner-findings|Manage My Health]] and [[nz-medimap-attack-patients-marked-deceased|MediMap]] incidents, the government's cyber action plan asked the Ministry of Justice to look at **civil penalties** ([[nz-cyber-security-action-plan-stolen-data-offence|details]]).

In other words: the watchdog has a loud bark. It's asking for some teeth.

## What you can do today

Even without erasure rights, you already have strong rights under the current Act:

- **See what an organisation holds** on you ([[nz-privacy-act-access-request-how-to|access requests]]).
- **Ask for corrections.**
- **Complain** to the Privacy Commissioner ([[nz-privacy-commissioner-complaint-how-to|how]]).
- **Freeze your credit** if your ID leaked ([[nz-credit-suppression-how-to|credit suppression]]).

A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] shows which breaches your email is in, a good starting list for access requests.
