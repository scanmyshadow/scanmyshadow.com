=== TLDR
A caller says they're from your bank and need to "verify" you. A code arrives by SMS or email. They ask you to **read it back**. That code is usually the key to **something they're doing in your account**: a login, a new payee, a digital wallet, a transfer. **ANZ** warned of this exact "**Advisor Verification**" scam in February 2026. **ANZ, Westpac, NAB and CommBank** all say they'll never ask for your one-time codes. Hang up.

=== BODY
## What happens

ANZ's [February 2026 alert](https://www.anz.com.au/content/anzcomau/en/personal/security/latest-scams-australia.html): callers pretending to be ANZ send an email with a fake "verification code" and ask you to read it back.

Variations you'll meet:

- "I'm sending a code to confirm it's you."
- "We've blocked a payment; read me the code to cancel it."
- "We need the code to secure your account."

The twist: the scammer is often **logging in or approving something right now**, and your bank's real security system just sent **you** the code for it.

## The banks are unanimous

| Bank | What it says it never asks for |
|---|---|
| **ANZ** | One-time passcodes for payments, ANZ Shield or token codes |
| **Westpac** | Online Banking Security Codes, whether by SMS, token or app ([source](https://www.westpac.com.au/security/latest-scams/)) |
| **NAB** | Personal info by email or text; your PIN ([source](https://www.nab.com.au/about-us/security/latest-fraud-scam-alerts)) |
| **CommBank** | Your NetCode ([source](https://www.commbank.com.au/security-privacy/report-hoaxes.html)) |

A code arriving on your phone is like a key in your hand. The bank gave it to **you**. If someone on the phone wants you to hand it over, they're not the bank.

## Why it works so well

- The code is **real**. It came from your actual bank, so it feels legit.
- The caller **sounds professional** and may know your name or partial card number from data breaches.
- It feels like you're **helping security**, not undermining it.

## The 5-second rule

When someone asks for a code: **stop, read the SMS itself.** Real codes often say what they're for ("to add a new payee", "to register a device") and "never share this code." That text is your bank talking to you directly.

## If you read out a code

1. **Hang up** and call your bank on the number on your card or in the app.
2. Ask them to check for **new payees, devices, wallets or transfers**.
3. Change your password from a different device.
4. Report to Scamwatch; get support from IDCARE (1800 595 160).

The same trick hits food delivery and social apps too ([[australia-food-delivery-scams-scamwatch|food delivery scams]]). A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] shows if your details are in the breaches scammers use to sound convincing.

=== FAQ
Q: Will my bank ever ask me to read out a one-time code?
A: ANZ, Westpac, NAB and CommBank all say they never ask for one-time codes or security codes.
Q: What is the ANZ Advisor Verification scam?
A: Callers posing as ANZ send a fake verification code by email and ask you to read it back.
Q: Why do scammers want my verification code?
A: The code often authorises something they're doing in your account, like logging in or adding a payee.
Q: What should I do if I gave a code to a caller?
A: Call your bank on its official number immediately and ask it to check for new payees, devices or transfers.
