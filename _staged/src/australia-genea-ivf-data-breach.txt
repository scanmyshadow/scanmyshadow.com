=== TLDR
Hackers breached fertility provider **Genea** on **31 January 2025** and pulled about **940 GB** of data on **14 February**. The **Termite** ransomware group published patient data. Genea's patient management system held **contact details, Medicare card numbers, health insurance details, medical histories, test results and medications**. Genea says there's no evidence financial details were taken, and it won a **court injunction** banning anyone from using or sharing the data. Patients should expect sensitive, targeted scams.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/26/hackers-publish-sensitive-patient-data-allegedly-stolen-from-australian-ivf-provider-genea):

- Hackers got into Genea's **Citrix environment** on **31 January 2025**.
- They extracted about **940 GB** on **14 February**.
- **Termite** listed Genea on its leak site and claimed to publish patient data.
- CEO Tim Yeoh confirmed "data taken from our systems has been published externally."
- The MyGenea app was taken offline, per ABC News.

## What was in the system

Genea said the compromised patient management system held:

- contact details;
- **Medicare card numbers**;
- **health insurance details**;
- **medical histories, test results and medications**.

Samples seen by TechCrunch appeared to include **government ID documents**. Genea said there's **no evidence** that credit card or bank details were taken. The number of affected patients wasn't disclosed.

## The court injunction

Genea obtained an injunction that bars the attacker **and anyone who receives the data** from accessing, using, sharing or publishing it. That won't stop criminals, but it gives legal weight against anyone in Australia who tries to spread it.

## Why fertility data is different

Fertility treatment is about as private as health information gets. The danger isn't just fraud. It's **extortion and emotionally targeted scams**: messages that mention treatment, demand payment to "keep it quiet," or pose as the clinic asking for "outstanding fees."

## What patients can do

1. **Never pay extortion demands.** Report them to police and ReportCyber at cyber.gov.au.
2. **Verify clinic contact** by calling Genea on a number you look up yourself.
3. **Use MFA** on email and myGov.
4. **Talk to IDCARE** (1800 595 160) for free support.
5. **If ID documents were exposed**, see our guide to [[australia-passport-number-data-breach-replace|passports in breaches]].
6. If you feel unhappy with how it was handled, see [[australia-oaic-complaint-how-to|how to complain to the OAIC]].

And look after yourself. Having health details exposed can be distressing; it's reasonable to lean on people you trust.
