=== TLDR
You're selling something online. A "buyer" asks for your **PayID and email**. Soon you get an official-looking email saying your account must be **"upgraded to a business account"** and you need to **refund a deposit** or pay a fee before the money is released. It's fake. **PayID is free**, and you **never need to upgrade** your account to receive a PayID payment. Don't pay, report the buyer, and check payments only in your banking app.

=== BODY
## How the scam works

Per [Westpac](https://westpac.com.au/security/articles/using-payid-and-avoid-online-marketplace-scams):

1. A buyer on a marketplace is keen, often without asking about the item. Bonus red flag.
2. They ask for your **PayID** (usually your mobile or email) **and your email address**.
3. You receive a fake email, styled like a bank or PayID, saying your account was **upgraded to a business account**, and you must **refund a "deposit"** or pay an upgrade fee.
4. The buyer pressures you: "I've already paid, the money's waiting, just send the fee."

There's also a **new account** version where the "buyer" insists you open a new bank account or PayID to complete the sale.

## How common it is

NAB [warned in 2023](https://www.nab.com.au/news/personal-finance/payid-scam) that scam reports to the bank had risen **38%** year on year, and that Australians lost **$260,000** to PayID impersonation scams in the previous year, according to Scamwatch. NAB's fraud chief Chris Sheehan put it simply: "the biggest red flag of any PayID-related scam is often if someone asks you for money to upgrade an account."

WA ScamNet [describes the ending](https://scamnet.wa.gov.au/scamnet/Scam_types-Buying_or_selling-Classified_scams-PayID_impersonation_scams.htm): you pay the "refund", the buyer vanishes, and your item is **still unsold**. One WA victim lost **$1,300**.

## The facts that kill the scam

- **"PayID is a free service."** You never pay a fee to use it.
- You **never need to upgrade** your account to receive a PayID payment.
- PayID payments show up in **your banking app**, not in an email.
- NAB says **PayID doesn't contact users by email, text or message**. Any "PayID" email is fake.
- **Never send money first** to receive a payment. Real buyers pay you; you don't pay them.

If it's not in your app, it hasn't arrived. That's the whole test.

## Red flags

- Buyer asks for your **email** as well as your PayID
- Buyer is in a rush, sends a "courier" or "friend" to collect
- Offers above asking price, or money in advance
- **New profiles**, no friends, or overseas buyers
- Moving the chat off the marketplace to email or text

## What to do

1. **Don't reply** to upgrade or refund emails. Don't pay anything.
2. **Check your banking app.** No money there means no sale.
3. **Report the buyer** to the marketplace.
4. **Forward the fake email** to your bank's hoax address (Westpac: hoax@westpac.com.au; texts to 0497 132 032). Others are in our [[australia-report-spam-sms-acma-0429-999-888|reporting guide]].
5. **Report to Scamwatch.**
6. **Already paid?** Call your bank immediately.

## Selling safely

- Keep chats on the platform.
- Accept payment only once it appears in your account.
- Avoid international transfers, gift cards and crypto.

=== FAQ
Q: Does PayID send emails about payments?
A: NAB says PayID doesn't contact users by email, text or message, so treat any "PayID" email as a scam. Check your banking app instead.
Q: Does PayID charge fees?
A: No. PayID is a free service and you should never pay a fee to use it.
Q: Do I need a business account to receive PayID payments?
A: No. You never need to upgrade your account to use PayID.
Q: How do I know a PayID payment arrived?
A: Check your banking app. Emails claiming money is "pending" are a scam sign.
Q: Where do I report a PayID scam?
A: Report the buyer to the marketplace, forward the email to your bank and report to Scamwatch.
