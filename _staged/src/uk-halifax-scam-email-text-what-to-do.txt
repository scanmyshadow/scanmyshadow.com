=== TLDR
Fake Halifax emails ask you to confirm personal information through a link to a lookalike site (Which? flagged one in September 2026). Halifax says genuine emails include your name and part of your postcode, and it never links straight to the Online Banking sign-in page, asks you to move money, or asks for a test payment. Forward scam emails to security@halifax.co.uk and texts to 7726.

=== BODY
## The email

[Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-latest-scam-alerts-from-which-aBRLy2b02WkC) warned on September 7, 2026 about a Halifax impersonation email asking you to confirm personal information via a link to a fake Halifax site. It looks routine. That's the trick.

## Halifax's tells for a genuine email

From [Halifax's scam messages page](https://www.halifax.co.uk/helpcentre/protecting-yourself-from-fraud/scam-messages.html):

- Genuine Halifax emails include **your name and part of your postcode**.
- They may link to information or the secure app, but **never directly to the Online Banking sign-in page**.
- Check the sender's full address by hovering or tapping. If it doesn't match the name, it's likely a scam.

The postcode detail is a nice, simple check: scammers sending to millions rarely have it.

## What Halifax will never do

- send you to a page asking for sign-in, personal or banking details;
- tell you to move money to another account;
- ask you to make a test payment online;
- ask you to share personal details or move money by phone, email or text;
- ask you to log in or verify yourself through links or QR codes.

## Why bank impersonation keeps working

UK Finance's annual fraud report, [covered by The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5256106) in June 2026, put total UK payment fraud losses for 2025 at **£1.28 billion** across **3.81 million cases**. Authorised push payment (APP) fraud, where you're tricked into sending the money yourself, rose **19% to over £576 million**, and **impersonation** scams were among the types with double-digit rises in case numbers. **66%** of incidents started on online platforms and **17%** through telecoms, which is exactly where a fake Halifax email or text lands.

Treat a fake "confirm your information" email as a possible first step. Details entered on a lookalike site give criminals enough to sound convincing in a follow-up call claiming to be from your bank.

## How to report

- **Emails**: forward to **security@halifax.co.uk**, then delete. You can also message Halifax through the app.
- **Texts**: forward to **7726**, then delete ([[uk-7726-how-to-report-scam-texts-calls|how]]).
- Unsure? Call Halifax on a number you trust, or dial **159** ([[uk-159-scam-hotline-how-it-works|how 159 works]]).

## If you entered details

Contact Halifax immediately using its official [contact page](https://www.halifax.co.uk/helpcentre/protecting-yourself-from-fraud/contact-us.html) or 159, change your login details and check recent payments. Report losses to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/).

## Will you get your money back?

If you were tricked into making a bank transfer, the UK's mandatory reimbursement rules for APP scams, in force since **October 2024**, cover most victims, up to **£85,000** per claim, with some exceptions. The Payment Systems Regulator's July 2026 review found reimbursement rates rose from **54% to 65%**, and APP losses over Faster Payments fell by around **21%**, according to [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/07/payment-fraud-falls-by-73m-following-psr-reimbursement-scheme/). Report quickly; see our guide to [[uk-action-fraud-crm-reimbursement-code|reimbursement for scam victims]]. If Halifax refuses, you can take it to the [[uk-financial-ombudsman-scam-complaint-guide|Financial Ombudsman]].

A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows if your email address is in breach data, which is how these emails find you.
