=== TLDR
Yes, under UK data protection law you can claim compensation if an organisation broke the law and caused you harm, including non-financial harm like distress. But the ICO can't award compensation. You ask the organisation directly, and if it refuses, a court decides. If you can't show damage or distress you may get nothing and could pay the other side's costs, so the ICO strongly recommends independent legal advice first.

=== BODY
## The right exists

According to the [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/data-protection-and-journalism/taking-your-case-to-court-and-claiming-compensation), you can claim compensation if an organisation broke data protection law and that caused you harm. That covers:

- **material damage**, such as money you lost;
- **non-material damage**, such as distress.

## What the ICO does and doesn't do

The ICO investigates complaints and can fine organisations, but it **cannot award you compensation**, even when it finds the organisation broke the law. Its findings can be useful evidence, but a court makes its own decision. If you want the ICO to look at how your data was handled, see [[how-to-complain-to-the-ico|how to complain to the ICO]].

## How a claim works

1. **Ask the organisation first.** Write explaining what happened, the harm it caused and what you want. Some organisations simply pay.
2. **If it refuses**, write saying you intend to go to court. Courts expect you to have tried to settle.
3. **Go to court** to enforce your rights, claim compensation, or both. There's court guidance for England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.
4. **The judge decides the amount**, based on how serious the breach was and its impact on you.

## The risks nobody mentions in the adverts

“You could be owed £££ for the [big brand] breach” adverts are everywhere after a headline. The ICO's warning is sobering: if you can't show damage or distress, you may get nothing and **could be ordered to pay the other side's costs**. Take independent legal advice before claiming. If you use a claims firm, read the fees carefully and check them on the [FCA Firm Checker](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams) or with the Solicitors Regulation Authority.

And beware fake compensation calls: scammers love “you're owed money for a breach,” the same way they piggyback on [[uk-car-finance-compensation-scam-calls-texts|car finance redress]].

## Build your evidence

- keep the breach notification;
- note what happened next (phishing attempts, fraud, time spent, stress);
- keep bank statements showing losses;
- ask for your data with a [[subject-access-request-dsar-guide|subject access request]].

## Know what leaked

Before claiming, it helps to know which breaches you're actually in. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|email exposure check]] shows the known breaches that include your email.

=== FAQ
Q: Can I get compensation for a data breach in the UK?
A: Yes, if an organisation broke data protection law and caused you financial loss or distress.
Q: Can the ICO award compensation?
A: No. The ICO can't award compensation; you claim from the organisation or through the courts.
Q: Can I claim for distress alone?
A: Non-material damage like distress is covered, but you need to show it. Get legal advice first.
Q: Is there a risk in claiming?
A: Yes. If you can't show damage or distress you may get nothing and could be ordered to pay costs.
