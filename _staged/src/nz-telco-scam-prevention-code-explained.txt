=== TLDR
Under the **TCF Scam Prevention Code**, NZ telcos must **detect, verify, block and report** scam activity. In practice: **call and SMS filtering**, daily scam alerts between providers, **in-store ID checks for SIM swaps**, and a **"yes" text reply** before your number can be **ported** to another provider. Most major banks and key agencies can flag scam numbers to telcos. The Code went under **review in June 2025**, and the 2026 anti-scam programme includes it.

=== BODY
## What the Code requires

From the [TCF's June 2025 release](https://www.tcf.org.nz/news/telecommunications-industry-to-review-scam-prevention-code-with-consumer-focus):

- Stop scam calls and texts **as close to the source** as possible.
- **Share** scam call and SMS information across the sector and with third parties.
- Set out how providers and SMS partners **flag and communicate** suspicious traffic.
- **Educate** consumers.
- Providers must be able to **detect, verify, block and report** suspected scams.

**Nine** non-telco organisations, including most major banks and key government agencies, can send scam notifications to telcos to investigate and block.

## What it means for your phone

From the [TCF's scam measures page](https://www.tcf.org.nz/digital-living/online-safety/scam-prevention-measures):

| Protection | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Call and SMS filtering** | Telcos detect scam patterns and block them |
| **Daily scam alerts** | TCF working group shares alerts; providers block numbers |
| **7726** | DIA sends daily reports to operators ([[nz-report-scam-text-7726|how to use]]) |
| **SIM swaps** | Customers must **verify ID in store** |
| **Number porting** | You must **reply "yes" by text** to approve a port, which also lets you flag fraud |
| **IMEI check** | Free lookup to see if a second-hand phone is block-listed |

That port confirmation text is your tripwire. If you get one you didn't ask for, **don't reply yes**. Call your provider straight away.

## The review

The TCF is reviewing the Code to include new anti-scam initiatives, ministerial roundtable feedback, consumer education and reporting, and closer work with **digital platforms**. The [[nz-anti-scam-alliance-2026-work-programme|2026 Anti-Scam Alliance plan]] lists the review as a priority.

TCF CEO Paul Brislen: "scams are becoming more sophisticated, and the impacts on everyday New Zealanders are increasing."

## Limits

Filters can't catch everything. A fake cell tower can send texts that never touch your carrier's network ([[nz-sms-blaster-fake-cell-tower-auckland|SMS blaster case]]).

## What you can do

- Report scam numbers to your telco and texts to 7726.
- Protect your number from SIM swaps; see [[sim-swap-fraud-how-it-works-and-how-to|SIM swap fraud]].
- Run a free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] to see if your number is in breach data.

=== FAQ
Q: Do NZ telcos block scam calls?
A: Yes. Under the Scam Prevention Code, telcos filter and block suspected scam calls and texts.
Q: How do NZ telcos prevent SIM swap fraud?
A: Customers must verify their ID in store for SIM swaps.
Q: How does number porting protection work in NZ?
A: You must reply "yes" by text to approve a port, which also lets you flag a fraudulent request.
Q: Is the Scam Prevention Code being updated?
A: The TCF announced a review in June 2025, and it's part of the 2026 anti-scam programme.
