=== TLDR
In **June 2026**, Scamwatch warned about **food delivery scams** hitting customers, restaurants and delivery workers. Scammers pose as **DoorDash, Uber Eats**, a restaurant or a customer and ask for **one-time codes, logins, your mobile number or bank details**. They use them to **take over accounts**, change payment settings or **redirect earnings**. Rule: never share a code, and deal only through the official app.

=== BODY
## Who gets targeted

[Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-food-delivery-scams) says the scams hit three groups:

| Who | What the scammer says |
|---|---|
| **Customers** | "There's a problem with your order/refund, read me the code we just sent" |
| **Restaurants** | "We're Uber Eats/DoorDash, we need to verify your account" |
| **Delivery workers** | "That order was fraudulent / a double order, give us your details for compensation" |

## How it works

1. Scammers contact you about an **order, refund, cancellation, account issue or payment problem**.
2. They might say they **can see your order** to sound legit, then ask for "extra" details or payment **outside the app**.
3. They ask for a **one-time code, login or bank details**.
4. With that, they **take over the account**, change payment settings or **redirect money**. For riders, that can mean **earnings** go to the scammer.

Your dinner arrives cold. Your account doesn't come back at all. Bad night.

## Red flags

- Unexpected calls or messages about orders, refunds, cancellations or account issues
- **Requests for codes**, passwords, logins or bank details
- Unexpected **password reset emails**, login alerts or account changes
- **Pressure** to act fast

## Stop, Check, Protect

- **Stop:** don't rush, don't click links in unexpected messages, don't share codes or financial details.
- **Check:** contact the restaurant or platform through its **official app or website**.
- **Protect:** if you shared money or info, act fast. Change passwords, contact the platform and your bank.

## If you've been hit

1. Change your app password and email password.
2. Remove unknown devices and payment methods from the app.
3. Call your bank if card or bank details were shared.
4. Report to Scamwatch and get help from IDCARE (1800 595 160).

A one-time code is the key to your account. Nobody legit needs you to read it out. That's the same lesson as our guide to [[australia-bank-code-read-back-scam|"read me the code" bank scams]]. A free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] shows if your number or email has leaked.

=== FAQ
Q: Do Uber Eats or DoorDash ask for verification codes by phone?
A: Treat any request for a one-time code as a scam. Scamwatch says scammers use codes to take over accounts.
Q: How do food delivery scams target drivers?
A: Scammers tell workers an order was fraudulent or doubled and ask for details as "compensation", or access their accounts to redirect earnings.
Q: What should I do if I gave a code to a scammer?
A: Change your passwords, remove unknown devices and payment methods, contact the platform and your bank, and report to Scamwatch.
