=== TLDR
On **16 August 2025**, TPG Telecom confirmed someone used an **iiNet employee's stolen login** to access an order management system. Taken: **280,000 active iiNet email addresses**, **20,000 landline numbers**, **10,000 names, addresses and phone numbers**, and **1,700 modem set-up passwords**. No ID documents, cards or financial details. If your modem still uses its original password, change it today.

=== BODY
## What happened

According to [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/aussie-isp-iinet-breach-280000/), an "unknown third party" logged in with **stolen credentials belonging to an iiNet employee** and reached an order management system. TPG discovered it on 16 August 2025, cut off access, brought in external experts and notified regulators. It's unclear how the login was stolen.

## What was taken

| Data | Count |
|---|---|
| Active iiNet email addresses | ~280,000 |
| Active iiNet landline numbers | ~20,000 |
| Usernames, street addresses, phone numbers | ~10,000 |
| **Modem set-up passwords** | ~1,700 |
| Inactive emails and landlines | Unspecified |
| ID documents, cards, financial info | Not taken, per TPG |

## The modem passwords matter most

Most people never change the password their modem shipped with. If yours was one of the 1,700, someone could use it to get into your home network settings. Ten minutes now beats finding out the hard way.

**Fix:** log in to your modem's admin page and change both the **admin password** and the **Wi-Fi password**. If you're unsure how, iiNet support can walk you through it.

## What to do

1. **Change your modem passwords** (admin and Wi-Fi).
2. **Watch for iiNet-themed phishing**: fake "account suspended", "overdue bill" or "upgrade your modem" emails.
3. **Don't trust caller ID.** A call "from iiNet tech support" asking for remote access is a scam pattern. See [[tech-support-scam-remote-access-recovery|remote access scam recovery]].
4. **Turn on MFA** on your email account.
5. If you use your iiNet email for everything, check our free [[/digital-footprint-checker/|exposure check]] to see other breaches.

## A lesson in stolen logins

This breach didn't need clever hacking, just one employee's password. It's the same weakness behind many big breaches, from [[was-i-affected-by-the-qantas-data-breach|Qantas]] to [[australia-mydeal-woolworths-data-breach|MyDeal]].
