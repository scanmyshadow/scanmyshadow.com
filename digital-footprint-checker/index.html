Digital Footprint Checker: See What You Reveal Online

A practical guide to what a digital footprint is, what other people can actually find about you, how to check it yourself for free, and where an automated scan can help.

Most people have never deliberately searched their own name, phone number, or email address the way a stranger might. When you do, the results can be surprising — old social profiles, forum posts, marketplace listings, or directories you forgot you were ever part of. Collectively, this is your digital footprint: the trail of information about you that's visible or discoverable online.

This page explains what that footprint is made of, what you can check yourself right now for free, and where an automated tool like Scan My Shadow fits in — including what it does and does not claim to do.

The basics

What is a digital footprint?

Your digital footprint is the combination of information about you that exists online, whether you put it there yourself or someone else did. It's usually split into two kinds:

Active footprint

Information you knowingly shared — a LinkedIn profile, an Instagram account, a product review under your real name, a comment on a news article, a listing on OLX or Quikr.

Passive footprint

Information collected or exposed without a deliberate post from you — your email or phone number appearing in a company's data breach, your details listed in a data broker's directory, or your number showing up because you once filled out a form that got shared or sold on.

Why this matters

Passive footprint is the part most people underestimate. You can control what you post, but you can't fully control what a service you signed up for years ago did with your information afterward.

Real exposure

What can people find about you online?

The exact answer depends entirely on your own history online, but common categories include:

  • Contact details — your phone number or email surfacing in old sign-ups, directories, or leaked databases
  • Social and professional profiles — public LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram, or Facebook pages tied to your name
  • Marketplace and classifieds listings — old OLX, Quikr, or similar posts that still show a phone number
  • Breach records — your email or phone appearing in a company's data breach that was later published or indexed
  • Forum and comment history — posts on Reddit, Quora, or news sites linked to an identifiable username or email
  • Business or vendor listings — a number registered against a shop, freelance profile, or classified ad
Example

A phone number used once to sign up for a food delivery app in 2019 can end up associated with that number in a later breach of that company's database — long after the person stopped using the app.

The two identifiers that matter most

Why your phone number and email matter

Your phone number and email address are two pieces of contact information that many accounts, forms, and online services ask for. Because they can appear across different online sources, they can be useful starting points for checking your own digital exposure.

Unlike your name, which many people share, a phone number or email is usually unique to you — so if either one turns up somewhere unexpected, it's a strong signal worth investigating.

Do it yourself, free

How to check your digital footprint yourself

1
Search your own name and number
Search your full name, phone number, and email address individually in quotes on Google — quotes force an exact match instead of a loose one.
2
Check your major accounts
Review the privacy settings on LinkedIn, Instagram, and Facebook to see exactly what's visible to people who aren't your friends or connections.
3
Check a breach database
Sites like Have I Been Pwned let you check whether your email appears in publicly known breaches, free of charge.

This manual approach is genuinely useful and costs nothing — but it's also slow, and it only covers what you think to search for. It won't catch listings you don't know exist, breaches that weren't widely reported, or directory sites that don't rank on the first page of Google.

Tools vs. manual search

What a digital footprint checker does

A digital footprint checker automates the search-and-compare process: instead of you manually Googling variations of your name, number, and email, the tool checks your details against a defined set of sources — breach databases, public directories, and indexed pages — and returns what it finds in one place.

The value isn't magic; it's coverage and speed. A good checker can look through far more sources than a person reasonably would by hand, and it does so in minutes.

What no checker can promise

No tool, including ours, can search "the entire internet" or guarantee 100% accuracy. Coverage is always limited to the sources a tool actually checks, and results reflect what was publicly indexed at the time of the scan.
