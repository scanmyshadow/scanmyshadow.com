Supreme Court Steps In: Nationwide Directives Against Digital Arrest Scams

India’s judiciary has had enough. On August 4, 2026, the Supreme Court issued a sweeping series of nationwide directions aimed at curbing the runaway rise of digital arrest scams. The bench, led by Chief Justice Surya Kant along with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana, directed the Reserve Bank of India to prepare a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) within four weeks for identifying and dealing with mule accounts — bank accounts used by criminals to receive, transfer, or launder illicit money, often without the account holder’s knowledge. States were also told to operationalise cyber fraud grievance and money restoration mechanisms and adopt the e-Zero FIR system.

The Court passed these directions after reviewing a status report filed by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) under the Union Ministry of Home Affairs, which detailed steps already taken following earlier interim orders from December 2025 and February 2026. The Court noted that the latest report reflected encouraging but insufficient progress.

What Is the Digital Arrest Scam?

The digital arrest scam is one of India’s most psychologically devastating cybercrime formats. Here is how it typically unfolds:

  • A fake police officer calls the victim out of the blue.
  • The victim is told a parcel in their name contains illegal drugs or forged passports.
  • Within minutes, the victim is connected to someone posing as a CBI officer or a judge over a video call.
  • The victim’s bank accounts are declared “under investigation,” and they are ordered to transfer money to a so-called “safe account.”
  • The money is then funnelled through layers of mule bank accounts and disappears.

Criminals often use leaked Aadhaar data and other personally identifiable information sourced from the dark web to make their threats sound credible, accusing victims of having their Aadhaar ID used for drug smuggling or hawala transfers.

Goa Police Bust Multi-State Cyber Fraud Ring

In one of the most significant state-level operations of August 2026, Goa police arrested seven people and summoned 24 others for questioning in connection with 40 cyber fraud cases that caused nearly $262,000 in losses. About 40 police teams raided more than 40 locations across 11 states, targeting suspects allegedly involved in investment, parcel, sextortion, digital arrest, and fake hotel-booking scams. Officers seized 21 electronic devices for forensic examination and identified 313 suspected mule bank accounts used to receive or transfer fraud proceeds. Investigators are now tracing links to larger organised cybercrime networks operating across state lines.

Delhi Police Crack Rajasthan Mule Account Supply Ring

Adding to the crackdown wave, the Delhi Police Cyber Cell arrested two key suppliers of mule bank accounts from Jodhpur, Rajasthan. The suspects, identified as Dinesh and Vikas Siyag, were taken into custody following an intensive technical investigation into a task-based investment scam that defrauded a Delhi resident. Mule account suppliers have emerged as a critical link in India’s cyber fraud ecosystem — without these accounts, fraudsters cannot move or launder their stolen money effectively.

Why Mule Accounts Are the Backbone of These Scams

  • Fraudsters recruit individuals (often unknowingly) to open bank accounts in their name.
  • Stolen money is deposited into these accounts and quickly moved through multiple layers.
  • The trail becomes harder to trace with every transfer layer added.
  • Both witting and unwitting mule account holders face criminal liability.

How to Protect Yourself from Digital Arrest Scams

Awareness is your first line of defence. Here are key precautions every Indian internet user should take:

  • No real government agency will video-call you to demand a money transfer. Hang up immediately.
  • Never share bank account details, OTPs, or Aadhaar numbers with unsolicited callers.

The Bigger Picture: India’s Cyber Fraud Epidemic

India lost over ₹11,000 crore to cyber frauds in just the first half of 2024 alone, and the numbers have only climbed since. The country’s rapid digitisation — while transformative for banking, commerce, and governance — has simultaneously created a vast attack surface for organised cybercriminal networks. The lack of digital literacy among large segments of the population, particularly the elderly and those in rural areas, continues to be ruthlessly exploited.

FAQ: Digital Arrest Scams in India

1. What is a digital arrest scam?

A digital arrest scam is a type of cybercrime where fraudsters impersonate law enforcement or judicial officials over a phone or video call, falsely accuse the victim of a crime, and coerce them into transferring money to avoid fake “arrest.”

2. What is a mule bank account?

A mule bank account is a bank account used by cybercriminals to receive and launder stolen money. Accounts are either opened fraudulently or belong to individuals who are paid to let criminals use them.

3. What has the Supreme Court ordered in August 2026?

The Supreme Court directed the RBI to frame an SOP on mule accounts within four weeks, ordered states to operationalise cyber fraud grievance mechanisms and adopt the e-Zero FIR system, and called for stronger safeguards to help victims recover stolen money.

5. What should I do if I receive a digital arrest scam call?

Do not engage or transfer any money. Disconnect the call, note down the number, and report it to India’s National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930 or file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.
