Your phone number can appear online through data breaches, data brokers, public records, and everyday transactions. Reducing exposure is an ongoing process that requires monitoring and regular maintenance.

Your Phone Number Is Worth More Than You Think

In India, your phone number has become a valuable piece of personal information. India is in the middle of a phone number privacy reset, with UPI now masking numbers and NPCI sending circulars to all payment apps to reduce exposure. Yet many of us don't realize how widely our numbers are already circulating online.

A new website called Proxyearth is raising serious privacy concerns by claiming to pinpoint the exact location of any Indian citizen using only their mobile phone number, with initial testing confirming the tool provides accurate and up-to-date personal details. Proxyearth requests a mobile number and returns detailed information including full name, father's name, email address, telecom provider, home address, alternate phone numbers, and even Aadhaar card number.

This isn't just about unauthorized services. Several users, particularly women, reported receiving unsolicited calls and WhatsApp messages from strangers who accessed their phone numbers through UPI transactions. The broader risk is real and growing.

Where Phone Numbers Get Exposed: The Main Sources

Understanding how your phone number ends up online is the first step to protecting it.

Data Brokers and People-Search Sites

India is the second-most affected country when it comes to data broker breaches, with more than 18.7 million personal records of Indian citizens compromised through data breaches in the past 20 years. People search sites expose your address, work, email, phone number, family members, political and religious beliefs, hobbies and routines, and much more.

More than 20 Indian data brokers collect and sell your data, including Truecaller, JustDial, Acxiom India, ZoomInfo, and Zaubacorp. If you ever inquired about a product or listed a business on JustDial, your contact details are publicly visible. Real estate portals share your property search data and contact details with multiple brokers, and every listing inquiry generates lead data that's sold to multiple agents.

Public Records

Your information can appear online because some details come from public records, including property records, vehicle registrations, voter rolls, professional licenses and court filings. This happens even if you're careful about what you share online.

Data Breaches and Leaks

When companies suffer security breaches, your phone number and other details can be exposed. The Durex India website spilled customer names, phone numbers, email addresses, shipping addresses, products ordered and payment amounts because of a lack of proper authentication on its order confirmation page.

Everyday Transactions

In crowded stores, when customers recite their mobile numbers at billing counters, those numbers can be overheard, copied, or misused, which is precisely the kind of exposure India's data law aims to curb. Similarly, a Pune woman posted a video describing how a restaurant staff member obtained her phone number through the outlet's QR-based digital menu and began messaging her late at night, and the incident sparked national debate about whether QR-based data collection was being misused.

Why Phone Number Exposure Matters in India

Every single incident that triggered India's phone number masking movement involved a woman as the victim, and women in India are disproportionately targeted when their phone numbers are accessible to strangers.

Beyond harassment, exposed phone numbers can enable:

  • Impersonation and fraud attempts that sound personal and believable
  • Spam calls, unwanted marketing, and telemarketing harassment
  • Identity theft if combined with other leaked information
  • Targeted phishing and social engineering attacks
  • Physical harassment or tracking in some cases

How to Check if Your Phone Number Is Exposed

Step 1: Run a Digital Exposure Check

Scan My Shadow can check your phone number across more than 1,500 available sources and produce an automated digital exposure report. This gives you a comprehensive baseline of where your number appears online. Start by entering your phone number into an exposure checker to see immediate results.

Step 2: Search Manually in Search Engines

Use Google and Bing to search for your phone number directly. Put your number in quotes to find exact matches: "+91 XXXXX XXXXX" or without the country code. Save any results that show your number publicly listed.

Step 3: Check Data Broker and People-Search Sites

Search for your name and phone number on known Indian data brokers: Truecaller, JustDial, Naukri, LinkedIn, and property portals like MagicBricks and 99acres. Many of these sites allow you to search for yourself to see what information they have.

Step 4: Audit Your Social Media Accounts

Check Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, WhatsApp, and other platforms where you may have listed your phone number in your profile or contact details. Many users unknowingly keep phone numbers visible in their bio or about section.

Step 5: Review Old Account Sign-ups

Think back to websites and services you've used over the years—email services, e-commerce sites, matrimonial sites, property portals, job sites. Your phone number may still be associated with these accounts, even if you haven't used them recently.

Steps to Reduce Your Phone Number Exposure

Opt Out of Data Brokers

You can submit opt-out and deletion requests to data brokers and people-search sites, starting with the big aggregators. This process requires finding each site's opt-out form, verifying your identity, and waiting for confirmation. It's repetitive work, but necessary.

Request Removal from Search Results

Use personal information removal tools in Google and Bing to request that your phone number be removed from search results. Google's removal requests won't delete the source page, but it will stop that listing from showing up in search results.

Lock Down Your Social Media Privacy

Remove your phone number from public social media profiles. Set your account to private where possible, and restrict who can see your contact details. Check privacy settings regularly, as platforms often change their defaults.

Delete Old and Unused Accounts

Close or delete accounts you no longer use, especially old email addresses, dating sites, matrimonial sites, and e-commerce accounts. Remove contact details, restrict profile visibility, and delete old accounts.

Be Cautious With New Signups

Before providing your phone number to any service, ask yourself whether it's truly necessary. Consider using alternative contact methods. If you must share your number, check the company's privacy policy and avoid public platforms where your number will be visible to others.

Use Privacy-Conscious Practices

Avoid publicly announcing mobile numbers, as this exposes sensitive data and undermines "reasonable safeguards," and retailers are expected to shift to secure collection methods such as customer-side entry on POS systems, privacy-protected screens, or QR code-based forms.

Monitor Your Exposure Over Time

Re-check results after seven, 30, and 90 days and repeat removals when data reappears, or use an automated tool to do this. Data brokers sometimes re-list information after deletion, and new breaches can expose your number again. Ongoing monitoring is essential.

Periodic checks using a digital footprint checker help you stay informed about your exposure and catch new listings before they spread further.

Understanding the Legal Framework in India

India's upcoming Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act places stricter obligations on organisations handling personal information, and mobile numbers, names and other customer identifiers are increasingly being treated as sensitive personal data requiring stronger safeguards.

This shift means companies are being held more accountable for how they collect and protect phone numbers. However, existing data already online may take time to be removed, and many data brokers continue to operate with existing datasets. Your proactive steps remain important while the regulatory framework is implemented.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can I completely remove my phone number from the internet?

No, complete removal is extremely difficult. However, you can significantly reduce your exposure by opting out of data brokers, requesting Google and Bing removal, securing your social media accounts, and monitoring new listings. New information may surface over time from new breaches or transactions, so ongoing monitoring is important.

2. If my phone number is on a data broker site, does that mean I've been hacked?

Not necessarily. Your information may include current and old addresses, relatives, phone numbers and public records that were never tied to a phishing link or hacked password, and even if you're careful online, your information can still show up online because some details come from public records.

3. What should I do if I find my phone number exposed on multiple sites?

Start with the highest-traffic sites first (Truecaller, JustDial, Google). Submit removal requests, document your requests with screenshots, and follow up after 7-30 days. Use an exposure checker periodically to track whether removed data reappears. Consider reporting particularly sensitive exposures to CERT-In (India's Computer Emergency Response Team) if they result from security breaches.

4. Is it safe to share my phone number with online services?

Assess whether sharing is necessary. Read privacy policies first. Use strong unique passwords for accounts that have your phone number. Enable two-factor authentication where available. Avoid services with poor security records. Consider using a virtual phone number for services you're less certain about.

5. How often should I check my phone number exposure?

Run a comprehensive check every 3-6 months. After you've submitted removal requests, check again at 7, 30, and 90 days to verify removals were successful and catch any new listings. If you've experienced a data breach, check more frequently (weekly for a few weeks).
