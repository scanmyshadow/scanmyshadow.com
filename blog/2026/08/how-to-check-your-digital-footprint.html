How to Check Your Digital Footprint (Step-by-Step, for Indian Users)

Scan My ShadowAugust 26, 20264 min read

TL;DR: Checking your digital footprint means finding out where your phone number, email, and personal details show up online — through search engines, account lookups, breach-checking tools, and privacy-focused scans. Here's a practical, step-by-step way to do it.

Most people never actually check what's out there about them — it just feels like too big a task, or they assume there's no real way to find out. There is, and it doesn't take long. You don't need to be technical, and you don't need to hire anyone. Here's a straightforward process you can run through in under an hour.

Step 1: Search yourself the basic way

Start with a plain Google search of your name, your phone number, and your email address — each on its own, in quotes. This won't catch everything, but it surfaces the obvious stuff: old social profiles, forum posts, business listings, or news mentions you forgot existed. Try variations too — nicknames, old usernames, your name with your city.

Step 2: Check your email against known breach databases

There are free tools built specifically to tell you if your email address has appeared in a known data breach — these compare your email against leaked databases from past incidents and tell you which ones, and roughly what kind of data was exposed (passwords, phone numbers, etc.). This is a fast way to see if any account of yours was part of a breach you were never notified about.

Step 3: Review what apps and sites actually have your data

Think through everything that has your phone number or email — banking apps, e-commerce, food delivery, old sign-ups, loan or fintech apps, even that one giveaway you entered years ago. Most people are surprised by how long this list gets once they actually sit down and write it out. This step doesn't tell you about leaks directly, but it tells you your exposure surface — the more places have your data, the more places it can leak from.

Step 4: Check your privacy settings on major platforms

Go through Google, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and any other major account and check what's publicly visible versus private. Many people are exposing their phone number or email publicly on a profile without realizing it — this alone can account for a chunk of what shows up in a footprint check.

Step 5: Run a dedicated scan across data broker and leak sources

Search engines and single-breach tools only show a slice of the picture. Data brokers, aggregator sites, and leaked databases often sit outside what shows up on Google. This is where a dedicated privacy scan is useful — a service like Scan My Shadow takes your phone number and email and checks them across 1,500+ sources, then sends back a report showing exactly where and how they're exposed, instead of you manually checking site by site.

Step 6: Decide what actually needs action

Once you know what's out there, you don't have to fix everything at once — or at all. Some exposures are low-risk (an old, inactive forum listing). Others are worth acting on (your number showing up on a data broker site, or a password reuse across accounts tied to a breach). Prioritize based on what's actually sensitive, not just what's longest on the list.

How often should you check?

Once every 6–12 months is reasonable for most people. If you've recently changed jobs, moved cities, signed up for a lot of new services, or suspect something's off (unusual OTPs, spam calls spiking), it's worth checking sooner.

FAQs

Is checking my digital footprint free?

Some parts are — basic search engine checks and single-breach lookup tools are free. Comprehensive scans across broker sites and multiple leak sources typically involve a paid report, since that kind of aggregation takes real infrastructure to run.

Will checking my digital footprint fix the exposure?

No — checking tells you what's out there; it doesn't remove it. Removal usually means contacting individual sites, using opt-out processes, or exercising your rights under India's DPDP Act to request deletion from specific organizations.

Is it safe to enter my phone number and email into a scanning tool?

Use tools that are clear about what they do with your data — ideally ones that delete your submitted details after generating the report rather than storing them indefinitely.

Can someone else check my digital footprint without my permission?

Technically, basic search-engine lookups can be done by anyone. That's part of the reason it's worth checking your own exposure — to see what someone else could already find without you knowing.
