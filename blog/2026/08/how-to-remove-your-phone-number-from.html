How to Remove Your Phone Number From the Internet in India

Scan My Shadow · August 26, 2026
PrivacyCyber SecurityIndiaPhone Number ExposureData Brokers

TL;DR: There's no single button for this — removing your number means working through several categories one at a time: caller ID databases, data brokers, old accounts, and public listings. Here's the actual checklist.

1. Opt out of caller ID databases

Apps like Truecaller build searchable profiles from crowdsourced contact lists — meaning your number may be listed even if you never installed the app yourself, because someone else had you saved. Truecaller provides an opt-out/unlisting process through their website where you can request your number be removed from public search results. This won't stop new listings from reappearing entirely, but it's worth doing and repeating periodically.

2. Request removal from data broker and people-search sites

These sites compile public records and scraped data into searchable profiles. Most operate under a "we'll remove it if you ask" model — but you have to find each one and submit a request individually, since there's no central regulator forcing them to delete proactively yet. Search your own number and name together to identify which sites are actually listing you before submitting removal requests.

3. Clean up old accounts still holding your number

Every forgotten sign-up — an old shopping account, a contest entry, a food delivery profile from a city you no longer live in — is a place your number sits, dormant but still there. Go through your password manager or email search for old confirmation emails, log into what you can still access, and delete the account rather than just uninstalling the app. Deleting the app removes the doorway; deleting the account is what actually triggers removal on the company's end.

4. Reduce public listings

Check your social media profiles — Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram — for whether your number is set to public visibility. Also check any business directories, marketplace listings (OLX, Quikr-style platforms), or old classifieds where you may have posted your number for a sale or service years ago and forgotten to take it down.

5. Register on DND to cut telemarketing

India's Do Not Disturb (DND) registry, managed through TRAI, reduces registered commercial telemarketing calls and SMS. It won't stop scam calls from unregistered or spoofed numbers, but it meaningfully cuts down legitimate marketing traffic, which is worth doing as a baseline step.

6. Use a secondary number going forward

The most effective long-term move isn't cleanup — it's compartmentalization. Keep your primary number reserved for banking, close contacts, and essential services. Use a secondary number (a second SIM, or a virtual number if you're comfortable with one) for sign-ups, giveaways, and one-off forms, so future exposure doesn't touch your main line.

7. Check what's actually still exposed

After going through the steps above, it's worth confirming what remains — since manual removal is incomplete by nature and new listings appear over time. A scan across 1,500+ sources, like the one Scan My Shadow runs on your phone number and email, shows you what's still surfacing after cleanup, so you're not just hoping the work paid off.

What removal realistically achieves

Full, permanent removal from the internet isn't achievable — new data brokers appear, old breaches keep circulating, and some listings reappear after being removed once. What's realistic is a meaningful reduction: fewer active listings, fewer places your number can be scraped from going forward, and a clearer sense of your actual exposure instead of an assumption either way.

FAQs

How long does it take for a removal request to actually work?

It varies by site — some process requests within days, others take weeks, and a few require repeated requests since listings can reappear after re-scraping.

Do I need to pay a service to remove my data?

No — most opt-outs are free if done manually. Paid services exist mainly to automate the process across many sites at once, which saves time but isn't strictly necessary.

Can I request companies delete my data under Indian law?

Yes — the DPDP Act gives you the right to request erasure of personal data once its original purpose has been served, and companies must either comply or provide a valid legal reason for retaining it.
