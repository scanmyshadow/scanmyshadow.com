What Data Do Health and Fitness Apps in India Collect, and Where Does It Go?

Scan My Shadow · August 26, 2026
PrivacyCyber SecurityIndiaPersonal Information

TL;DR: Health and fitness apps — from gym and workout apps to diagnostic and telemedicine platforms — collect some of the most sensitive personal data that exists: biometric readings, medical history, menstrual cycle data, and behavioural patterns. Here's what's actually gathered, how India's health data framework treats it, and what to check before you share it.

Fitness and health apps have become a normal part of daily life for many Indians — workout trackers, diet apps, telemedicine platforms, and diagnostic services. What's less visible is just how sensitive the underlying data is, and how differently it's treated compared to, say, a shopping app.

What These Apps Actually Collect

  • Biometric and physiological data — heart rate, sleep patterns, step count, sometimes blood glucose or other health metrics from connected wearables
  • Medical history — conditions, medications, and consultation notes on telemedicine and diagnostic platforms
  • Location data — workout routes on running/cycling apps, which can reveal home address and daily routine with surprising precision
  • Sensitive personal details — menstrual cycle tracking, mental health check-ins, and similar categories that reveal far more than a typical fitness metric
  • Photos and body-composition data — progress photos, sometimes analysed by AI features for body composition tracking

Where This Data Goes

Once collected, health data typically moves through a few paths: it's stored on the app's servers for the core function to work, it may be shared with connected wearable manufacturers if the app syncs with a device, and — this is the part users often don't expect — it may be shared with third-party advertising or analytics partners depending on the app's specific data practices, even when an account is set to private.

India's approach to health data is shaped in part by the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM), which is building a framework for health record storage and consent-based sharing, alongside the DPDP Act 2023's broader consent and security requirements. This is still an evolving regulatory space — health data doesn't yet have the kind of dedicated, sector-specific protection that exists in some other countries, so much of the practical protection currently depends on the individual app's own policies.

Why Health Data Deserves Extra Caution

Health data is different from most other categories because it's difficult or impossible to change if exposed — you can change a password, but not your medical history. It's also uniquely useful for targeting: insurers, employers (where relevant and legally permissible), or bad actors could theoretically use detailed health patterns in ways that feel invasive even when no single data point seems alarming on its own.

What to Check Before Using a Health or Fitness App

  • Review the app's data-sharing section in its app store listing (both Google Play and the App Store now require developers to disclose data practices)
  • Check whether the app is affiliated with ABDM if you're using it for medical records specifically — ABDM-linked platforms generally follow a more structured consent framework
  • Limit what you connect — not every fitness app needs access to your full health data history; grant only what the specific feature requires
  • Be cautious with free apps that monetise through advertising, since health-adjacent ad targeting is a known industry practice worth being aware of
  • Review permissions periodically, particularly location and photo access, which are often broader than the app's core function requires

This sits alongside the broader question of what happens to your data after you delete an app — health data in particular is worth confirming was actually deleted, not just made inaccessible from the interface.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is health data more protected than other personal data in India?

Not currently through a dedicated sector-specific law, though the DPDP Act's general consent and security requirements apply, and the ABDM framework is building structured protections specifically for digital health records shared through its ecosystem.

Can my fitness app share my data with my health insurance provider?

This depends entirely on the specific app's privacy policy and whether you've consented to such sharing, often through a connected wellness or insurance-partner feature. It's worth checking explicitly rather than assuming either way.

What is ABDM and should I use apps connected to it?

The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission is India's government initiative for digital health records, aimed at giving individuals more control over consent-based sharing of their health data with providers. ABDM-linked apps generally follow a more structured consent process than standalone commercial apps.

Do fitness trackers reveal my home location?

Yes, potentially. Running and cycling route data, if shared publicly or set to a low privacy level, can reveal home address and regular routes with meaningful precision — this has been documented as a real security concern in several contexts.

Should I delete my health app data if I stop using the app?

It's a reasonable precaution, particularly for apps holding medical history or biometric data. Check the app's account settings for a data-deletion option, and follow up with a direct request if one isn't readily available.
