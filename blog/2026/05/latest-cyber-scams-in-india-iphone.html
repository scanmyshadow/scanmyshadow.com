Latest Cyber Scams in India: iPhone Phishing Alert & Rs 52,000 Crore Fraud Loss

Scan My Shadow · May 25, 2026
PrivacyCyber SecurityIndiaUPI fraud

Photo by rupixen on Unsplash

TLDR: India has lost over Rs 52,000 crore to cyber frauds in the past five years, with criminals now targeting iPhone users through sophisticated phishing campaigns. The Ministry of Home Affairs has issued urgent warnings about fake Apple Support messages exploiting users whose devices have been lost or stolen.

India's Staggering Cyber Fraud Losses

According to the Department of Telecommunications (DoT), cyber fraud losses in India have crossed Rs 52,000 crore in the last five years, representing a massive escalation in digital crimes across the country. The cybersecurity incident count surged from 10.29 lakh in 2022 to 22.68 lakh in 2024, reflecting the growing scale and complexity of digital threats.

The financial toll continues to mount, with cyber frauds amounting to Rs 36.45 lakh reported on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP). This alarming trend highlights how India has become both "an iron pillar of progress and a magnet for cyber fraudsters" in its digital transformation journey.

New iPhone Phishing Threat Emerges

The Ministry of Home Affairs' Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C) has identified a sophisticated phishing campaign specifically targeting Apple iPhone users whose devices have been lost or stolen. This represents a new evolution in cybercrime tactics, exploiting the psychological vulnerability of users who have lost access to their digital lives.

How the iPhone Scam Works

According to the National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit (NCTAU), attackers are impersonating Apple Support and "Find My iPhone" services through fraudulent SMS messages. The scam follows this pattern:

  • Criminals send fake messages claiming the lost device has been located or requires urgent security action
  • Messages appear to come from legitimate Apple Support or Find My iPhone services
  • Victims receive SMS with malicious links leading to counterfeit Apple login pages
  • Users are prompted to enter Apple ID credentials and two-factor authentication codes
  • Once obtained, criminals gain complete access to Apple accounts and linked services

Warning Signs to Watch For

Security experts advise users to be suspicious of messages that:

  • Claim urgent action is needed to erase contacts, media, or personal data
  • State that the lost device has been "temporarily switched off"
  • Contain links redirecting to websites with suspicious domain names
  • Request immediate login credentials or OTP verification
  • Create artificial urgency to prevent careful consideration

Major Fraud Busts Across India

Law enforcement agencies have been actively dismantling cyber fraud networks. In May 2026, Delhi Police busted an interstate cyber fraud racket with arrests of five people, including four teenagers from Uttarakhand. The operation involved supplying mule bank accounts to route Rs 3,40,877 in fraudulent money across multiple complaints.

A more significant case involved a Taiwan-linked international cybercrime syndicate that defrauded around Rs 100 crore through "digital arrest" scams, where criminals impersonated anti-terror and law enforcement officers to extort money from victims.

Government Response and Protection Measures

The government has implemented multiple initiatives to combat the cyber fraud epidemic. Over 59 lakh suspicious bank accounts have been frozen through the Digital Intelligence Platform, preventing losses exceeding Rs 1,000 crore. Additionally, approximately 16.97 lakh WhatsApp accounts linked to cyber fraud have been deactivated.

The Sanchar Saathi platform has been strengthened to help users identify and block fraudulent activities. Law enforcement agencies have also established 205 workshops across the country under the Cyber Crisis Management Plan to build capacity and awareness.

Evolving Fraud Techniques

Modern cyber frauds are adapting to new technologies, with criminals employing:

  • AI-powered deepfakes for impersonation
  • Sophisticated spoofing techniques mimicking trusted sources
  • Exploitation of UPI payment systems through compromised mobile numbers
  • Social engineering attacks targeting specific user vulnerabilities
  • International coordination through fraud factories in Southeast Asia

Protection Strategies for Users

Immediate Actions

  • Never click links in unsolicited security messages
  • Verify any Apple communication by contacting support directly
  • Keep "Find My iPhone" activated on all devices
  • Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts
  • Report suspicious messages to cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930

Long-term Security Measures

  • Regular security audits of connected devices and accounts
  • Use strong, unique passwords for all digital services
  • Stay informed about latest fraud techniques and warnings
  • Maintain updated software and security patches
  • Exercise caution when downloading content from unknown sources

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How can I verify if an Apple Support message is genuine?

Legitimate Apple communications never ask for passwords or security codes via email or SMS. Always contact Apple directly through official channels rather than clicking links in messages.

Q2: What should I do if I've already clicked a suspicious link?

Immediately change your Apple ID password, enable two-factor authentication, check account activity, and report the incident to cybercrime.gov.in. Monitor bank accounts for unauthorized transactions.

Q3: Are there specific indicators that help identify phishing messages?

Look for urgent language, requests for immediate action, suspicious sender addresses, generic greetings, and links that don't match official Apple domains. Legitimate companies rarely create artificial time pressure.

Q4: How effective are current government measures against cyber fraud?

The government has frozen over 59 lakh suspicious accounts and prevented Rs 1,000+ crore in losses. However, the Rs 52,000 crore total loss indicates that prevention efforts need continued strengthening and public awareness.

Q5: What makes iPhone users particularly vulnerable to this scam?

iPhone users often have significant digital integration through iCloud, making device loss particularly stressful. Criminals exploit this anxiety and the trusted Apple brand to create convincing phishing campaigns targeting emotional vulnerabilities.
