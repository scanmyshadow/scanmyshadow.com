CBI's Operation Chakra VI: India Strikes Back Against Digital Arrest Fraudsters

India's cybercrime crisis reached a dramatic flashpoint this week. On June 25, 2026, the Central Bureau of Investigation executed one of the largest cybercrime crackdowns in the country's history. Dubbed Operation Chakra VI, the nationwide sweep targeted the fast-growing menace of digital arrest scams — and the numbers are staggering.

At ScanMyShadow, we track how personal data fuels these attacks. Here's a full breakdown of what happened and what it means for every Indian with a smartphone.

What Is Operation Chakra VI?

The CBI conducted simultaneous raids at over 80 locations spread across 16 states to bust a well-organised syndicate of cybercriminals who defrauded victims by posing as law enforcement officers. The group is believed to be responsible for over 200 individual digital arrest scam cases across India. Investigators targeted those running fake call centres, creating dummy bank accounts, and building the technical infrastructure used to terrorise and defraud victims.

What Is a Digital Arrest Scam?

Digital arrest is an online scam where criminals impersonate officials from agencies such as the CBI, police, customs, or the Enforcement Directorate. Here is exactly how the fraud unfolds:

  • Step 1 — The Call: The victim receives an unsolicited call claiming their Aadhaar card, bank account, mobile number, courier package, or taxes are under investigation.
  • Step 2 — The Transfer: The call is handed off to someone posing as a senior government officer or investigator.
  • Step 3 — The Pressure: Fake ID cards, fabricated documents, official logos, and video calls are used to instil fear. Victims are told they are under "digital surveillance" and forbidden from contacting family.
  • Step 4 — The Theft: Scammers keep victims on continuous video calls — sometimes for days — in a fake "custody," until the victim transfers everything they have to "clear their name."

Fraudsters have even been found conducting fake video calls while dressed in police uniforms or sitting in rooms designed to look like government offices. AI-generated voices and spoofed caller IDs are now being deployed to appear completely genuine.

The Scale of the Crisis: By the Numbers

  • In 2025, Indians lost an estimated ₹22,495 crore (~US$2.7 billion) to cyber fraud, with complaint volume jumping 24% to approximately 2.81 million cases.
  • More than 30,000 digital arrest complaints were filed in 2025 alone, with the Supreme Court estimating nationwide losses near ₹3,000 crore for that year.
  • Maharashtra alone registered 10,505 cyber financial fraud cases between January 2025 and May 2026, leading to 2,379 arrests. Mumbai recorded losses of over ₹1,031 crore.
  • The I4C projects annual losses could exceed ₹1.2 lakh crore, amounting to roughly 0.7% of India's GDP.
  • Over 50% of cyber frauds targeting Indians originated from Southeast Asian countries, including Cambodia, Myanmar, Vietnam, Laos, and Thailand.

Who Is Being Targeted?

Senior citizens and working professionals are the primary targets, but no demographic is immune. Notable victim cases include a Mangaluru woman who lost ₹1.80 crore, a retired Delhi doctor couple who lost roughly ₹15 crore, and a 92-year-old Delhi resident drained of ₹2 crore.

Indian students are also increasingly in the crosshairs. As covered in our ScanMyShadow cybersecurity blog, young professionals seeking internships and jobs are being targeted with hybrid scams that mix fake recruitment with digital arrest threats.

How India Is Fighting Back

  • The 1930 helpline and National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) have helped save ₹7,130 crore across 23 lakh complaints.
  • I4C's broader interdiction has blocked over ₹8,031 crore in fraudulent transfers.
  • In 2025, authorities deactivated 1.2 million SIM cards, froze 1.33 million mule accounts, and recovered ₹5,489 crore.
  • I4C partnered with Microsoft to block over 1,000 Skype IDs used in digital arrest scams.
  • The Union Budget 2025-2026 allocated ₹782 crore for cybersecurity projects.

How to Protect Yourself Right Now

  • Remember: No legitimate Indian agency ever conducts arrests over WhatsApp video or demands money over the phone.
  • Hang up immediately if anyone claiming to be a government official pressures you for money or personal details.
  • Never transfer funds to any "secure government account" — these do not exist.
  • Report immediately via the cybercrime helpline 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in — acting within the first few hours is critical for freezing illicit transactions.
  • Educate elderly family members about how these scams operate — awareness is the single most effective defence.
  • Enable two-factor authentication on all banking and social media accounts.

FAQ: Digital Arrest Scams in India

Q1. What is a digital arrest scam?

A digital arrest scam is a type of cyber fraud where criminals impersonate law enforcement officers and convince victims they are under investigation. Victims are psychologically coerced — often kept on video calls for hours or days — until they transfer money to fraudulent accounts.

Q2. What was Operation Chakra VI?

Operation Chakra VI was a CBI-led nationwide crackdown launched on June 25, 2026. The agency raided over 80 locations across 16 states, targeting the organised syndicate behind hundreds of digital arrest scam cases in India.

Q3. Can Indian police really arrest you over a video call?

No. Indian Law Enforcement Agencies never conduct arrests through video calls. Physical due process is mandatory for any legal action. Any such call is a scam — hang up immediately.

Q4. Who should I call if I have been targeted by a digital arrest scam?

Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930 immediately, or file a complaint online at cybercrime.gov.in. Acting within the first few hours significantly increases the chance of freezing the fraudster's accounts and recovering funds.

Q5. How can I reduce my risk of being targeted by digital scammers?

Keep your personal data exposure to a minimum. Use strong unique passwords, enable two-factor authentication, avoid clicking unknown links, and regularly audit what information about you is available online using tools like ScanMyShadow.
