Five Years of Scam Losses in the US, UK, Canada and Australia: 2021 to 2025

Scan My Shadow · October 7, 2026 · Figures checked against the sources below in October 2026
Scam StatisticsDataTrendsUnited StatesUnited KingdomCanadaAustralia

TL;DR: Between 2021 and 2025, reported losses to the FBI IC3 rose from $6.9 billion to $20.877 billion (about three times). Canada's CAFC total rose from C$379 million to C$704 million. Australia's combined total peaked at A$3.15 billion in 2022 and was about 30% lower in 2025. UK Finance's bank-confirmed fraud total has stayed 3% to 11% below its 2021 level. The countries do not move together.

What the lines show

  • United States: IC3 losses rose every year, to about 3.0 times their 2021 level. The FTC series tells a similar story: $5.8 billion in 2021, $8.8 billion in 2022, more than $10 billion in 2023, $12.5 billion in 2024 and $15.9 billion in 2025.
  • Canada: CAFC losses rose every year (on either 2023 figure cited above), to about 1.9 times 2021. The Competition Bureau says reported losses since 2022 now exceed C$2.4 billion.
  • Australia: combined losses jumped to A$3.15 billion in 2022, fell to A$2.741 billion in 2023 and A$2.03 billion in 2024, then rose 7.8% to A$2.18 billion in 2025. The 2025 total is about 30% below the 2022 peak (the ACCC puts it at 29.7%, measured against a peak of about A$3.1 billion).
  • United Kingdom: UK Finance's total has been broadly flat. APP scams were £583.2 million in 2021, £459.7 million in 2023 and £576.4 million in 2025, as published in each year's report (UK Finance restated 2024).

UK Finance counts only bank-confirmed payment fraud, while Report Fraud, which counts reports on all payment routes, recorded £879.8 million lost to investment fraud alone in 2025. The two measure different things.

Reading these trends carefully

A rise in reported losses does not prove that more fraud is happening. Better reporting tools, public awareness campaigns and bank data-sharing can all raise the count, and Canada's anti-fraud centre estimates that only 5 to 10 per cent of fraud is reported at all. Australia's decline since 2022 is a reported-loss decline, and the National Anti-Scam Centre's own series changed its mix of sources over the period.

For what is behind the US rise, see the FBI IC3 2025 report in numbers; for the other three countries, UK fraud and APP scam numbers for 2025, Canada's 2025 fraud data and Australia's 2025 scam data. Older adults carry a large share of the harm, covered in elder fraud statistics for 2026.

How we compiled this data

US figures are the FBI IC3's annual loss totals as printed in its 2021 to 2025 reports, with 2021 to 2024 cross-checked across later reports. UK figures are UK Finance's annual totals of unauthorised plus APP fraud losses. Canadian figures are CAFC reported losses. Australian figures are the combined National Anti-Scam Centre total.

Index values divide each year by that country's own 2021 value. Nothing is converted between currencies and no values are estimated. Where an agency restated an earlier year, we say so in the table notes rather than silently using the new value.

Some exact values could not be opened in the full PDFs from our publishing environment, so we confirmed them against official press releases or a second official source and rounded where the agency did.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are scams increasing in the US, UK, Canada and Australia?

Reported losses rose in the US and Canada every year from 2021 to 2025, were broadly flat in the UK on UK Finance's bank-confirmed measure, and in Australia fell from their 2022 peak to 2024 and rose again in 2025. Reporting changes make this different from a true crime rate.

Why did Australian scam losses fall after 2022?

The National Anti-Scam Centre's reported total fell from A$3.15 billion in 2022 to A$2.03 billion in 2024, then rose 7.8% to A$2.18 billion in 2025. The data show the pattern but we do not attribute a cause.

How much did the US lose to scams in the last five years?

Adding the FBI IC3's per-year totals gives about $67.2 billion for 2021 to 2025. That is our own sum of the yearly figures, several of which are rounded to $0.1 billion, so treat it as approximate; we could not find a five-year total in the 2025 report.
