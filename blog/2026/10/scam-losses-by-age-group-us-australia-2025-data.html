Scam Losses by Age Group: 2025 Data From the FBI, FTC and Australia’s Scamwatch

By Siddharth Shankar · October 12, 2026 · Figures checked against the sources below in October 2026
Scam StatisticsDataAge Groups

TL;DR: In 2025, people aged 60 and over filed 201,266 complaints with the FBI’s IC3 and reported about $7.7 billion in losses, around 37.1% of the $20.877 billion headline total. In Australia, people aged 65 and over make up about 17.1% of the population but accounted for 26.5% of losses reported to Scamwatch. Official UK and Canadian age breakdowns were not available in the sources we reviewed.

Cryptocurrency losses by age group

IC3’s cryptocurrency section breaks losses down by age too. In every age group over 29, roughly half or more of reported losses involved cryptocurrency.

What the data does not show

The sources we reviewed give no age breakdown for UK Finance’s 2025 fraud figures or for the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre’s 2025 top-10 table, so Canada and the UK are not included here. Each agency measures differently: IC3 counts complaints, the FTC counts reports in its Sentinel database, and Scamwatch counts reports to one of several Australian sources. The numbers are not directly comparable across countries.

How we compiled this data

Age-group complaint counts and losses are the FBI IC3 Internet Crime Report 2025 figures (age table, page 6; crypto losses by age, page 52). The FTC figures are from its Protecting Older Consumers report (calendar 2024, the latest in the sources we reviewed). The Australian figures are from the National Anti-Scam Centre’s Targeting Scams reporting for 2025.

Computed values are labelled with an asterisk and rounded. We did not adjust for different age bands (IC3 uses 60+, Australia 65+, the FTC 60+). We did not estimate unreported losses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which age group loses the most money to scams in the United States?

People aged 60 and over. They filed 201,266 complaints with the FBI’s IC3 in 2025 and reported about $7.7 billion in losses, around 37.1% of the $20.877 billion total.

Do older Australians lose a disproportionate amount?

Yes. People aged 65 and over are about 17.1% of the population but accounted for 26.5% of losses reported to Scamwatch in 2025.

How much of older Americans’ losses involve cryptocurrency?

IC3 reports $4,432,224,488 in cryptocurrency-nexus losses for the 60+ group in 2025, roughly 57% of that group’s reported losses by our calculation.

Why are the UK and Canada missing?

The official sources we reviewed for 2025, UK Finance and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre top-10 page, do not give an age breakdown.
