How Scams Start: 2025 Data on Contact Methods From UK Finance, Scamwatch and the FTC

By Siddharth Shankar · October 12, 2026 · Figures checked against the sources below in October 2026
Scam StatisticsDataContact Methods

TL;DR: Where a scam begins changes how costly it is. UK Finance says online origins were 66% of APP scam cases but 32% of losses, while telecommunications were 17% of cases and 28% of losses. In Australia, Scamwatch phone-call scams averaged about A$36,000 per loss report in 2025, against about A$8,300 for online scams. The FBI’s IC3 and Canada’s CAFC do not publish contact-method counts in the 2025 sources we reviewed.

“How did the scammer reach you?” is one of the most-asked questions in scam statistics, and one of the least consistently answered. This page brings together the 2025 origin and contact-method figures that the UK, Australian and US agencies actually publish.

UK Finance counts “cases” as cards or accounts defrauded, not individual people. Shares do not need to total exactly 100 because of rounding.

United States and Canada

The FTC’s 2025 testimony says text messages were the top contact method by number of reports and that social media contact accounted for over $2 billion in aggregate losses in fiscal year 2025; it gives no loss figures for phone, text, email, website or mail. In the FTC’s 2024 data for older adults, phone-initiated fraud had a median individual loss of $2,210 versus $650 for social media, and social media produced the highest aggregate losses. The FBI’s IC3 report describes common first-contact channels (text messages, social media, advertisements, dating applications) without counts. The CAFC top-10 page gives no contact-method breakdown.

For examples of each route, see a spoofed-number call alert from City of London Police, Ofcom’s rules to block scam texts, Australia’s “Unverified” text label and redirect systems that send users to fraud sites. How we compiled this data

UK figures are from the UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2026 press release (APP origin shares). Australian figures are from the National Anti-Scam Centre Targeting Scams Report 2025, Scamwatch tables (contact method, pages 35 to 36). US figures are from the FTC testimony of 2026 and the FTC Protecting Older Consumers report.

Definitions differ: UK Finance counts bank-confirmed APP cases, Scamwatch counts reports to Scamwatch only (not the combined A$2.18 billion), and FTC figures are consumer reports. We did not convert currencies or compare countries directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which contact method causes the biggest UK scam losses per case?

Telecommunications. UK Finance reports it as 17% of APP cases but 28% of APP losses in 2025, compared with online at 66% of cases and 32% of losses.

How much did Australians lose to phone scams in 2025?

Scamwatch reports A$72.5 million lost across 2,012 reports with a loss, a median loss of A$3,800 and about A$36,000 per loss report by our calculation.

Did scam texts fall in Australia?

Text-message scam reports to Scamwatch fell 62.4%, from 77,365 in 2024 to 29,058 in 2025, though losses rose from A$14.0 million to A$17.9 million.

Why is there no US or Canadian contact-method table?

The official sources we reviewed (FBI IC3 report, FTC testimony, CAFC top-10 page) do not give contact-method counts and losses in a comparable form.
