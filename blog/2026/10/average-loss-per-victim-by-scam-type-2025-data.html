Average Loss per Victim by Scam Type: 2025 Data Computed From FBI IC3, CAFC and UK Finance Figures

By Siddharth Shankar · October 12, 2026 · Figures checked against the sources below in October 2026
Scam StatisticsDataAverage Loss

TL;DR: Totals hide the typical size of a loss. Dividing the FBI IC3’s published 2025 losses by complaints gives about $123,000 per business email compromise complaint and $118,500 per investment fraud complaint, against about $1,100 for phishing. In Canada, the CAFC’s investment fraud losses of C$351 million over 3,867 victims average about C$90,800. These averages are our calculations from published totals, not agency statistics.

Agencies publish totals and counts, not averages, and the average is what a person asking “how much could I lose?” wants. This page computes them, labels them as ours and explains the limits.

Means and medians differ: a median is the middle loss and is far lower than the mean because large losses pull the mean up.

The pattern in the stories: the largest losses come from long-running relationship or business-payment scams. See a UK romance fraud worth £180,000 over four years, a C$3.5 million payment redirection recovery in Quebec and the FTC’s investment training case. Our guides on business email compromise and romance scam statistics give more context, and the free digital footprint check shows what personal data scammers could use against you.

How we compiled this data

IC3 losses and complaints are from the Internet Crime Report 2025 (crime type tables, pages 7 and 8). CAFC figures are from its Top 10 frauds in 2025 page (February 2026). UK Finance figures are from the Annual Fraud Report 2026 press release. Australian and FTC figures are as published by the National Anti-Scam Centre and in FTC testimony.

Every “average” on this page is calculated by us by simple division and is labelled. Agencies define complaints, reports, cases and victims differently, so averages are comparable within a source but not across countries or currencies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which scam type has the highest average loss in the IC3 data?

Business email compromise, at about $123,000 per complaint in 2025 by our calculation ($3,046,598,558 over 24,768 complaints), followed by investment fraud at about $118,500.

What is the average loss in Canada’s investment fraud?

About C$90,800 per victim by our calculation: C$351 million over 3,867 victims in the CAFC 2025 table.

Are these averages official statistics?

No. They are our calculations from published totals and counts, and the page labels each one.

Why does the Australian median differ from the average?

Scamwatch’s median loss is A$400, but large losses pull the mean to about A$11,900 by our calculation. The median describes a typical report, the mean describes total money lost per report.
