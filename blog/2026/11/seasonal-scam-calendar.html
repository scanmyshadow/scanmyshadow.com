Seasonal Scam Calendar: 37 Scams to Watch Through the Year

By Siddharth Shankar · November 24, 2026
Scam CalendarScam Prevention

TL;DR: Scammers follow the calendar. Delivery, gift card and charity scams climb in winter, tax and exam-result scams in spring, travel and back-to-school scams in summer, and festival, election and sale scams in autumn. This page lists 37 of them with when they hit, how they work and how to protect yourself.

Most seasonal scams reuse a normal event as cover: a parcel, a refund, a ticket, an exam result or a sale. Knowing what is coming helps you spot the message before you act on it. Pick your season below, or jump to a scam.

  1. Winter: year-end shopping, New Year and Valentine's Day
  2. Spring: tax season, sport, exams and weddings
  3. Summer: travel, sales and back to school
  4. Autumn: festivals, elections, open enrollment and Black Friday
  5. Year-round life events
  6. Four habits that work in every season

Holiday Package Delivery Scam Surge

When: This scam surges through December as package volume peaks industry-wide and most households are tracking multiple real deliveries at once, making a fake delivery text easy to mistake for a legitimate one.

How it plays out: Texts and emails claim a delivery failed or needs a small redelivery fee, linking to a page designed to steal payment card details rather than any real carrier system.

Protect yourself: Track packages only through the retailer's or carrier's official app or website, and never enter payment information through a link sent in a text message.

Gift Card Scams During the Holidays: How They Work

When: Gift card fraud climbs steadily from late November through New Year's as more people buy, send, and receive them, giving scammers a large pool of plausible targets and cover for unusual requests.

How it plays out: A caller or emailer poses as a utility, government agency, relative in distress, or even a boss, and insists the only way to resolve an urgent problem is to buy gift cards and read the codes over the phone.

Protect yourself: No legitimate organization ever asks to be paid in gift cards, so treat that request alone as confirmation of a scam, and verify any urgent request from a relative or employer through a separate, known contact method.

End-of-Year Charity Donation Scams

When: Charity scam activity rises through November and December, when genuine year-end giving and tax-deduction motivated donations peak and people are fielding more solicitations than usual.

How it plays out: Fraudulent charities use names deliberately similar to well-known organizations and lean on emotional appeals and donation deadlines to rush people into giving before they've verified the organization is real.

Protect yourself: Search for the charity by name plus 'scam' or check it on an independent charity evaluator before donating, and keep a receipt for any legitimate year-end gift.

Deeper guide: Holiday and E-Commerce Shopping Scams: A Seasonal Guide

Pre-Holiday Package Theft and Porch Piracy

When: Package theft rises through November and December as online order volume peaks and more parcels sit on porches for longer during the busiest delivery weeks of the year.

How it plays out: This is primarily a physical theft pattern rather than a phishing one: thieves follow delivery trucks or simply watch porches in a neighborhood, grabbing packages shortly after they're dropped off.

Protect yourself: Require a signature or use a delivery locker for higher-value items, and consider a doorbell camera or having packages held at a pickup location during the highest-risk weeks.

New Year, New Identity Theft Resolutions Checklist

When: Early January is a natural moment for this kind of housekeeping, since people are already reviewing the past year's finances and setting habits for the one ahead.

How it plays out: This isn't a scam pattern so much as a seasonal prompt: the risks that built up over the previous year — reused passwords, old data breaches, dormant accounts — tend to go unaddressed without a specific trigger to review them.

Protect yourself: Pull your free annual credit reports, update passwords on financial accounts, and check whether your email or phone number has appeared in any known data breach.

New Year Gym Membership and Subscription Scams, Including India

When: The scam surge lands in the first two to three weeks of January, when resolution-driven sign-ups for gyms and subscription apps are at their yearly high and people are less careful reading terms.

How it plays out: Ads for gym memberships, meal plans, and fitness apps advertise a deep first-month discount but bury an auto-renewing annual contract in the fine print, along with a cancellation process designed to be difficult to complete.

Protect yourself: Read the cancellation policy before signing up for any January promotion, and set a calendar reminder before any free trial or discounted period ends.

January Gym and Fitness App Data Privacy

When: January's surge in new fitness app sign-ups, driven by New Year's resolutions, is when people are least likely to read a privacy policy carefully before granting permissions.

How it plays out: This isn't a classic scam so much as a privacy exposure pattern: fitness apps often request broad access to location, health, and contact data, and some share or sell that data more widely than users realize.

Protect yourself: Review an app's data-sharing settings before granting location or health permissions, and limit access to only what the core features actually require.

Valentine's Day Romance Scams

When: Romance scam activity climbs in the weeks before Valentine's Day, when dating app usage rises and people already in online relationships are emotionally primed for a request tied to the holiday.

How it plays out: A scammer who has spent weeks or months building a relationship online asks for money for a plane ticket, a gift, or an emergency right around the holiday, often after avoiding any video call or in-person meeting.

Protect yourself: Never send money, gift cards, or cryptocurrency to someone you haven't met in person, and treat a refusal to video chat as a serious warning sign regardless of how long you've been talking.

Super Bowl Ticket and Betting Scams

When: Scam activity spikes in the two weeks before the Super Bowl, when demand for tickets and interest in sports betting apps both peak sharply around a single fixed date.

How it plays out: Fake ticket sellers advertise seats that don't exist on social media and classifieds, while fraudulent betting-app clones and prop-bet websites collect deposits or personal information without ever paying out winnings.

Protect yourself: Buy tickets only through official team or verified resale platforms with buyer guarantees, and only use betting apps licensed in your state or region.

Republic Day and Independence Day Themed Scams

When: Scam activity ticks up around Republic Day (Jan 26) and Independence Day (Aug 15) each year.

How it plays out: Fake 'government scheme' messages claiming a special holiday cashback or subsidy circulate on WhatsApp, directing users to a phishing page that asks for Aadhaar and bank details, while fraudulent flag or merchandise sellers take payment and never ship.

Protect yourself: Check any government scheme announcement only on the relevant ministry's official website, and be skeptical of any 'special holiday' cashback that requires entering bank details to claim.

Spring: tax season, sport, exams and weddings

March to May.

March Madness Betting App Scams

When: Scam activity clusters tightly around the college basketball tournament each March, when betting interest and app downloads spike sharply among people who don't normally use betting platforms.

How it plays out: Fraudulent betting apps and bracket-contest sites mimic legitimate platforms to collect deposits or personal information, and never pay out winnings once the tournament ends.

Protect yourself: Use only betting platforms licensed in your state or region, and avoid apps or sites promoted solely through unsolicited social media ads or messages.

Spring Break Travel Booking Scams

When: The scam window opens in the weeks leading into spring break, typically March, when students and families are booking trips fast and often shopping by price alone.

How it plays out: Scammers post fake all-inclusive package deals and rental listings priced well below market, then either demand payment through untraceable methods or vanish once the deposit clears.

Protect yourself: Book travel packages through established agencies or platforms with cancellation protection, and cross-check any deal that looks too good against the property's independent reviews.

IPL Betting App Scams

When: IPL season runs roughly April–May, when cricket interest and betting-related traffic surge across India.

How it plays out: Illegal betting apps and fantasy-sports clones advertise heavily on social media promising guaranteed winnings, collecting deposits through personal UPI IDs that are never paid out, while some apps harvest banking details under the guise of KYC.

Protect yourself: Remember betting on these apps is illegal in most Indian states and offers no legal recourse if funds are lost, and never share banking OTPs with any app claiming to need them for 'winnings verification'.

Tax Season Scams: What to Watch For in 2026

When: Tax season runs roughly from late January through the mid-April filing deadline, and scammers ramp up right alongside it because millions of people are actively expecting communication from tax authorities and waiting on refunds.

How it plays out: Fraudsters send phishing emails and texts posing as the tax agency, claiming a problem with your return or an unexpected refund, and direct you to a fake login page designed to harvest your Social Security number and banking details.

Protect yourself: Remember that tax agencies typically initiate contact by mail, not email, text, or phone, and never click links in unsolicited tax-related messages — go directly to the official site instead.

Income Tax Filing Season Scams (India)

When: Tax filing season builds toward the July deadline, when taxpayers expect communication from the department.

How it plays out: Fraudsters send SMS and email phishing links claiming a refund is pending or a return needs urgent correction, directing users to a fake e-filing login page designed to steal PAN, Aadhaar, and banking details.

Protect yourself: The Income Tax Department never asks for banking details or an OTP by SMS or email — file and check refund status only through the official incometax.gov.in portal.

Mother's Day and Father's Day Gift Scams

When: Scam listings spike in the two to three weeks before Mother's Day and Father's Day, when last-minute shoppers are searching for gifts and more willing to try an unfamiliar online store.

How it plays out: Fake gift shops advertise personalized or handmade items at attractive prices through social media ads, taking payment for products that never ship or arrive as cheap substitutes.

Protect yourself: Check an unfamiliar shop's reviews and return policy before buying, and pay with a credit card so you have recourse if the order never arrives.

Graduation Season Scams Targeting Grads and Parents

When: Scam activity builds through May and June as graduation ceremonies happen nationwide and both new graduates and their families are more publicly identifiable and reachable online than usual.

How it plays out: Fraudsters pose as employers with too-good-to-be-true job offers that require an upfront 'training fee,' or send fake congratulatory messages with malicious links timed to when a graduate is actively job hunting.

Protect yourself: Treat any job offer that asks for money upfront as a scam, and verify a company's legitimacy independently before providing personal or banking information during a job application.

Exam Result Season Phishing Scams

When: Phishing spikes around board and entrance exam result dates, roughly May through July each year.

How it plays out: Fake result-checking websites and SMS links mimic official exam boards, asking for personal details or roll numbers to 'unlock' results, while some prompt a fee for 'priority' rechecking or certificate delivery that doesn't exist.

Protect yourself: Check results only on the official board website, such as the CBSE or respective state board portal, and never pay a fee through a link claiming to speed up a result or certificate.

Wedding Season Scams: Registry and Vendor Fraud

When: Peak wedding planning and booking activity runs roughly from late spring through early fall, when couples are managing large budgets, dealing with unfamiliar vendors, and often making decisions on tight timelines.

How it plays out: Scammers set up fake venue, photography, or catering listings that require a deposit to hold the date, or intercept vendor email threads to send a spoofed invoice with different payment details right before a deposit is due.

Protect yourself: Verify any vendor's business registration and reviews before sending a deposit, and always confirm payment details by phone using a number you looked up independently, not one in the email itself.

Wedding Season Scams in India

When: Indian wedding season runs roughly October–February, plus April–June, when families manage big budgets.

How it plays out: Fake venue, catering, and decor listings collect a booking advance and vanish, while scammers impersonating a genuine vendor send a spoofed message with 'updated' bank details right before a payment is due.

Protect yourself: Verify any vendor's registration and reviews before paying an advance, and always confirm payment details by phone using a number looked up independently, not the one in a message.

Summer: travel, sales and back to school

June to August.

Summer Travel Scams to Watch For Memorial Day and Labor Day Sale Scams Prime Day and Flash Sale Scams Monsoon Insurance Claim Scams Back-to-School Scams Targeting Parents Back-to-School Laptop and Device Scams End-of-Summer College Move-In Scams

Summer Travel Scams to Watch For

When: Summer's high travel season, roughly June through August, draws scammers because so many people are booking flights, hotels, and rentals at once and are eager to lock in a good price before it's gone.

How it plays out: Fraudulent listings for vacation rentals and heavily discounted flight or hotel packages appear on social media and classified sites, asking for payment outside the platform's secure checkout where there's no buyer protection.

Protect yourself: Book through reputable platforms that offer payment protection, and be wary of any host or agent who pushes you to pay by bank transfer or app outside the booking site.

Memorial Day and Labor Day Sale Scams

When: These long-weekend sales in late May and early September draw scam activity because shoppers expect genuinely large discounts, making an inflated fake markdown harder to spot.

How it plays out: Fake retail sites and social ads advertise steep discounts on furniture, appliances, and electronics tied to the holiday weekend, collecting payment for orders that never ship.

Protect yourself: Verify a retailer's legitimacy before buying from an unfamiliar site during a sale event, and be cautious of ads for brands you've never seen advertised outside the sale period.

Prime Day and Flash Sale Scams

When: Scam activity clusters tightly around major flash-sale events, which now happen multiple times a year, because the compressed time window pushes shoppers to decide fast and skip normal checks.

How it plays out: Fake storefronts and phishing emails mimic the retailer's branding almost exactly, advertising doorbuster deals on high-demand items that either don't exist or are used to harvest login and card details.

Protect yourself: Shop by typing the retailer's URL directly rather than clicking a promotional link, and enable purchase notifications so you'd notice a charge you didn't authorize.

Monsoon Insurance Claim Scams

When: Monsoon season, roughly June–September, brings a wave of flood-damage insurance claims scammers exploit.

How it plays out: Fake 'claim assistance' agents contact flood-affected policyholders offering to speed up a payout for an upfront fee, and phishing messages impersonating insurers ask for policy and bank details to 'process' a claim faster.

Protect yourself: File and track claims only through your insurer's official app, website, or branch, and remember a genuine insurer never charges a fee to process a claim faster.

Back-to-School Scams Targeting Parents

When: The lead-up to the new school year, typically August into early September, brings a spike in scams as parents shop for supplies, register for classes, and search for scholarships or financial aid under time pressure.

How it plays out: Fake supply and electronics retailers advertise steep discounts on backpacks and laptops, while bogus scholarship and financial-aid services charge upfront fees for applications that are actually free through the school or government.

Protect yourself: Buy school supplies from established retailers, and remember that legitimate scholarships and federal financial aid never require an upfront payment to apply or to guarantee an award.

Back-to-School Laptop and Device Scams

When: This scam wave overlaps with general back-to-school shopping in August, when demand for laptops and tablets for the new school year is at its yearly peak.

How it plays out: Fake electronics retailers and marketplace listings advertise name-brand laptops at deeply discounted prices, taking payment for devices that never arrive or arrive as counterfeit, non-functional units.

Protect yourself: Buy devices from authorized retailers or the manufacturer directly, and be wary of marketplace listings priced far below the device's typical retail price.

End-of-Summer College Move-In Scams

When: This pattern clusters in August and early September, when college move-in dates create a short window of high demand for dorm supplies, storage rentals, and short-term sublets.

How it plays out: Fake storage and sublet listings target students and parents searching last-minute for options near campus, collecting a deposit for a unit or room that doesn't exist or was never theirs to offer.

Protect yourself: Book storage and housing through platforms with verified listings and buyer protection, and never send a deposit for a unit you or your student haven't seen in person or on a verified video call.

Autumn: festivals, elections, open enrollment and Black Friday

September to November.

Election Season Political Donation Scams Open Enrollment Health Insurance Scams Diwali Online Shopping Scams Festive Season Delivery and Courier Scams Cyber Week Fake Retailer Website Scams

Election Season Political Donation Scams

When: Political donation scams rise in the months leading up to a major election, when legitimate fundraising volume is high and people are used to receiving frequent, urgent-sounding solicitations.

How it plays out: Fraudulent fundraising sites and texts mimic real campaign branding, urging an immediate small donation through a payment page that has no actual connection to the campaign or party named.

Protect yourself: Donate only through a campaign's official website, and be skeptical of any donation request that pressures you to act within minutes.

Open Enrollment Health Insurance Scams

When: Open enrollment periods, typically in the fall, bring a wave of scams because people are actively comparing health plans and are used to receiving unsolicited calls and mailers about coverage during this window.

How it plays out: Callers posing as insurance agents pressure people into disclosing Medicare or Social Security numbers to 'verify eligibility' for a plan, or sign them up for a policy they never agreed to in order to collect a commission.

Protect yourself: Only enroll through the official marketplace website or a licensed agent you contacted first, and never give personal identifiers to someone who called you unprompted.

Diwali Online Shopping Scams

When: Diwali is India's biggest online shopping season, with major sales running through October and November.

How it plays out: Fake deal sites and social media ads mimic popular retailers with prices far below the real sale price, taking payment and shipping nothing or a counterfeit. Fraudulent courier and 'delivery failed' SMS links sent around the same window try to phish payment details under the guise of a redelivery fee.

Protect yourself: Shop only through official retailer apps and websites, pay through the platform's own checkout rather than a link in a message, and treat any 'redelivery fee' SMS as a scam.

Festive Season Delivery and Courier Scams

When: Courier scam activity climbs through the Diwali and year-end festive season, roughly October–December.

How it plays out: Fake 'delivery failed' or 'customs duty pending' SMS messages link to phishing pages asking for a small payment and full card details, while fraudulent calls claim a parcel is being held and needs urgent 'verification' payment.

Protect yourself: Track deliveries only through the official courier app or website, and never pay a 'customs' or 'redelivery' fee through a link sent by SMS.

Cyber Week Fake Retailer Website Scams

When: Cyber Week, the stretch from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday, draws a concentrated wave of fake retailer sites because online shopping volume and ad spending both peak at the same time.

How it plays out: Scammers clone the look of well-known retail sites almost exactly, buying ads that lead to a fake checkout page built purely to capture card numbers with no product ever shipped.

Protect yourself: Navigate to retailers by typing the URL directly rather than clicking an ad, and check for a legitimate return address and customer service line before entering payment details on an unfamiliar site.

Year-round life events

Scams that follow a life event or a news event, not the calendar.

Home Buying Season Scams (Spring/Summer) Moving Season Scams: Fake Movers and Rental Fraud End-of-Lease Moving Scams Concert and Festival Ticket Scams Hurricane and Natural Disaster Relief Scams

Home Buying Season Scams (Spring/Summer)

When: The busiest home-buying months are spring and summer, when transaction volume is highest and buyers are juggling multiple large wire transfers in a compressed closing timeline.

How it plays out: Scammers monitor real estate email threads and send a spoofed message right before closing, impersonating the title company or agent with new wiring instructions that redirect the down payment to a fraudulent account.

Protect yourself: Always confirm wiring instructions by phone using a number from the title company's official website, never one from an email, and be suspicious of any last-minute change to payment details.

Moving Season Scams: Fake Movers and Rental Fraud

When: The busiest window for residential moves is late spring through summer, when leases turn over and families relocate around the school calendar, creating a surge of people urgently comparing movers and rentals online.

How it plays out: Rogue moving companies quote a low price, then hold belongings hostage for a much higher fee once loaded onto the truck, while rental scammers list apartments that don't exist or aren't theirs to rent, collecting a deposit before vanishing.

Protect yourself: Check a mover's registration with the relevant transportation authority before booking, and never wire a rental deposit or pay in gift cards before viewing the unit in person or via a live video walkthrough.

End-of-Lease Moving Scams

When: This pattern peaks around common lease-end dates, often the end of spring or summer, when a large share of renters are searching for a new place on a fixed deadline.

How it plays out: Scammers list apartments that don't exist or that belong to someone else, using photos taken from legitimate listings and pressuring applicants to send a deposit before viewing the unit to 'secure' it against other renters.

Protect yourself: Never send a deposit before viewing a unit in person or via a live video call with the actual property, and verify the listing against the property management company directly.

Concert and Festival Ticket Scams

When: Scam listings surge in the days after major tour or festival dates go on sale, when demand outstrips supply and fans are scrambling on resale sites and social media for sold-out shows.

How it plays out: Scammers post tickets for events they don't have, often using screenshots of real tickets, and take payment through apps with no buyer protection before the buyer discovers the ticket was never valid.

Protect yourself: Buy only through the official box office or a verified resale marketplace with a guarantee, and never pay with a friends-and-family transfer for a ticket from someone you don't know.

Hurricane and Natural Disaster Relief Scams

When: Fake charity and relief scams typically spike in the 24 to 72 hours after a hurricane, wildfire, or other natural disaster is declared, when public attention and donation activity are at their highest and verification is hardest to do quickly.

How it plays out: Scammers register charity-sounding names, run social media ads with dramatic imagery, and solicit donations by phone or text, funneling money to accounts that have no connection to actual relief efforts.

Protect yourself: Donate directly through a charity's official website rather than a link in a text or social post, and check the organization's standing with an independent charity evaluator before giving.

Four habits that work in every season

  1. Go to the source yourself. Open the retailer, bank or government app directly instead of tapping a link in a message.
  2. Be slow when a deadline is the pressure. "Today only", "last chance" and "act now" are the usual hooks.
  3. Pay in ways that can be reversed. Gift cards, wire transfers and crypto cannot be taken back; cards and regulated payment apps often can.
  4. Report quickly. In India call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in; elsewhere tell your bank first, then your national fraud reporting service.
