UK: Action Fraud Reporting and the APP Fraud Reimbursement Rules Explained

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: If you've been scammed in the UK, report to Action Fraud (actionfraud.police.uk). For bank transfer scams specifically, the UK has a mandatory reimbursement scheme, regulated by the Payment Systems Regulator, that replaced the older voluntary CRM Code in October 2024 — UK banks must generally reimburse eligible authorised push payment (APP) fraud claims within five business days, up to £85,000.

If you're researching UK scam recovery, you may come across references to the "CRM Code" — it's important to know that this was replaced by a stronger, mandatory scheme in October 2024, so the rules have changed since many older articles were written.

Step 1: Report to Action Fraud

Action Fraud (actionfraud.police.uk) is the UK's national reporting centre for fraud and cybercrime. Reporting here is separate from, and in addition to, contacting your bank — it feeds into national crime data used by the National Fraud Intelligence Bureau to identify patterns and repeat offenders.

What changed: from the CRM Code to mandatory reimbursement

Until October 2024, UK banks that voluntarily signed up to the Contingent Reimbursement Model (CRM) Code committed to reimbursing victims of authorised push payment (APP) fraud under certain conditions — but it was voluntary, and not every bank participated, and payouts weren't always consistent.

On 7 October 2024, the CRM Code was retired and replaced by a statutory reimbursement scheme regulated by the Payment Systems Regulator (PSR). Under the new rules:

  • Reimbursement is now mandatory for eligible APP fraud claims, not just for banks that opted in.
  • Banks must generally reimburse victims within five business days of receiving a valid claim.
  • Reimbursement is capped at £85,000 per claim, in line with the Financial Services Compensation Scheme limit.
  • Claims must generally be submitted within 13 months of the last related payment.
  • The cost is shared between the sending and receiving payment provider, rather than falling solely on the sending bank as under the old code.

What can still get a claim declined

Reimbursement isn't automatic in every case. Claims can be refused where the customer is found to have acted fraudulently themselves, or with what the scheme defines as gross negligence — for example, ignoring clear, specific warnings from your bank during the transaction. If your claim is declined and you disagree, the Financial Ombudsman Service can review individual disputes.

What to do practically

  • Contact your bank's fraud team immediately if money has just been sent — before filing anything else, since speed still affects whether funds can be intercepted before withdrawal.
  • File your Action Fraud report as soon as possible afterward.
  • If your bank declines reimbursement and you believe the decision is wrong, escalate to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the CRM Code still in effect in the UK?

No. The CRM Code was retired on 7 October 2024 and replaced by a mandatory, statutory reimbursement scheme regulated by the Payment Systems Regulator (PSR), which applies more broadly than the old voluntary code did.

How much can I be reimbursed for under the new UK scheme?

Up to £85,000 per claim, matching the Financial Services Compensation Scheme limit, and banks must generally respond within five business days of a valid claim.

What is Action Fraud, and is reporting there the same as filing a bank claim?

Action Fraud is the UK's national fraud and cybercrime reporting centre — reporting there is separate from your bank reimbursement claim and feeds into national crime intelligence, so you should do both.

Can my reimbursement claim be refused?

Yes, in specific cases — such as first-party fraud or if you're found to have ignored clear, specific fraud warnings from your bank during the transaction (defined as gross negligence under the scheme).

What if my bank declines my claim and I disagree?

You can escalate the dispute to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which reviews individual cases independently of the bank's decision.
