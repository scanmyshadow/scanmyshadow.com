Loan App Harassment: What to Do About Recovery Agent Blackmail

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: RBI's directions, issued August 6, 2026 and effective January 1, 2027, explicitly ban recovery agents from harassing borrowers or contacting their relatives, friends, or co-workers to pressure repayment. If a loan app is doing this now, it's already against RBI norms — here's how to report it and what to do about it.

Unregulated instant-loan apps have built a well-documented pattern: request excessive phone permissions at install, extract a small loan, then use contact-list access and threats to pressure repayment — often contacting people who have nothing to do with the loan.

What RBI's rules actually say

RBI issued formal directions on 6 August 2026 (effective 1 January 2027, though harassment has always violated existing RBI norms) that explicitly prohibit:

  • Threatening, abusive, or intimidating language toward a borrower or guarantor.
  • Anonymous or excessive calls, or contact outside permitted hours (recovery calls and visits are restricted to 8am–7pm).
  • Contacting or harassing a borrower's relatives, friends, guarantor, or co-workers to pressure repayment — this is explicitly barred.
  • Public humiliation or threats to a borrower's reputation, assets, or family.
  • Field visits without at least one day's prior notice, and agents must carry identification and a bank authorization letter.

Is this app even legal?

Only banks, NBFCs, and entities regulated by state governments under statutory provisions are permitted to offer lending in India. Many apps behind harassment complaints aren't regulated at all — check whether the app discloses its partner bank or NBFC upfront, which is now mandated. If it doesn't, that's a significant red flag on its own.

What to do if you're being harassed

  • Don't engage with threats. Document everything — screenshots of messages, call logs, and any threats made — rather than responding directly.
  • Complain to the bank or NBFC the app claims to partner with, if disclosed. Ask specifically about their grievance redressal process.
  • Escalate to the RBI Ombudsman via the Complaint Management System (CMS) at cms.rbi.org.in if the lender doesn't resolve it.
  • Report unauthorized apps to the RBI directly — unregulated lending is a statutory violation, separate from any harassment complaint.
  • File a police complaint if threats escalate to intimidation of family, workplace contact, or anything resembling extortion — this crosses from a lending dispute into a criminal matter.

Securing your phone

Loan apps that gathered contact-list, SMS, or gallery access during install can misuse that data well beyond the loan itself. Review and revoke unnecessary app permissions, and if the app accessed your contacts, assume those numbers may already be exposed — worth checking with a Scan My Shadow report what's tied to your own number and email.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal for a loan app to contact my family about my debt?

No. RBI's directions explicitly bar recovery agents from contacting or harassing a borrower's relatives, friends, guarantor, or co-workers to pressure repayment — this applies to all regulated lenders.

What hours are recovery calls actually allowed?

RBI's framework restricts recovery calls and field visits to between 8am and 7pm, with at least one day's notice required before an in-person visit.

Where do I complain if a bank or NBFC won't address harassment?

Escalate through the RBI Ombudsman via the Complaint Management System at cms.rbi.org.in if the lender's own grievance process doesn't resolve it.

What if the loan app isn't linked to any real bank or NBFC?

Only banks, NBFCs, and state-regulated entities can legally offer lending in India — an app that won't disclose its partner institution may be operating outside RBI regulation entirely, which is worth reporting separately.

Can a loan app block my phone if I miss a payment?

For personal, car, or home loan defaults, no — RBI's rules prohibit disabling a borrower's phone. Device-blocking is only permitted in narrow, tightly regulated cases where the loan specifically financed the device itself.
