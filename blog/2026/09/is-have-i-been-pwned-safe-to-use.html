Is Have I Been Pwned Safe to Use? Email Privacy Explained

Scan My ShadowSeptember 10, 20264 min read

TL;DR: Have I Been Pwned says it does not store your personal information when you conduct a normal email or phone-number search. It also explains its encryption, operational logging and the distinction between public searches and verified features. No online service should be treated as risk-free, but HIBP publishes detailed privacy information about its search.

Why people hesitate to search

Typing your email into a breach checker can feel backwards: if you are worried that your address is exposed, why give it to another website? That is a reasonable privacy question.

HIBP addresses this directly in its privacy policy. It says that when you conduct a normal search, it does not store the personal information you searched for as a new search record.

What HIBP says about normal searches

HIBP states that searches for an email address or phone number retrieve data from its existing storage and return the result; the searched personal information is not explicitly stored as a new record from that search.

The service also says searches are performed over encrypted connections. Like other services, it describes operational logging and anti-abuse measures, so privacy should be understood as a documented system rather than an absolute promise of invisibility.

Public search is not the same as sensitive breach access

Some breach information is treated as sensitive and is not returned through ordinary public searches. HIBP says verified users can access certain sensitive-breach information through its account features.

Seeing a normal breach result therefore does not mean HIBP exposes every piece of information associated with a breach.

What you should still avoid

Do not give a breach-checker your account password simply because you are checking an email address. Email-breach lookup and password checking are separate tasks.

HIBP's Pwned Passwords feature uses k-anonymity so the full password is not sent to the service. That privacy model is different from simply typing a password into an ordinary web form.

How to evaluate any breach checker

Ask four questions: What information does the service need? What does it store? What does it return? Is its privacy policy clear?

HIBP publishes detailed answers to those questions, making its approach easier to evaluate than an unknown breach-checking site with no clear data policy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does HIBP store the email I search?

HIBP says it does not explicitly store personal information from a normal email or phone-number search as a new search record.

Is HIBP encrypted?

HIBP says searches are performed over encrypted connections and describes operational logging used to keep the service running and prevent abuse.

Can HIBP show sensitive breaches publicly?

Some sensitive breach information is restricted and requires verified access rather than ordinary public search.

Should I enter my password into HIBP's email search?

No. Email-breach searches and password checking are separate functions.

Where can I read HIBP's privacy policy?

HIBP publishes its current privacy policy on its website and explains its search, storage, logging and notification practices there.
