Wire Fraud Recovery in the US: The 72-Hour Bank Recall Window

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: Wire fraud recovery in the US depends heavily on speed: call your bank immediately to request a wire recall, then file with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) within 72 hours to trigger the Recovery Asset Team's Financial Fraud Kill Chain. Recovery rates drop sharply after that window.

If you've just wired money to someone who turned out to be a scammer, the next few hours matter more than anything else in this recovery process.

Step 1: Call your bank's fraud department directly

Not general customer service — ask specifically for the wire transfer or fraud department, and request a wire recall (a SWIFT or Fedwire recall request). Minutes matter: scammers typically try to move funds out of the receiving account as fast as possible.

Step 2: File with the FBI's IC3 immediately

Go to ic3.gov and file a report. This is what activates the FBI's Recovery Asset Team (RAT), which runs a process called the Financial Fraud Kill Chain (FFKC) — contacting the receiving bank directly to request a freeze on the transferred funds before they're released to the scammer.

Reported recovery rates for cases filed within 72 hours have ranged from roughly 58% to 70% in different reporting periods — after that window, recovery odds drop sharply as funds get split across accounts or moved internationally.

What the Kill Chain process typically requires

The formal FFKC process has historically been most reliably used for larger transfers routed through US correspondent banks, though victims of smaller amounts should still file with IC3 — every report contributes to the broader investigation even outside the formal kill-chain threshold.

If the wire already crossed international borders

Recovery becomes significantly harder once funds move outside domestic banking rails or get converted to cryptocurrency. This doesn't mean reporting is pointless — the case still matters for identifying repeat-offending accounts — but expect the process to shift from "get it back quickly" toward the standard investigation timeline.

Re-securing after the fact

Once you've filed both reports, change your online banking password, and monitor your account closely for any secondary attempts — scammers who successfully pull off one wire fraud often try a follow-up request. If the scam originated through a fake invoice or "your bank changed today" message, check your email account for signs of compromise too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I actually have to recover a fraudulent wire transfer?

The most commonly cited window is 72 hours from when the wire was sent, though acting within the first few hours gives meaningfully better odds. After 72 hours, recovery rates drop significantly.

What is the FBI's Recovery Asset Team?

A unit within the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) that runs the Financial Fraud Kill Chain — a process that contacts the receiving bank directly to request a freeze on fraudulently wired funds before they're withdrawn.

Do I need to file with IC3 even for a smaller amount?

Yes — while the formal Financial Fraud Kill Chain has historically focused on larger transfers, filing with IC3 matters regardless of amount, since it feeds into the broader fraud investigation.

What should I say when I call my bank?

Ask specifically for the wire transfer or fraud department, explain that you were the victim of fraud, and request an immediate wire recall — a general customer service representative typically can't process this.

Is recovery still possible if the wire already left the country?

It's significantly harder once funds cross international borders or convert to cryptocurrency, but reporting is still worthwhile for the ongoing investigation even if quick recovery isn't realistic.

Can a wire transfer be recalled?

Sometimes. A wire recall is a request your bank sends to the receiving bank asking for the money back, not an automatic reversal. Wire transfers are generally treated as final once they're credited, so the receiving bank has to agree to return the funds, and it can only do that if the money is still in the account. Reporting the fraud to your bank as soon as you notice gives the request its best chance.

How long does a wire recall take?

There's no fixed timeline. Your bank can usually start the recall request the same day you report the fraud, but the receiving bank decides whether and when to respond, and that can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. What you control is speed: a request sent before the funds are withdrawn has far better odds than one sent after the 72-hour window.
