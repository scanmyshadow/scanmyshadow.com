How US Data Broker Sites Expose Your Phone Number and Email — and How to Opt Out

Scan My Shadow · September 2, 2026

TL;DR: US data broker sites compile public records, purchase history, and old accounts into searchable profiles — often including your phone number, email, and family members. Removal is possible but requires going site by site.

👉 Check your digital footprint free in 30 seconds →

If you've ever searched your own name and found a profile listing your age, past addresses, relatives, and phone number, you've run into a data broker. These sites aren't hacking anyone — they're aggregating information that's already technically public and packaging it into a single, searchable page.

Where the data actually comes from

  • Public records. Property deeds, voter registrations, and court records are legally public in most US states.
  • Marketing and purchase data. Loyalty programs, warranty registrations, and online purchases get sold to data aggregators as a routine part of many companies' business models.
  • Old breaches. Emails and phone numbers from past data breaches often end up folded into broker profiles alongside legitimately public information.

The result is a profile that feels more invasive than any single source alone — because it combines several thin layers of exposure into one page.

Why this matters beyond mild embarrassment

Broker profiles are a common resource for scammers building a convincing pretext before a call — a name, a relative's name, and a past address are often enough to make a scam call sound credible. They're also used for harassment and stalking in more serious cases, which is why several US states have started legislating opt-out rights specifically for these sites.

How to actually get removed

There's no single master opt-out. Each broker runs its own removal process, usually requiring you to find your specific profile and submit a request through their form. It typically takes anywhere from a few days to a few weeks per site, and profiles can reappear over time as brokers refresh their data.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal for these sites to list my information?

In most cases, yes — they're aggregating information that's already publicly available through records, marketing data, or other public sources, which is why removal rather than legal action is usually the practical path.

How long does opt-out usually take?

It varies by site, from a few days to a few weeks, and some brokers require periodic re-submission since profiles can regenerate.

Are there paid services that remove me from all broker sites at once?

Some exist and can save time, but they vary in coverage and thoroughness — it's worth checking what specific sites a service actually covers before paying for it.

Can I stop new data from being collected in the future?

Not entirely, since it draws from public records and purchase data, but opting out of marketing data sharing where possible does reduce future collection.

Does removing myself from broker sites stop breach-related exposure too?

No — those are separate issues. Broker opt-outs address aggregated public profiles, while breach exposure needs to be checked separately.
