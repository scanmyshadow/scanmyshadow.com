Google Account Hacked? What to Do Now

Scan My ShadowSeptember 15, 20266 min read

TL;DR: If your Google account has been hacked or you're locked out, Google's own recovery tool at g.co/recover is the fastest path back in. Here's the exact process, what to check once you're back in, and how to lock it down so it doesn't happen again.

👉 See if your data's already leaked — free 30-second check →

A hacked Google account is more disruptive than most — it's usually the recovery email for everything else, the login for Android, Drive, Photos, and often Gmail itself. If you're seeing unfamiliar logins, a changed password, or you're locked out entirely, here's what actually works.

Signs Your Google Account Has Been Hacked

  • You're logged out and your password no longer works.
  • Emails in your Gmail "Sent" folder that you didn't send.
  • A "new sign-in" alert from a device or location you don't recognize.
  • Your recovery phone number or email has been changed without your action.
  • Unfamiliar apps listed under connected third-party access.

Step 1: Start Google's Account Recovery

Go directly to g.co/recover (typed manually, not clicked from an email) and follow the prompts. Google will ask a series of questions — your last known password, recovery email or phone, and approximate account creation date all help. Answer as accurately as you can; partial answers still help Google's system build confidence it's really you.

Step 2: If You Still Have Access

If you can still log in but suspect something's wrong, go straight to myaccount.google.com/security and check:

  • Recent security activity — review every listed device and sign-in location; sign out anything unfamiliar.
  • Recovery phone and email — confirm both are still yours.
  • Third-party app access — revoke anything you don't recognize or no longer use.
  • Password — change it immediately, and don't reuse a password from any other account.

Step 3: Turn On 2-Step Verification

If it wasn't already on, enable 2-Step Verification under the Security tab — ideally with an authenticator app rather than SMS, which is more resistant to SIM-swap attacks. See Setting Up Passkeys and Authenticator Apps for the exact setup.

If Recovery Doesn't Work the First Time

Google's automated recovery can take a few attempts, and sometimes a short waiting period, before it succeeds — this is a deliberate security measure, not a sign you're doing something wrong. Keep retrying g.co/recover with whatever details you can confidently supply, and avoid creating a new account in the meantime, which can complicate recovery of the original.

Once You're Back In

A hacked Google account is often a sign your email address is more broadly exposed — the same credentials get reused or traded after a breach elsewhere. If you're dealing with this, it's worth checking what to do after any email account is hacked, and considering whether other accounts using the same password need attention too.

FAQs

What is g.co/recover?

It's Google's official account recovery tool — the fastest, most direct path back into a locked or hacked Google account.

Why does Google keep saying it can't verify it's me?

Google's recovery system weighs how many details you can accurately confirm; the more you can supply — last password, recovery email/phone, approximate creation date — the higher the chance of success.

Should I create a new Google account if I can't recover the old one?

Try recovery a few more times first — creating a new account doesn't help regain the old one and can complicate matters if the old account is later recovered.

Is SMS-based 2-Step Verification enough?

It's better than nothing, but an authenticator app is more resistant to SIM-swap attacks — worth upgrading once you're back in.

How do I know if my Google account being hacked means my data was breached elsewhere?

Not necessarily, but it's common for hacked accounts to trace back to a reused password from an earlier breach — worth checking your broader exposure.
