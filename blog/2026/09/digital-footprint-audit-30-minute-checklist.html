Digital Footprint Audit: A 30-Minute Checklist to Run on Yourself Once a Year

Scan My Shadow · September 2, 2026

TL;DR: Most digital footprint advice is scattered across dozens of separate articles. This is the condensed version — a single 30-minute checklist worth running once a year to catch what's changed.

Digital footprint advice tends to live in a dozen separate places — one article about breach checking, another about social media settings, another about data broker opt-outs. This is the condensed version: one sitting, roughly 30 minutes, covering what actually matters most.

Minutes 0-10: Check what's already exposed

  • Run your phone number and email against a breach and public-record check — a Scan My Shadow report covers 1,500+ sources in one pass, which is faster than checking each source manually.
  • Search your own name in a private browser window and note what shows up on the first page.

Minutes 10-18: Review account security

  • Confirm two-factor authentication is active on your email, banking, and any account tied to payments.
  • Check that your phone number and recovery email are current on your most important accounts — banking, primary email, and government service portals.
  • Look for any reused passwords, particularly on accounts you don't use often.

Minutes 18-25: Review what's publicly visible

  • Check your LinkedIn and major social profiles the way a stranger would see them, not logged in as yourself.
  • Review your connections list visibility and activity-sharing defaults on LinkedIn specifically, since these are often more open than people expect.
  • Confirm your privacy settings on WhatsApp, Instagram, and any platform you use regularly haven't reset after an app update.

Minutes 25-30: Close the loop

  • Note anything that surprised you and address the highest-risk item first — usually a reused password or an outdated recovery number.
  • Set a calendar reminder for the same check next year.

Why once a year is enough

Most exposure accumulates slowly — a new account here, an old forgotten profile there — rather than all at once. An annual check catches the drift without needing constant vigilance. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need any special tools to run this audit?

No — a browser in private mode and a breach-checking service like Scan My Shadow cover the technical part; the rest is reviewing settings you already have access to.

How is this different from a one-time digital footprint check?

This is designed as a recurring, once-a-year routine rather than a single check, since exposure accumulates gradually through new accounts and forgotten old ones.

What's the single highest-priority item on this checklist?

Two-factor authentication on your most important accounts — email and banking specifically — since that's the setting most likely to prevent an actual account takeover if a password is ever exposed.

Should I do this audit more often than once a year?

For most people, annually is sufficient, though it's worth running an extra check after a major life event like changing jobs, moving, or switching phone numbers.

What if the audit turns up something serious, like an active breach?

Prioritize changing that specific password immediately and enabling two-factor authentication, then check whether the same password was reused anywhere else.
