Your Number Is Being Used to Scam Others: SIM Clone Aftermath

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: If people are receiving scam calls or messages that appear to come from your number, it's usually either SIM cloning (your actual SIM was duplicated) or caller ID spoofing (your number is faked without touching your SIM). The response differs, so confirm which one it is before acting.

Finding out your number has been calling or messaging other people without your knowledge is unsettling — and the fix depends on figuring out which of two very different things is actually happening.

First: is your SIM actually cloned, or is your number spoofed?

These get confused constantly, and the response is different for each:

  • SIM cloning/swap: Your actual SIM has been duplicated or reissued to someone else, meaning your real SIM may lose service, and the attacker can receive your calls, SMS, and OTPs. This is a much more serious breach — full detail in SIM swap fraud: how it works and how to spot it.
  • Caller ID spoofing: Your SIM is untouched and working normally. A scammer is simply faking your number as the displayed caller ID on someone else's phone — a technique that doesn't require access to your SIM at all.

Signs pointing to SIM cloning: your own phone suddenly loses network signal, "No Service" appears unexpectedly, or you stop receiving OTPs you're expecting. If your phone works completely normally and people are just telling you they got a call "from your number," spoofing is far more likely.

If it's SIM cloning

  • Contact your telecom provider's fraud line immediately and request they investigate and block the duplicate SIM.
  • Visit a store in person with ID if your provider requires it to reissue your original SIM.
  • Check the Sanchar Saathi portal (sancharsaathi.gov.in) to see all active connections registered under your ID and report any you don't recognize.
  • Change passwords on any accounts that use SMS-based OTP as a security factor, since a cloned SIM can intercept those.

If it's caller ID spoofing

There's unfortunately no direct technical fix on your end — your provider can't "un-spoof" your number for other networks. What helps:

  • Report it via cybercrime.gov.in with details of who received the spoofed calls, if you know.
  • Tell close contacts and family that your number has been spoofed, so they're skeptical of unexpected calls or requests appearing to come from you.
  • File a complaint with TRAI/your telecom provider, since spoofing patterns feed into broader carrier-level fraud detection.

Either way: assume some of your data is exposed

Both SIM cloning and targeted spoofing usually mean a scammer has your number and possibly more — enough to convincingly impersonate you. It's worth checking what else is tied to your phone number across breach sources with a Scan My Shadow scan, and reviewing what changes for your privacy when you switch SIM cards if you end up needing a new number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my SIM was cloned versus just spoofed?

SIM cloning usually shows up as your own phone losing signal or missing expected OTPs. Spoofing leaves your phone working normally — the scammer never touched your actual SIM, only faked your number as caller ID on other people's phones.

Can I stop someone from spoofing my number?

Not directly — there's no way to technically block spoofing from your end. Reporting it helps carrier-level fraud detection and gives you a record if it escalates.

What should I tell people who received scam calls appearing to be from me?

Explain clearly that your number was spoofed or cloned and you didn't make the call, and advise them not to trust the number for any request involving money or personal details until this is resolved.

Where do I check what SIM connections are registered under my ID?

The Sanchar Saathi portal (sancharsaathi.gov.in) shows active mobile connections linked to your identity, and lets you report ones you don't recognize.

If my SIM was cloned, are my other accounts at risk?

Yes — any account using SMS-based OTP as a security step could be compromised through a cloned SIM. Change those passwords and consider switching to app-based two-factor authentication where possible.
