Australia: Scamwatch and IDCARE — The Recovery Process Explained

Scan My ShadowSeptember 3, 20264 min read

TL;DR: In Australia, Scamwatch (run by the National Anti-Scam Centre) is where you report a scam, while IDCARE is a free, dedicated service that helps you actually recover from identity misuse — the two serve different purposes and it's worth using both.

Australia's scam-response system has two main organizations doing different jobs, and understanding the difference helps you get the right kind of help faster.

Scamwatch: where you report

Scamwatch is run by the National Anti-Scam Centre (NASC), a unit within the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) established in 2023. Reporting a scam here:

  • Feeds into national data used to warn others about emerging scam patterns.
  • Helps coordinate action between government, law enforcement, banks, and telcos to disrupt scam operations.
  • Is not, by itself, a personal recovery service — it's primarily a reporting and intelligence function.

Report at scamwatch.gov.au regardless of whether money was lost — reports of scam attempts that didn't succeed still help build the pattern data used to warn others.

IDCARE: where you get identity recovery help

IDCARE is Australia and New Zealand's national identity and cyber support service — a not-for-profit that specifically helps individuals whose identity information has been misused or exposed, whether through a scam, a data breach, or theft. Unlike Scamwatch, IDCARE provides direct, personalized support:

  • A dedicated case manager who walks you through exactly which accounts, documents, and records need attention based on what was compromised.
  • Guidance specific to Australian systems — Medicare, myGov, driver's licence, and financial institution processes.
  • Support is free for individuals.

When to use each

  • Money was lost, or you want to flag a scam pattern: Scamwatch.
  • Personal identity documents, Medicare details, or myGov access were compromised: IDCARE, in addition to Scamwatch.
  • Both: Report the scam itself to Scamwatch, then contact IDCARE for hands-on help securing what was exposed.

If your bank accounts were affected

Contact your bank directly and immediately — Scamwatch and IDCARE both complement bank-level fraud reporting rather than replace it. Ask your bank about their specific scam reimbursement policy, since major Australian banks have their own internal fraud teams and processes separate from the national services above.

Government identity documents specifically

If a Centrelink Customer Reference Number, Medicare, or myGov detail was compromised, also contact the Services Australia Scams and Identity Theft help desk directly, since some document-specific protections need to go through the issuing agency rather than IDCARE alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Scamwatch and IDCARE?

Scamwatch is where you report a scam — it feeds national data and coordination efforts. IDCARE is a dedicated support service that helps you personally recover from identity misuse, with a case manager guiding you through specific accounts and documents.

Is IDCARE a paid service?

No, IDCARE's support for individuals is free — it's a not-for-profit national identity and cyber support service for Australia and New Zealand.

Do I need to report to Scamwatch even if I didn't lose money?

Yes, it's still worth reporting — scam attempts that didn't succeed still contribute to the pattern data Scamwatch uses to warn others about emerging tactics.

Who runs Scamwatch?

The National Anti-Scam Centre (NASC), a unit within the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC), established in 2023.

What if my Medicare or myGov details were compromised?

Contact the Services Australia Scams and Identity Theft help desk directly for document-specific protections, in addition to reporting to Scamwatch and contacting IDCARE for broader identity recovery support.
