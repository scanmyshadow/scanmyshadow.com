Meta and Google's "Download Your Data": What It Actually Gives You

Scan My ShadowSeptember 2, 20263 min read

TL;DR: Meta and Google both offer data-download tools built to satisfy access-request laws like GDPR. The archives are large but not always easy to interpret — here's what's actually in them and what to look for.

Both Meta (through the Accounts Center) and Google (through Google Takeout) let you request a full export of the data tied to your account. These tools exist largely because access-request laws like GDPR require it, but the resulting archive is often more overwhelming than illuminating without knowing what to look for.

What's typically in a Meta data download

  • Posts, comments, and messages across the platforms tied to your account.
  • Ad interest categories — the topics Meta's ad system has inferred about you based on activity, often more revealing than people expect.
  • Login and device history — locations and devices used to access your account over time.

What's often missing or hard to find is the actual inference logic — the "why" behind an ad category, or how a specific piece of behavior led to a particular categorization. The data is there; the reasoning generally isn't.

What's typically in a Google Takeout download

  • Search and location history, if enabled, going back as far as your account settings retained it.
  • Individual product data — Gmail, Drive, Photos, YouTube watch history — each exportable separately or together.
  • Ad personalization data, similar to Meta's interest categories.

Google Takeout lets you select specific products rather than downloading everything at once, which makes it more manageable but also easy to miss something if you're not sure what you're looking for.

What to actually do with the archive

Most people download it once, get overwhelmed, and close the file. A more useful approach is targeted: check your ad interest categories specifically, since that's the clearest window into what these platforms have inferred about you beyond what you directly posted. If something seems inaccurate or overly invasive, both platforms let you adjust ad preferences directly in account settings.

This kind of access right exists under frameworks like GDPR and PIPEDA — both worth understanding if you want the fuller legal picture behind why these tools exist. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get my Meta or Google data download?

Usually a few minutes to a few hours depending on how much data is tied to your account, though large accounts with years of photo or video history can take longer.

Is the data download free?

Yes, both Meta's Accounts Center download and Google Takeout are free tools available to any account holder.

Does the download include data that's been deleted?

No, generally only data currently associated with your active account is included, not previously deleted content.

Can I request my data if I'm not in the EU?

Yes, both tools are available globally regardless of where you live, though the specific legal right prompting the tool's existence may vary by your jurisdiction.

What's the most useful part of the archive to check first?

Ad interest and personalization categories tend to be the most revealing about what the platform has actually inferred about you, beyond the content you directly posted.
