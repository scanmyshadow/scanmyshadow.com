Instagram Scams: Fake Giveaways, Impersonation and DM Traps

Scan My Shadow · September 20, 2026
Scam GuideInstagramSocial Media

TL;DR: Instagram scams mostly arrive as DMs and comments: fake giveaways, hacked-friend messages, brand offers and account-recovery phishing. Report the account inside Instagram, secure your login with two-factor authentication, and report money lost to your national fraud body, such as the FTC in the US or the cybercrime portal in India. Instagram mixes public profiles, direct messages and ads, which gives scammers three ways to reach you. Most scams copy a pattern you can learn to recognise.

Common Instagram scams

  1. Fake giveaways. You "won" a prize but must pay shipping or tax, or enter card details.
  2. Hacked-friend messages. A friend's account asks you to vote for them, forward a code or send money. The hacked account recovery guide applies here too.
  3. Policy-violation phishing. A DM says your account will be banned unless you click a link. A screenshot of a badge proves nothing.
  4. Brand-collab scams. A "sponsor" asks for an upfront product cost, or sends a fake payment and asks you to refund the difference.
  5. Cash-flip and mentor offers. Send a small amount, "get more back". You do not.
  6. Fake shops and ads. Prices far below market. See how to check if a website is a scam.
  7. Romance and sextortion. A friendly stranger builds trust, then asks for money or images. Read the romance scam red flags and the 48-hour takedown rule.
  8. Like-and-earn jobs. The same task script as the fake job offer scams.

I got scammed on Instagram: what to do

  1. Stop replying and do not send more money.
  2. Save screenshots of the profile, username, chats and payment proof.
  3. Report the account and messages inside Instagram.
  4. Tell your bank and report to the FTC (US), Action Fraud (UK), the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre or Scamwatch (Australia). In India, call 1930 and file at the cybercrime portal; the complaint filing guide explains the steps.
  5. Change your password, turn on two-factor authentication and review where you are logged in.

Protect your account

  • Use an authenticator app for two-factor authentication.
  • Do not click links in DMs about violations, bans or verification; check Instagram's own security settings instead.
  • Limit who can message you and consider a private profile.
  • Avoid tagging your exact location; see how geotagged photos reveal your address.
  • Ad data also shapes what scammers can target; see what Instagram and Facebook ad preferences reveal about you.

FAQs

How do I know if an Instagram account is a scammer?

Warning signs include a new account with few posts, stolen photos, a username that copies a real brand, pressure to move to WhatsApp or Telegram, and any request for money, codes or prepayment.

What should I do if I got scammed on Instagram?

Stop contact, save screenshots, report the account in Instagram, tell your bank, report to your national fraud body such as the FTC, secure your login with a new password and two-factor authentication, and in India call 1930 and file at cybercrime.gov.in.

Are Instagram cash-flip or money-flip offers real?

No. They promise a big return on a small payment and do not deliver. Once you send money, it is not returned and scammers may ask for more.

How do I report a scam on Instagram?

Open the profile or message, tap the menu, choose Report and pick the scam or fraud option. Also report money lost to your bank and to your national fraud body such as the FTC, and in India to the cybercrime portal.
