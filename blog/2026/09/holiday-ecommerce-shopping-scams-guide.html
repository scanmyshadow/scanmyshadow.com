Holiday and E-Commerce Shopping Scams: A Seasonal Guide

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: Online shopping scams spike sharply every November and December, driven by fake stores advertised through social media ads and phony shipping/delivery text notifications. Social media is now the single costliest way scammers reach shoppers, with losses in the billions.

Shopping scams follow the calendar closely — volume climbs steadily from late October through the holiday season, and the tactics are consistent enough year to year to recognize in advance.

The fake store pattern

  • A social media ad shows a heavily discounted, popular item — often 50–80% off a brand name product.
  • The linked store looks professional but is newly registered, with no genuine customer reviews outside the site itself.
  • Payment goes through, but the item never arrives, arrives as a cheap counterfeit, or the site disappears entirely after enough orders come in.

The fake shipping/delivery text pattern

A text or email claims a delivery attempt failed, or a small "customs" or "redelivery" fee is required to release a package. This mirrors the toll-text scam mechanic closely — see the EZ Pass toll scam for the same underlying pattern. Legitimate carriers don't collect small redelivery fees through a text link.

Why social media specifically is the costliest channel

Social media has become the most expensive way scammers reach shoppers, with losses roughly eightfold higher than five years ago — driven largely by highly targeted ads that can be shown to exactly the demographic most likely to buy a specific "deal."

How to verify a store before buying

  • Search the store's name plus "reviews" or "scam" before purchasing — genuine complaint patterns show up quickly for active scam stores.
  • Check how long the site's domain has existed; a domain registered within the last few weeks selling heavily discounted branded goods is a strong warning sign.
  • Pay with a credit card rather than a debit card, wire transfer, or gift card — credit cards carry the strongest built-in chargeback protection if the order never arrives.
  • Be skeptical of deals significantly below the price at major, established retailers for the same item.

If you've already been scammed

  • If you paid by credit card, dispute the charge with your card issuer as soon as the item fails to arrive within a reasonable window.
  • If you paid by gift card or wire transfer, see Gift Card Scam Recovery or Wire Fraud Recovery depending on the method used.
  • Report the store to the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov) and to the platform where the ad appeared, so it can be taken down before reaching more shoppers.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do shopping scams typically spike?

Volume climbs sharply from late October through the holiday season, tracking the same pattern as legitimate holiday shopping demand — scammers deliberately time campaigns to this window.

Why is social media the costliest way scammers reach shoppers?

Highly targeted advertising lets scammers show a specific fake deal to exactly the audience most likely to buy it, driving losses roughly eightfold higher than five years ago.

Is it safe to pay for online purchases with a debit card during holiday shopping?

Credit cards generally offer stronger built-in chargeback protection than debit cards, wire transfers, or gift cards, making them the safer payment method for purchases from unfamiliar stores.

How can I check if an online store is legitimate before ordering?

Search the store's name alongside 'reviews' or 'scam,' check how recently the site's domain was registered, and be skeptical of prices significantly below major established retailers for the same item.

What should I do if a package I ordered never arrives?

Dispute the charge with your card issuer if paid by credit card, and report the store to the FTC and the platform where you saw the ad.
