PAN Card Misuse: How to Check and Protect Yourself

Scan My ShadowSeptember 14, 20266 min read

TL;DR: Your PAN (Permanent Account Number) is the single identifier tying together your tax history, bank accounts, and loans — which is exactly why it's become a favorite target for fraud in India. Here's how PAN misuse actually happens, the clearest way to check if yours has been used without your knowledge, and what to do if it has.

A PAN card doesn't look like much — ten characters, a name, a photo. But because every loan, every bank account, every large financial transaction in India runs through it, a misused PAN can open doors a stolen password never could. Fraudsters have taken notice: reports in 2025 pointed to a sharp rise in PAN-linked fraud, much of it tied to fake loan applications and shell company registrations that victims only discover months later.

How PAN misuse actually happens

Most PAN fraud doesn't start with a dramatic hack — it starts with routine oversharing. A PAN photocopy handed to a broker for a KYC form, uploaded to an unverified "instant loan" app, or left on a public job or matrimonial listing can end up circulating far beyond its original purpose. From there, the common patterns are:

  • Loan and credit card fraud — a fraudster uses your PAN to apply for a loan or credit card, often through a lender with weaker verification, and the debt shows up under your name.
  • Shell company registration — your PAN is listed as a director or partner in a company you've never heard of, sometimes used to launder money or file fraudulent tax returns.
  • Phishing disguised as PAN "updates" — messages claiming your PAN needs re-verification or linking to Aadhaar, designed to harvest your PAN, date of birth, and OTP in one step.
  • Data breaches at third parties — PAN details stored by an app, broker, or financial platform get exposed when that company's own security fails, regardless of anything you did.

The clearest way to check for misuse

You don't need to guess. The most direct signal of PAN misuse is your own credit report: any loan, credit card, or credit inquiry made using your PAN shows up there, whether you applied for it or not. Pull your credit report from TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, or CRIF High Mark (most offer one free check per year, and several finance apps offer more frequent free pulls) and look specifically for:

  • Loans or credit cards you never applied for
  • Hard credit inquiries from lenders you don't recognize
  • Any account listed with an address, employer, or phone number that isn't yours

It's also worth checking the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal for any income, TDS, or company-directorship records linked to your PAN that you don't recognize — a legitimate and free way to spot the shell-company variant of this fraud.

What to do if you find something

If a loan or account shows up that isn't yours, the sequence that actually helps is: (1) contact the lender or bank directly and flag it as fraudulent, in writing; (2) raise a dispute with the credit bureau that's showing the incorrect entry — this triggers a formal investigation and can get the entry corrected or removed; (3) file a complaint on India's National Cybercrime Reporting Portal, since PAN misuse for financial fraud is a cognizable offence; and (4) if you suspect broader identity misuse beyond just this one loan, treat it as a signal to check what else of yours might be circulating, not just this one incident.

Reducing your exposure going forward

  • Share your PAN only through official channels (a bank's own app or branch, not a third-party aggregator or unknown lending app).
  • Avoid uploading PAN photos to social media, matrimonial profiles, or classifieds — even briefly, even in a private group.
  • Set up SMS/email alerts with your bank and credit bureau for any new credit inquiry — this is often the fastest way to catch misuse before it snowballs.
  • If you've shared your PAN with a broker, agent, or third party for a one-time transaction, ask what happens to that copy afterward.

A PAN is just one piece of a much wider financial identity, and checking it in isolation only tells part of the story — your phone number and email are usually leaking in parallel through separate channels. FAQs

Can anyone misuse my PAN card just by knowing the number?

The 10-digit number alone isn't usually enough — most fraud needs a photo, signature, or a supporting document alongside it. But the number combined with a scanned copy is often all that's needed for many lending apps' KYC checks.

How often should I check my credit report for PAN misuse?

At least once every 3–6 months, and immediately if you get an unexpected SMS about a loan, EMI, or credit inquiry you didn't initiate.

Will getting a new PAN card fix the problem?

No — your PAN number itself typically doesn't change even when you get a reprint or duplicate card, so misuse tied to the number persists until you actively dispute the fraudulent entries.

Is PAN card fraud a criminal offence in India?

Yes. Using someone else's PAN for financial fraud, tax evasion, or identity misrepresentation is a cognizable offence and can be reported through India's National Cybercrime Reporting Portal.
