Fake FTC Agents Are Running a 'Refund and Recovery' Scam

Scan My Shadow · September 17, 2026
Scam AlertUSAGovernment Impersonation

TL;DR: Scammers are impersonating Federal Trade Commission officials by phone and text, using fake employee badge photos and invented 'verification' steps to pressure people — often past scam victims specifically — into authorizing payments under a fake 'refund and recovery' pretext. The FTC's real June 2026 consumer alert is explicit: real staff never text you, then ask you to move money.

This scam has a particularly cruel target: people who have already lost money to a previous scam, now being told the FTC can help recover it — for a fee. The Federal Trade Commission's own June 2026 consumer alert describes exactly this "refund and recovery" pattern, warning people not to trust anyone claiming they can claw back money from a past scam who then demands more cash to do it.

How the scam is built to feel credible

  • Fake employee badge photos sent by text or messaging app to "prove" the caller's identity — a level of visual detail earlier phone scams didn't bother with.
  • Invented "verification" steps that sound bureaucratic and official, designed to feel like normal government process rather than a red flag.
  • High-pressure urgency pushing targets to authorize payments quickly, often through methods that are difficult or impossible to reverse once sent.
  • Targeting past scam victims specifically — people already primed to want their money back, and whose contact details may have circulated further after the original scam.

What the real FTC says about this

According to the FTC's own alert, genuine staff will not text or message someone and then ask them to move money, and a real employee will never send a badge photo as identity proof. The FTC does not call, text, or email individuals demanding payment to release a refund — legitimate FTC refunds are distributed automatically to eligible consumers from a settlement, never requiring a prior payment from the recipient.

Why this specific version is worth knowing about

Anyone who has been scammed before is an especially attractive target for this follow-up fraud — their information is often already circulating from the original incident, and the emotional desire to recover a real loss makes the "recovery" pitch land harder than a cold approach would. If you've been scammed once, expect follow-up attempts and know in advance what a real recovery process does and doesn't look like.

How to protect yourself

  • Hang up or stop responding to any unsolicited call or text claiming to be the FTC about a refund.
  • Verify independently by contacting the FTC directly through its official website, not a number or link provided by the caller.
  • Never send money, gift cards, or crypto to "unlock" or "process" a refund you're owed — that is never how legitimate refunds work.
  • If you've been scammed before, assume your contact details may be circulating and treat unsolicited "recovery" offers with extra skepticism, not less.

If you've already responded

Stop all further payments immediately. Report the incident at reportfraud.ftc.gov, and contact your bank if any payment was made. A similar pretext is being used in California, where scammers posed as a US Marshal over "compromised" data. Consider that your information may already be circulating among other scammers — checking your broader digital exposure is a reasonable next step.

FAQs

Does the real FTC ever call or text asking for payment to release a refund?

No. The FTC's own alert states real staff never text or message someone and then ask them to move money — legitimate refunds are distributed automatically, with no upfront payment required from the recipient.

Why do scammers specifically target people who've already been scammed once?

Past victims are seen as easier targets — their information may already be circulating from the original incident, and their genuine desire to recover a real loss makes a fake 'recovery' pitch feel more urgent and believable.

What should I do if someone contacts me claiming to be from the FTC?

Don't engage with the contact directly. Verify independently by going to the FTC's official website yourself, and report any suspicious contact at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
