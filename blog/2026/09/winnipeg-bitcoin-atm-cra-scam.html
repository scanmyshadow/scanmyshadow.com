Winnipeg's Bitcoin ATM and the CRA Impersonation Scam Pattern

Scan My Shadow · September 17, 2026
Scam AlertCanadaCRA ImpersonationCryptocurrency

TL;DR: In a 2020 Global News report, a Winnipeg grocery store owner began warning customers directly after noticing a pattern: scammers posing as the CRA (Canada Revenue Agency) were repeatedly pressuring people into using his shop's Bitcoin ATM to pay fake tax debts. Crypto ATMs have become a preferred payment channel for scammers precisely because the transactions are fast and effectively irreversible.

In 2020, the pattern emerged clearly enough at one Winnipeg grocery store that its owner posted a warning sign directly on the shop's Bitcoin ATM. Customers were repeatedly arriving in visible distress, having received phone calls claiming they owed money to the Canada Revenue Agency — or in some cases Manitoba Hydro — and were told a Bitcoin payment was the required or fastest way to resolve it.

Why scammers specifically push victims toward crypto ATMs

  • Speed and irreversibility. Once a Bitcoin ATM transaction completes, it's extremely difficult to reverse — unlike a bank transfer, which sometimes still has a window for a bank to intervene.
  • Physical presence adds pressure. Directing a victim to a specific machine, sometimes while staying on the phone with them the whole time, keeps them isolated from a chance to pause and verify independently.
  • Crypto still feels unfamiliar enough to many victims that unusual instructions (scan this code, insert this much cash) don't trigger the same skepticism a more familiar payment method might.

What real CRA and utility communications look like

  • The CRA does not demand payment via Bitcoin, gift cards, or any single specific payment method under threat of immediate consequences.
  • Genuine government debt notices arrive through official mail first, not an unexpected urgent phone call demanding same-day resolution.
  • A legitimate utility company (like Manitoba Hydro) does not direct customers to a cryptocurrency ATM to settle a bill.

What store owners and staff can do

This Winnipeg case is a useful model — a business owner noticing a pattern of distressed customers and posting a direct, visible warning at the point of transaction. If you operate or work near a crypto ATM, watching for customers who seem pressured, confused, or on a phone call while transacting is a real, low-cost intervention point.

If you're being pressured toward a crypto ATM payment right now

Stop and hang up. Contact the CRA or the relevant organization directly through its official number, not one provided by the caller, to verify independently. No legitimate debt requires immediate same-day payment through a Bitcoin machine — the same urgency tactic drives fake class-action settlement texts circulating in Canada right now.

FAQs

Does the CRA ever ask for payment through a Bitcoin ATM?

No — the CRA does not demand or accept payment via Bitcoin, cryptocurrency, or gift cards. Any call insisting on this payment method is fraudulent regardless of how official it sounds.

Why are scammers increasingly using crypto ATMs instead of gift cards?

Crypto ATM transactions are fast and effectively irreversible once completed, similar to gift cards, but crypto currently carries less public awareness as a common scam-payment method, making unusual instructions feel less immediately suspicious to some victims.

What should I do if I'm on the phone being told to go to a Bitcoin ATM?

Hang up immediately and contact the organization the caller claims to represent directly, using an official number you find independently — never one given by the caller.
