The Fake Parcel/Customs Scam, Explained

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: The fake parcel/customs scam claims your package is stuck at customs, contains something illegal, or triggers a fine — followed by a threat of arrest and a demand to pay immediately or share personal details. Real customs holds don't work this way, and this scam is frequently a doorway into a full digital arrest scenario.

This is one of the five most commonly flagged scam types in recent Indian banking-sector advisories, and it often escalates into something bigger than a simple payment request.

How it typically starts

  • A call or automated voice message claims a parcel addressed to you is being held at customs, sometimes claiming it contains something illegal — drugs, fake documents, or restricted items.
  • You're told a fine must be paid immediately to release it, or that failure to comply will result in arrest.
  • Some versions share a fake tracking link or "bill payment" link via SMS instead of, or in addition to, the call.

Where this often leads

The parcel scam is frequently used as an opening move that transitions directly into a full digital arrest scenario — the "customs official" transfers the call to a fake police or CBI officer, and the script shifts into sustained pressure and video surveillance. If a parcel call escalates into a threat of video call "verification" or arrest, treat it exactly like a digital arrest attempt: end the call.

What a real customs hold actually looks like

  • Genuine customs communication comes through official written notices tied to a specific, trackable shipment you're actually expecting — not an unsolicited call about a parcel you don't recognize sending.
  • Customs duties are paid through official government channels (ICEGATE or your courier's verified payment portal), never through a link sent via SMS or a UPI QR code shared over a call.
  • No legitimate customs process threatens arrest over the phone or demands immediate payment to avoid it.

The giveaways

  • You weren't expecting any parcel, or can't verify the sender.
  • The call demands immediate payment or personal details under threat.
  • You're pressured to stay on the line, transferred to another "department," or asked to move to video call.

What to do

Hang up. If you're genuinely expecting a shipment, verify directly through the courier's official app or website using the tracking number you already have — never a link or number given during the call. If you've already engaged further, been transferred to a fake official, or shared any payment or documents, see The Digital Arrest Call Just Ended for the next steps, and report the number at cybercrime.gov.in regardless of outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal for customs to call about a held parcel?

No — genuine customs communication is tied to a specific, trackable shipment you're actually expecting and comes through official written notices, not an unsolicited call demanding immediate payment.

Why does this scam often escalate into a digital arrest scenario?

The parcel/customs framing is frequently used as an opening move, with the caller transferring you to a fake police or CBI 'officer' once the initial fear is established — the same escalation pattern as other impersonation scams.

How do I pay real customs duties safely?

Through official government channels like ICEGATE or your courier's verified payment portal — never through a link sent via SMS or a QR code shared over a phone call.

What should I do if I'm on a call like this right now?

Hang up. If you're expecting a real shipment, verify independently through the courier's official app or tracking number, not anything provided during the call.

Should I report this even if I didn't lose money?

Yes — report the number at cybercrime.gov.in so it can be flagged before it's used on the next person.
