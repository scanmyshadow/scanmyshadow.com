Mumbai Woman Loses ₹4 Lakh to a Telegram Trading Group Scam

Scan My Shadow · September 17, 2026
Scam AlertIndiaInvestment FraudTelegram

TL;DR: A Mumbai woman lost nearly ₹4 lakh after being lured into a Telegram 'trading group' that looked like a smart investment opportunity but was a well-planned digital trap. Telegram-based stock market fraud has become one of the fastest-growing scam categories in India — here's the pattern behind it.

Telegram trading groups follow a consistent, well-tested format across the cases now being reported in India. A victim is invited — often via a WhatsApp or SMS link, sometimes through a social media ad — into a Telegram channel presenting itself as an exclusive stock-tips or trading community. The group is often padded with dozens or hundreds of fake "members" posting screenshots of impressive gains, creating social proof for what is, underneath, a fabricated trading dashboard.

How the Mumbai case unfolded

The victim was drawn in by messages showing consistent profits from group "trades," building confidence over several weeks. She was directed to deposit funds into what appeared to be a legitimate trading platform, watching her displayed balance grow — but the numbers were fabricated by the fake dashboard, not real market activity. When she attempted to withdraw her supposed profits, she was asked for additional "fees" or "taxes" before release — a classic mechanism used to extract further payments before the scam collapses.

Why this format is so effective right now

  • Telegram's group size and anonymity make it easy to simulate a thriving, credible trading community with fake accounts.
  • Fabricated trading dashboards show fake real-time gains, making the "investment" feel tangible and trustworthy long before any money is actually at risk of being withdrawn.
  • The withdrawal-fee trap keeps victims paying in, since each new fee feels small relative to the (fake) balance they believe they're protecting.

How to spot a fake Telegram trading group

  • A trading platform reached through a Telegram link, not a SEBI-registered broker's own app or website, is a strong warning sign on its own.
  • Guaranteed or consistently high returns with no visible losses — real markets don't work that way.
  • Any request for additional payment before a withdrawal goes through.
  • The same fake-authority pattern recently showed up in a fake-banker Facebook scam in Bengaluru. Check any advisor or platform against SEBI's official registered list at sebi.gov.in before depositing anything.

If you've been caught in one

Stop sending any further "fees" immediately — the withdrawal will not happen regardless of how much more is paid. File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930, and report the Telegram group/channel directly to Telegram's support so it can be taken down.

FAQs

How do fake Telegram trading groups make fake profits look real?

They use a fabricated trading dashboard that displays fake real-time gains, unconnected to any actual market activity — the numbers are simply set by the scammers to build confidence before the withdrawal stage.

Why do these scams ask for a fee before letting you withdraw?

It's a mechanism to extract further payments once a victim is emotionally invested in a large displayed balance — the withdrawal is designed to never actually go through regardless of fees paid.

How can I verify if a trading platform is legitimate?

Check it against SEBI's official registered intermediaries list at sebi.gov.in. A platform reached only through a Telegram link, with no verifiable SEBI registration, should not be trusted with any deposit.
