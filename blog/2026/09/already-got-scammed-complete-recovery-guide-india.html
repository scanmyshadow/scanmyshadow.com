Already Scammed in India? The Complete Recovery Guide

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: If you've just been scammed in India, the order of operations is: call 1930 (or file at cybercrime.gov.in) immediately, then your bank, then re-secure your accounts, then follow up. This page is the map — it links to a detailed guide for whichever situation matches yours.

If you're reading this because something just happened — you sent money, shared a document, or realized an account isn't yours anymore — start with the first section below. It's written for right now, not for research. The detailed guides are linked throughout if you need to go deeper on your specific situation.

Step 1: The first hour

Call the national cybercrime helpline, 1930, or file a report at cybercrime.gov.in. This is the single most time-sensitive action you can take. India's Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting & Management System (CFCFRMS) can flag a fraudulent transfer to the receiving bank before it's withdrawn or moved again — but only within a narrow window police call the "Golden Hour." We've broken down exactly how that mechanism works in The First Hour After Sending Money to a Scammer.

You'll get an acknowledgment number by SMS. Save it — you need it to complete the formal complaint within 24 hours.

Step 2: Match your situation to a specific guide

Recovery steps differ depending on what actually happened. Pick the one that fits:

  • Sent money via UPI → UPI Fraud Recovery: The Bank Dispute Process
  • Just got off a fake "digital arrest" call → The Digital Arrest Call Just Ended
  • Shared your Aadhaar or PAN → You Shared Your Aadhaar or PAN With a Scammer
  • Lost access to WhatsApp or a social account → Recovering a Hacked WhatsApp or Social Account
  • Scammed on a matrimonial site → Scammed on a Matrimonial Site: Recovery Steps
  • Paid a fake job "registration fee" → Job Scam Recovery Playbook
  • Lost money in a fake trading/investment app → Investment or Trading App Scam Recovery
  • Your number is now being used to scam others → SIM Clone Aftermath

Step 3: Re-secure everything, not just what was targeted

Scammers who get one piece of information — a password, an OTP, a document photo — often try it elsewhere. Change passwords on your email and banking apps first, turn on two-factor authentication (here's how 2FA actually protects your accounts), and check whether your number or email shows up in old breaches that could be used to target you again.

Step 4: File the FIR and follow up

The 1930 complaint isn't the same as an FIR. For larger losses, you may need to visit your local cyber cell or police station in person. Keep your acknowledgment number, transaction IDs, and screenshots ready — we cover exactly what happens after this in What Happens After You File a Cybercrime Complaint.

Step 5: Don't skip the emotional part

Being scammed is disorienting, and shame keeps a lot of people from reporting at all — which is exactly what lets these networks keep operating. If the money isn't coming back, reporting still matters, both for you and for the next person the scammer would have called. More on this in The Emotional Aftermath of Being Scammed.

Why this happened, and staying safer going forward

Most scams that reach the "money moved" stage started with the caller sounding credible — often because they had a real name, a real employer, or a real family detail to open with. That detail usually comes from data that's already public or already leaked, which is exactly what Scan My Shadow checks for. If you want to understand how scammers build that credibility in the first place, see How Scammers Build a Convincing Profile of You.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the very first thing I should do after being scammed in India?

Call 1930 or file at cybercrime.gov.in immediately. This triggers a fund-freeze request to the receiving bank while the money may still be traceable — the sooner you call, the better the odds.

Is 1930 the same as filing an FIR?

No. 1930 registers your complaint into the national fraud-reporting system (CFCFRMS) and can trigger a bank freeze, but for larger losses you'll typically still need to visit your local police station or cyber cell to file a formal FIR.

Can I recover money that's already been withdrawn by the scammer?

It's harder but not always impossible. Recovery depends heavily on how quickly you reported it and whether the funds were still sitting in an intermediary account when the freeze request went through.

What if I don't know which type of scam happened to me?

Start with the 1930 call regardless — that step is the same no matter the scam type. Then use this page to find the specific guide that matches what happened, whether it's UPI fraud, a fake investment app, or a digital arrest call.

Should I be embarrassed to report this?

No. Cybercrime cells process these reports constantly, and reporting — even without recovering the money — helps freeze accounts used against other victims.
