Investment Scam Playbook: How Fake Trading Groups and Deepfake Pitches Actually Work

Scan My Shadow · September 3, 2026

TL;DR: Investment scams — fake trading platforms and AI-generated deepfake pitches showing familiar faces endorsing returns — accounted for roughly 77% of India's reported cyber fraud losses in 2025. Here's the recognizable playbook behind them, before you ever deposit a rupee.

Investment fraud isn't just the largest category of scam loss in India by dollar amount — it's the fastest-evolving, now incorporating AI-generated video specifically to overcome the skepticism that stopped older versions of the same scam.

The typical entry point

  • A WhatsApp or Telegram group invitation, often forwarded by someone in your existing network who was themselves added earlier.
  • A social media ad featuring a deepfake video of a recognizable business figure, actor, or news anchor "endorsing" a trading platform or guaranteed-return scheme — Indians now encounter an average of several deepfakes daily according to recent industry reporting.
  • A "relationship manager" who reaches out directly, often referencing a real event or news story to sound current and credible.

How the group builds trust

  • Early "trades" show consistent gains, sometimes with small real withdrawals processed quickly to build confidence.
  • Other group members (often bots or paid participants) post enthusiastic results, creating social proof.
  • Requests to increase deposits follow, often framed as "VIP" or "premium" tiers with better returns.

The warning signs, before you deposit anything

  • Guaranteed or unusually consistent returns. Real markets don't work this way — consistent daily gains regardless of market conditions is a defining red flag.
  • Pressure to act quickly on a "limited window" or "special allocation."
  • Unregistered platforms. Check SEBI's registered intermediaries list directly on sebi.gov.in — never trust a claim of registration made within the app or group itself.
  • Withdrawal friction that appears only after larger deposits — early small withdrawals working smoothly, then unexpected "taxes," "processing fees," or account freezes once you try to withdraw a larger amount.

The deepfake layer specifically

AI-generated video and voice have made fake endorsements dramatically more convincing than a text testimonial ever was. Treat any investment pitch featuring a celebrity or business figure "endorsing" a specific trading app with default skepticism — verify independently through the person's own verified official channels, not anything shown to you inside the pitch itself.

If you've already invested and lost money

See Investment or Trading App Scam Recovery for what's realistic to recover, including reporting through both 1930 and SEBI's SCORES portal. Be especially cautious of anyone contacting you afterward offering to "recover" your losses for a fee — this follow-up scam specifically targets people who've already lost money once.

If you've been added to one of these groups but haven't invested, leave and report it — and consider whether the group had access to your number through a data exposure, which a Scan My Shadow report can help check. Frequently Asked Questions

What share of India's cyber fraud losses come from investment scams?

Roughly 77% of the approximately ₹19,813 crore lost to fraud and cheating in India in 2025 was linked to fraudulent investment schemes, according to Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre data.

How can I check if a trading platform is actually SEBI-registered?

Check the registered intermediaries list directly on sebi.gov.in — never trust a registration claim made within the platform or group itself, since these are frequently fabricated.

Why are deepfake videos specifically used in these scams now?

AI-generated video and voice of recognizable figures are significantly more convincing than a text testimonial, and are used specifically to overcome the skepticism that stopped older, text-based versions of the same scam.

What's the clearest single warning sign of an investment scam?

Guaranteed or unusually consistent returns regardless of market conditions — real investment returns fluctuate, and any pitch promising steady daily gains should be treated as a red flag by default.

Can I get my money back if I've already invested in a fake platform?

It's possible but harder than direct transfer fraud since funds are often moved quickly — report through both 1930 and SEBI's SCORES portal as soon as possible for the best chance.
