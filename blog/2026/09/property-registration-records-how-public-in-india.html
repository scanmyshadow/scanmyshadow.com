Property Registration Records: How Public Are They in India?

Scan My ShadowSeptember 14, 20265 min read

TL;DR: Yes — property registration in India is fundamentally a public record system, designed so that ownership, transaction history, and registered addresses can be verified by anyone, not just the parties involved. Here's what's actually searchable, through which state portals, and what that means if you own property and are thinking about address privacy.

Most privacy conversations focus on data that leaked by accident — a breach, a careless app, a scraped profile. Property registration records are a different category entirely: they're public by design, and have been for as long as land registration has existed as a legal institution. That doesn't make the exposure any less real for someone who'd rather not have their address easily searchable — it just means the starting point is different.

Why property records are public in the first place

Land registration exists to establish clear, verifiable title — so a buyer can confirm a seller actually owns what they're selling, a bank can verify collateral before approving a loan, and disputes can be resolved against an official record rather than competing claims. That core function requires the register to be genuinely public; a private, restricted-access land registry would defeat much of its own purpose.

What's actually searchable

Each Indian state maintains its own property registration and stamp duty portal (commonly branded as IGRS — Inspector General of Registration and Stamps — with state-specific names and interfaces), and most allow some form of public search using details like the property's survey number, registration document number, or the registered owner's name, depending on the state's specific implementation. What's typically visible through these searches:

  • The registered owner's name at the time of the transaction
  • The transaction date and registered value
  • The property's location and survey/plot details
  • In many states, a copy or index of the registered sale deed itself

This means someone with your full name and a rough idea of which state or city you own property in can, in many cases, locate the address tied to that ownership — without needing any special access or tool.

Who actually uses this, beyond curiosity

The legitimate use cases are substantial: real estate professionals verifying title before a purchase, banks confirming collateral for loans, lawyers conducting due diligence, and buyers checking a seller's genuine ownership history. This is also why most privacy-focused reform efforts around land registers internationally (a similar debate has played out in places like Estonia and Australia) tend to land on restricting search by name rather than closing the register entirely — since address-based or document-based search covers the legitimate use cases without making a person's full property holdings trivially name-searchable.

What this means practically, if it concerns you

  • If your registered address is one you'd prefer wasn't easily linkable to your name, understand that this specific exposure predates and exists independently of anything you've done online — it's a structural feature of how property ownership works in India, not a leak you caused.
  • Some states allow limited restriction of search results for specific safety or security circumstances — check your state's specific registration department for current provisions, since these vary and change.
  • Using a company, trust, or other legal structure to hold property is a more involved approach some people use for this reason, though it comes with its own costs, complexity, and tax implications worth discussing with a professional before pursuing purely for privacy reasons.
  • Being realistic about what's achievable matters here — property records being public is unlikely to change fundamentally, so the more practical question is usually about reducing other, non-structural exposure (phone, email, social profiles) rather than fighting a records system built around public verifiability.

The bigger picture

Property records are one of several categories of information that are public by design rather than by leak — voter rolls and court filings work similarly. None of these are "fixable" the way a data breach is, but knowing they exist changes how you think about your overall exposure: your address may already be one name-search away regardless of how carefully you guard your phone number or email elsewhere.

FAQs

Can I remove my name from property registration records?

Generally no — the registered transaction is a permanent legal record. Some states allow limited restrictions on public search access in specific safety circumstances, but the underlying record itself isn't typically erasable.

Do I need to register on a state portal to search property records, or is it free?

Most state IGRS portals allow free public search without registration, though some detailed document copies may involve a nominal fee.

Does selling the property remove my name from the historical record?

No — the record of your past ownership and the transaction remains in the register even after you sell; only the current ownership record updates to reflect the new owner.

Are property records the same as land tax or municipal records?

They're related but distinct — property registration records (IGRS) track ownership transactions, while municipal property tax records are a separate system, though both are generally public and often cross-reference the same property.
