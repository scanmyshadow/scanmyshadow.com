Romance Scams on Tinder, Bumble and Instagram: The Red Flags

Scan My Shadow · September 20, 2026

TL;DR: Romance scammers build trust fast, avoid live video, then invent an emergency or an investment that needs your money. Dating apps are real services, but any profile on them can be fake. Romance scams use the same script on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp. Only the channel changes. People who search for a "love trap" or "love scam" usually mean this pattern: affection used as a trap to get money.

How a romance scam usually unfolds

  1. Contact. A very attractive profile matches or messages you, then asks to move to WhatsApp or Telegram quickly.
  2. Love-bombing. Constant attention, compliments and talk of a future.
  3. Excuses. They are abroad, deployed or working on a rig, and cannot do a live video call.
  4. The ask. A customs fee, a hospital bill, a ticket to visit, or an investment tip. The last one is the pig butchering pattern.
  5. Pressure and secrecy. Do not tell your family. Pay again to unlock the money.
  6. Sextortion variant. Intimate images are used to demand payment. Do not pay; see the 48-hour takedown rule and why sextortion is rising.

Red flags

  • Photos that look like a model shoot, with few casual pictures.
  • A story that changes, or details that do not add up.
  • Refusal to video call, or a video call that is always "broken".
  • Requests for money, gift cards, crypto or bank details.
  • Pushing you off the app within days.

How to check someone before you trust them

  • Run a reverse image search on their photos.
  • Ask for a live video call early and see whether they agree.
  • Follow the steps in how to verify who you are talking to before you meet.
  • Know what your own profile reveals; see dating app privacy risks and how hidden profiles can be found.

If you are already involved

Stop sending money, do not pay "release" fees, and keep all messages and payment proof. Then follow romance scam recovery: practical and emotional next steps and report through the links above.

FAQs

Is Tinder a scam?

Tinder is a legitimate dating app. Scams come from fake or hijacked profiles on it, as they do on Bumble, Hinge and social media. Treat any request for money as a red flag.

How can I tell if a dating profile is fake?

Look for stock-style photos, a thin profile, a story that changes, a refusal to video call, and fast talk of love or money. A reverse image search often exposes stolen pictures.

Are Facebook and Instagram romance scams different?

The script is the same as on dating apps: fast trust, no live video, then a money request or investment tip. Only the platform differs.

Are there romance scammers on WhatsApp?

Yes. Scammers often move the chat from a dating app or Instagram to WhatsApp or Telegram within days, because it is harder to moderate. Treat a fast move off-app as a red flag.

Can I check an online dating scammer list?

No reliable public list exists. Scammers use new names and photos constantly, so check the behaviour and the photos rather than searching a list.

What is a Nigerian prince or advance-fee dating scam?

It is a classic pattern where someone claims wealth or trouble abroad and asks for a small fee to release a bigger reward or rescue. The story varies, but the ask for advance payment is the tell.
