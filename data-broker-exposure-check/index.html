Data Broker Exposure Check: What Information Do They Have About You?

Find out what data brokers are, how they collect personal information, where your details may appear, and how to check and reduce your own exposure.

TL;DR: Data brokers collect and combine information from many sources to build profiles about people. Your name, phone number, email, address, professional details, interests, and other information may appear in those profiles. You can check some exposure yourself, or use Scan My Shadow to check your phone number and email across 1,500+ sources.

The short answer

What is a data broker?

A data broker is a company that collects, combines, analyzes, and shares or sells personal information gathered from multiple sources. Unlike a service you knowingly sign up for, a data broker may build a profile about you without having a direct relationship with you.

That profile can connect pieces of information that look harmless on their own. A phone number, email address, public profile, purchase record, old listing, or other identifier can become part of a larger picture of who you are and how you can be reached.

Data brokers are different from a single public webpage. The concern is not only that one piece of information is visible. It is that information from different sources can be collected, linked, and made easier to search or use.
Where the data comes from

How do data brokers get your information?

Public information
Public records, business information, directories, public profiles, and other openly available information can contribute to a profile.
Online activity
Websites, apps, forms, cookies, purchases, loyalty programs, and other digital interactions can create data that may be shared with third parties.
Commercial sources
Companies may obtain information through partnerships, marketing lists, data licensing, or other commercial relationships and combine it with other records.
What may be in a profile

What information do data brokers have about you?

The exact information varies by broker and source. Depending on the service, profiles may contain or infer:

  • Name and contact details such as phone numbers and email addresses.
  • Addresses and location-related information gathered from available records and activity.
  • Professional or business information connected to public profiles or registrations.
  • Interests and activity signals used to categorize or market to people.
  • Relationships or linked identifiers inferred by connecting records across sources.

Not every broker has all of this information, and a profile may contain errors or outdated details.

Why this matters

Why should you care about data broker exposure?

The risk is not simply that a company knows your name. When multiple pieces of personal information are connected, your profile can become easier for marketers, people-search services, or malicious actors to use.

For an individual, exposed contact and identity information can contribute to unwanted marketing, targeted phishing, impersonation attempts, harassment, or more convincing scams. The practical goal is not to become invisible; it is to understand what is exposed and reduce unnecessary exposure.

Reducing exposure

How to reduce your data broker exposure

  1. Remove information you do not need public. Review old profiles, listings, directories, and public pages.
  2. Use broker opt-outs where available. Follow each site's stated privacy or deletion process.
  3. Limit future collection. Review app permissions, privacy settings, marketing preferences, and unnecessary sign-ups.
  4. Protect your key identifiers. Avoid publishing your phone number and personal email unnecessarily.
  5. Recheck periodically. Information can return or appear on another service after an opt-out.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a data broker?
A data broker collects, combines, analyzes, and shares or sells personal information from multiple sources, often without having a direct relationship with the person.
How do data brokers get my information?
They can collect information from public records, websites, apps, purchases, loyalty programs, marketing partners, public profiles, and other commercial or publicly available sources.
What information do data brokers have about me?
Depending on the broker, it may include names, phone numbers, email addresses, addresses, professional details, interests, demographic information, or other inferred attributes.
How can I find out if a data broker has my information?
Search your name, phone number, email address, and other identifiers on relevant people-search or data-broker sites, then review the site's privacy or opt-out process. A broader exposure scan can help identify relevant matches across its defined sources.
Can I remove my information from data brokers?
Often you can submit opt-out or deletion requests to individual brokers, although processes differ and information can reappear when brokers collect new data.
