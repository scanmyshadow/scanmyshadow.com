What Is the Matrimonial Digital Safety Checklist?

This free checklist helps you think through digital safety and verification considerations before marriage — covering identity consistency, communication patterns, pressure tactics, and financial red flags. It is not a covert investigation tool. It's built for mutual, consent-based use, ideally worked through together with open conversation.

The checklist takes about two minutes and gives you a Matrimonial Digital Safety Risk Score, along with your answers grouped into good signs, things worth discussing, and red flags requiring caution.

How Is the Score Calculated?

Each of the 10 questions targets a specific area — identity consistency, communication, financial requests, credential requests, and consent-based verification. Each answer contributes to the score based on the level of caution indicated by that response. The resulting score ranges from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating more caution-worthy answers. The score does not represent a probability that someone is fraudulent — it highlights areas worth further discussion or verification.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I stay safe on matrimonial websites?
Take time to have normal conversation before major decisions, watch for pressure to move quickly, never share OTPs or passwords, and be cautious of any request for money or financial details.
Should I verify someone's online identity?
Reasonable, consent-based verification is sensible. This means discussing things openly and using lawful means — not accessing someone's private accounts or obtaining information without their permission.
Should I ever share OTPs?
No. OTPs, passwords, and banking credentials should never be shared with anyone, including someone you are getting to know through a matrimonial platform.
What are financial warning signs?
Requests for money, loans, or investment transfers, urgent financial emergencies, or requests for OTPs and banking details are serious warning signs that call for caution.
How can digital verification respect privacy?
Verification should be mutual and consent-based — discussed openly between both people, using lawful means, rather than secretly accessing someone's accounts or information without their knowledge.
