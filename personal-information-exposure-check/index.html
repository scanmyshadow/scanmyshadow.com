Most people know what they have deliberately posted online. The harder part is everything that accumulated around it: an old profile, a forgotten marketplace listing, a public contact detail, a username reused for years, or information that appeared after a service was breached.

That is why the useful question is not simply “Do I have a digital footprint?” Everyone does. The useful question is “What personal information about me can someone actually find?”

The real question

What personal information can someone find about you online?

The answer depends on your history, the services you've used, what you've published, and what third parties have exposed or indexed. Common categories include:

  • Contact details — phone numbers and email addresses appearing in public pages, directories, old listings or exposed datasets.
  • Identity connections — names linked to usernames, email addresses, phone numbers or public profiles.
  • Social and professional profiles — public social accounts, portfolios, bios, forums and work-related pages.
  • Old accounts and listings — forgotten profiles, marketplace posts, event pages and other pages that remain indexed.
  • Breach exposure — an email address or phone number appearing in records associated with a known breach.
  • Public activity — forum posts, comments, reviews or other material that can be connected back to an identifier.
Important distinction

Information being publicly discoverable does not automatically mean it is accurate, current or harmful. The useful goal is to understand what is exposed, where it appears, and what connections it creates.

Exposure categories

Where personal information tends to surface

Public profiles
Social, professional, forum and portfolio pages can connect your name, usernames, photos, work information or contact details.
Listings & directories
Old classifieds, business listings, event pages and other public records can preserve contact details long after their original purpose.
Breaches & exposed data
A service breach can expose identifiers such as email addresses or phone numbers that may remain useful for future searches or targeting.
Why identifiers matter

Why your phone number and email are useful starting points

Your name can be shared by thousands of people. A phone number or email address is usually more specific. When one of those identifiers appears in multiple places, it can help connect otherwise separate pieces of public information.

That makes phone and email exposure particularly useful to check first. Scan My Shadow's paid scan is built around those two identifiers rather than claiming to perform a complete background investigation.

How the pieces can connect

An old public listing can expose a phone number. That number may also appear in another public source or breach record. Separately, an email address may be connected to an old profile. The individual records may look minor, but together they can reveal a much clearer picture of someone's online presence.

What happens next

What to do when you find personal information exposed

  1. Verify the match. Make sure the result actually relates to you, especially when your name is common.
  2. Decide whether it matters. Prioritise exposed contact details, sensitive information and old accounts you no longer control.
  3. Remove what you can. Contact the relevant website or platform and use its privacy or deletion process where available.
  4. Secure affected accounts. If a breach or reused credential is involved, update passwords and enable stronger account protections.
  5. Recheck later. Online exposure changes as old pages disappear, new accounts are created and new breaches occur.
Being upfront

Limitations and accuracy

  • No single search or scanner can see everything that exists online.
  • Search results can contain information belonging to someone with a similar name or identifier.
  • A clean result means no relevant matches were found across the sources checked — not that no information about you exists anywhere.
  • Public information can change, disappear, move or become newly indexed after a check.
  • Scan My Shadow does not access private inboxes, private messages, passwords or private account contents.
