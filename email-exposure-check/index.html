Email Exposure Check: See Where Your Email Appears Online

Your email address is the one identifier nearly every account is built around. Here's what that means for your exposure, and how to check it yourself.

Think about how many places your primary email address is tied to right now — banking, shopping, social media, newsletters, one-off sign-ups you've long forgotten. Every one of those is a service that holds your email, and every one is a service that could, at some point, be breached, sold, or scraped.

This page covers what your email address can reveal, where it tends to show up publicly, and how to check your own exposure — including where Scan My Shadow fits in.

Real-world exposure

What can someone find from an email address?

What's discoverable depends heavily on how you've used that email over the years, but common findings include:

  • Breach history — which known data breaches your email has appeared in, and roughly what kind of data was exposed in each
  • Linked accounts — some services reveal, intentionally or not, whether an email is registered with them (for example, a "forgot password" flow confirming an account exists)
  • Public profile connections — an email used as a public contact on a personal website, GitHub profile, or forum bio
  • Old sign-ups and directories — newsletter archives, event registration lists, or business directories that published contact emails
Worth knowing

Being found in a breach doesn't automatically mean your current password is compromised — it depends on what data that specific breach exposed and whether you've since changed your credentials.

Common sources

Where email addresses can appear online

Breach databases
Company data breaches that included email addresses, later compiled and indexed by breach-tracking sites.
Public profiles & bios
Personal websites, GitHub, forums, or portfolios that list an email as a public point of contact.
Directories & sign-up archives
Newsletter archives, event registrations, and business directories that published contact details.
Why it's worth checking

Why email exposure matters

An exposed email is a common entry point for phishing attempts and credential-stuffing attacks, where attackers try reused passwords from one breach against other accounts using the same email. Knowing which breaches your email has appeared in helps you prioritise where to update passwords and enable extra protections like two-factor authentication.

Do it yourself, free

How to check your own email manually

Review your password manager's alerts
Most password managers and browsers now flag saved logins involved in known breaches — worth a periodic check.
